\abstract{
Partitioned coupling dominates finite volume fluid--solid interaction (FSI), but its outer iterations converge slowly or fail when the added mass of the fluid is large.
This paper presents a quasi-monolithic cell-centred finite volume method in which the arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian fluid momentum and continuity equations and the total Lagrangian solid momentum equation, written in terms of velocity, form a single nonlinear residual with the fluid mesh motion treated outside the Newton system.
The residual is solved by a Jacobian-free Newton--Krylov method; an approximate Jacobian assembled from compact finite volume linearisations is used only to build a fluid--solid field-split preconditioner.
The interface treatment is assessed against exact solutions for an added-mass ring and a thick-walled Womersley tube: an interface pressure gradient taken from the normal momentum equation is needed for a second-order added-mass effect, the interface traction is taken from the discrete momentum flux, and a second-order velocity-based solid without force lag adds no measurable coupling damping to the ring.
For non-matching interface meshes, a conservative transfer balances the total interface force to round-off.
On matched mesh families the two formulations approach each other under refinement: on the finest meshes the displacements differ by $0.055\%$ on a flexible-bottom cavity and by up to $0.13\%$ on a three-dimensional tube, the least converged quantities (cavity force, tube wave speed) by $0.08\%$ and $0.20\%$; and on robustness maps over the density ratio and the time step it converges in two to four Newton iterations per step in every cell, including a massless wall, where, for solid-to-fluid density ratios of 0.1 and below, Dirichlet--Neumann coupling reaches its iteration cap in 11 (Aitken) and 7 (IQN-ILS) of the 12 combinations of density ratio and time step, and Robin--Neumann coupling in 3 of the 4 time steps at 0.01.
Using the reference solutions and the strict coupling convergence test of a companion verification study, the complete solvers are compared at matched verified error: on the Hron--Turek FSI3 benchmark the method is \tbd{factor} times \tbd{faster/slower} than IQN-ILS at \tbd{error level}.
On a three-dimensional pressure-pulse tube, where it costs 3.2--3.6 times as much as Robin--Neumann coupling, the method keeps a single-node parallel efficiency of 0.78 on 128 cores relative to 16, and a flexible flag in air, on which Newton and IQN-ILS both need a reduced time step, is reported as a limitation.
The implementation, built on solids4foam and PETSc, and all case set-ups are released as open source.
}
