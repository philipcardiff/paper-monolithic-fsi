\abstract{
Partitioned coupling dominates finite volume fluid--solid interaction (FSI), but its outer iterations converge slowly or fail when the added mass of the fluid is large.
This paper presents a quasi-monolithic cell-centred finite volume method in which the arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian fluid equations and the total Lagrangian solid momentum equation, written in velocity, form one nonlinear residual, with the fluid mesh motion outside the Newton system.
A Jacobian-free Newton--Krylov method solves the residual, preconditioned by a fluid--solid field split built from compact finite volume linearisations.
Exact solutions for an added-mass ring and a Womersley tube show that second-order accuracy needs an interface pressure gradient from the normal momentum equation and an interface traction equal to the discrete momentum flux; a conservative transfer balances the interface force to round-off on non-matching meshes.
On matched meshes the quasi-monolithic and partitioned solutions converge towards each other, with displacements differing by at most $0.13\%$ on the finest meshes of the consistency cases; on the Hron--Turek FSI3 benchmark, with a geometrically nonlinear flag, they agree within $1.2\%$.
The method converges in two to four Newton iterations per step over all density ratios and time steps tested, including a massless wall; at density ratios of 0.1 and below, Dirichlet--Neumann coupling reaches its iteration cap in most cases.
On the Hron--Turek FSI3 benchmark, where IQN-ILS needs only five to six coupling iterations per step, the two couplings agree within the uncertainty of the reference, and the method costs 1.2--1.6 times as much as IQN-ILS at equal error, the factor rising with mesh refinement.
On a three-dimensional pressure-pulse tube it costs 3.2--3.6 times as much as Robin--Neumann coupling and keeps a single-node parallel efficiency of 0.78 on 128 cores; a flexible flag in air, which needs a reduced time step with either coupling, is reported as a limitation.
The implementation, built on solids4foam and PETSc, and all case set-ups are open source.
}

\bmhead{Supplementary material}
Six animations accompany this paper (files in the \texttt{supplementary} directory of the paper repository):
S1, the Hron--Turek FSI3 flag over one period, with the vorticity (Section~\ref{sec:fsi3_m});
S2, the flexible foil behind a block as its wake becomes unsteady and it starts to flutter, with streamlines and the deformation magnified 50 times (Section~\ref{sec:lim_foil});
S3, the collapse of the collapsible channel, with the pressure and the speed (Section~\ref{sec:robustness_collapsible});
S4, the pulsatile compliant tube over one period, with the axial velocity (Section~\ref{sec:womersley_m});
S5, the ring vibrating in its $n = 2$ mode at the strong added-mass level, with the pressure and the deformation magnified 250 times (Section~\ref{sec:ring_m});
S6, a pressure pulse travelling along the three-dimensional elastic tube, displaced ten times (Section~\ref{sec:scaling_3d}).
All are computed with the quasi-monolithic coupling; S2--S6 are reruns with a later build than the campaign build (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}) that write fields frequently, and are visualisations rather than results of the paper.
