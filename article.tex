\abstract{
Partitioned coupling dominates finite volume fluid--solid interaction (FSI), but its outer iterations converge slowly or fail when the added mass of the fluid is large.
This paper presents a quasi-monolithic cell-centred finite volume method in which the arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian fluid momentum and continuity equations and the total Lagrangian solid momentum equation, written in terms of velocity, form a single nonlinear residual with the fluid mesh motion treated outside the Newton system.
The residual is solved by a Jacobian-free Newton--Krylov method; an approximate Jacobian assembled from compact finite volume linearisations is used only to build a fluid--solid field-split preconditioner.
The interface treatment is examined by ablation against exact solutions for an added-mass ring and a thick-walled Womersley tube: an interface pressure gradient taken from the normal momentum equation and an interface traction taken from the discrete momentum flux are each needed for second-order accuracy, and a second-order velocity-based solid without force lag adds no coupling damping.
For non-matching interface meshes, a conservative transfer balances the total interface force and power to round-off.
On the same discretisation the method agrees with the partitioned solver to within \tbd{difference}, and it converges over \tbd{range of density ratios and time steps} where Dirichlet--Neumann coupling with Aitken or IQN-ILS acceleration and Robin--Neumann coupling \tbd{fail or need more than N iterations}.
Using the reference solutions and the strict coupling convergence test of a companion verification study, costs are compared at equal verified accuracy: on the Hron--Turek FSI3 benchmark the method is \tbd{factor} times \tbd{faster/slower} than the best partitioned scheme at \tbd{error level}.
A three-dimensional pressure-pulse tube shows \tbd{parallel efficiency} on up to \tbd{N} cores, and a case requiring a reduced time step is reported as a limitation.
The implementation, built on solids4foam and PETSc, and all case set-ups are released as open source.
}
