\section{Accuracy and cost on benchmark problems}\label{sec:accuracy_cost}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% Streams feeding this section: R4 (Hron-Turek FSI3) and R6 (blobInTreacle).

A coupling is more efficient than another only if it reaches the same accuracy at lower cost.
This section compares the complete quasi-monolithic and partitioned IQN-ILS formulations on matched meshes and time steps (their discretisations differ as listed in Section~\ref{sec:consistency_partitioned}), and reports the error against the reference and the cost as a work--precision relation.

The FSI3 problem of~\citet{Turek2006}, an elastic flag behind a rigid cylinder oscillating at a low solid-to-fluid density ratio (Figure~\ref{fig:fsi3_geom_m}), is the most widely used test of strongly coupled FSI solvers, and its Featflow reference values carry an uncertainty of about $3$--$5\%$ in the displacement and force amplitudes and less than $0.3\%$ in the frequency and mean drag~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.

\paragraph{Set-up.}
Both couplings are run on the conformal meshes of the quasi-monolithic set-up at refinement factors 1, 2 and 4, with $\Delta t = 0.001$, $0.0005$ and $0.00025\,\mathrm{s}$ respectively, to $t = \tbd{7}\,\mathrm{s}$; these meshes have a quarter of the solid cells of the companion paper's meshes at each factor.
The flag is computed with the geometrically nonlinear velocity solid (quasi-monolithic) and the displacement solid with IQN-ILS under the strict convergence test (partitioned).
The fluid mesh is moved by a pseudo-solid stiffened near the flag and the cylinder (Section~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion}); with uniform stiffness the fluid mesh inverts during the start-up overshoot at $t = 2.76\,\mathrm{s}$ with either coupling.

\paragraph{Measured.}
The mean and amplitude of $u_x$(A), the amplitude and frequency of $u_y$(A), the mean and amplitude of the drag and the amplitude of the lift, over the last \tbd{second}, with the lift smoothed as in the companion paper, and their errors against the Featflow level-4 values.
The cost is timed over two oscillation periods from a periodic restart, with the same number of processes for both couplings at each level.
On the factor-2 mesh the iterative side of the work--precision relation is measured by a tolerance sweep: \texttt{snes\_rtol} of $10^{-4}$, $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-8}$ for the quasi-monolithic coupling and strict coupling tolerances of $10^{-4}$, $10^{-5}$ and $10^{-6}$ for IQN-ILS.

\citet{Liu2014} proposed an elastic half-cylinder attached to the floor of a highly viscous channel flow (Figure~\ref{fig:blob_geom_m}) to measure the temporal order of their quasi-monolithic finite element formulation, on which the present method is based~\citep{JaimanJoshi2022}.
Repeating their temporal study with the present cell-centred finite volume formulation is therefore the most direct comparison with the finite element method; the companion paper uses the case only for partitioned self-convergence~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.

\paragraph{Set-up.}
A linear elastic half-cylinder of radius $0.5\,\mathrm{m}$ ($\lambda_s = 500\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $\mu_s = 50\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $\rho_s = 1\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) is immersed in a fluid of the same density and kinematic viscosity $1\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, so that the Reynolds number is of order one and the added mass is strong.
A parabolic inlet velocity is ramped up over $2\,\mathrm{s}$, after which the flow becomes steady.
The solid is the geometrically linear velocity solid, and both regions use BDF2 in time.
% The quasi-monolithic variant uses backward for the fluid and the solid since
% solids4foam 6dc42799b (Euler for the fluid before, e.g. at 257b2e564).

\paragraph{Measured.}
The temporal study follows~\citet{Liu2014} and the partitioned study of the companion paper: on the tutorial mesh (480 fluid and 120 solid cells), $\Delta t = 0.25$ to $0.015625\,\mathrm{s}$, halved each time, to $t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$, monitoring the self-convergence of the top-point displacement $u_x$(A) and the interface position.
The quasi-monolithic coupling uses Newton tolerances of $10^{-8}$ and a Krylov tolerance of $10^{-6}$, so that its iterative error lies below the smallest change; IQN-ILS uses the strict test with the tutorial tolerance of $10^{-6}$.
The mesh study computes the steady $u_x$(A) on mesh factors 1, 2 and 4 ($\Delta t = 0.01$, $0.01$ and $0.005\,\mathrm{s}$) with the tutorial tolerances to $t = 3\,\mathrm{s}$, by which time it varies by less than $2\times10^{-4}$ of its value over the last $0.5\,\mathrm{s}$.
The temporal study of both couplings is timed serially under the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}, over each whole run excluding its first step; the mesh study is run on a workstation.

\paragraph{Temporal convergence.}
The quasi-monolithic sequence is broadly compatible with second-order convergence (Figure~\ref{fig:blob_time}), with observed orders of 2.31, 3.17 and 1.77 for $u_x$(A) and 3.68, 2.91 and 2.61 for the interface position, consistent with the second order reported by~\citet{Liu2014} for their finite element formulation.
IQN-ILS converges at a similar rate from the largest steps (orders 2.05 and 3.67 for $u_x$(A), 2.14 and 3.31 for the interface) but stops at an iterative floor, set by its tolerance of $10^{-6}$, of about $10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$ in $u_x$(A) and $5\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$ in the interface position: below $\Delta t = 0.03125\,\mathrm{s}$ the successive change no longer falls.
Tightening the tolerance does not remove the floor: the interface residual then stagnates at about $2.5\times10^{-8}$, the level set by the inner fluid and solid solver tolerances.
The quasi-monolithic coupling needs 2.0--3.0 Newton iterations per step and IQN-ILS 14--24 coupling iterations; the five quasi-monolithic runs take $20\,\mathrm{s}$ in total against $126\,\mathrm{s}$ for IQN-ILS, six times less, despite their tighter tolerances.
At the smallest step the couplings differ by $0.71\%$ in $u_x$(A), the same as in the steady state on this mesh (Table~\ref{tab:blob_mesh}), so the difference is spatial, not temporal.

\paragraph{Mesh convergence.}
At steady state (Figure~\ref{fig:blob_field}) the flow accelerates over the half-cylinder, which leans downstream by about $0.12\,\mathrm{m}$ at its top.
Both couplings converge at close to second order in space (Table~\ref{tab:blob_mesh}), and their difference halves with each refinement and falls to $0.06\%$ after extrapolation, so they converge to the same solution; this difference is discussed with the other discretisation differences in Section~\ref{sec:consistency_partitioned}.
The quasi-monolithic coupling needs 2.0, 2.0 and 2.6 Newton iterations per step on the three meshes, with 24, 36 and 62 Krylov iterations per Newton iteration, whereas IQN-ILS needs 18 and 26 coupling iterations per step on the two coarser meshes (up to 86 and 98 in a step); the quasi-monolithic runs are eight to nine times faster.

\paragraph{Discussion: cost per step and number of iterations.}
A Newton iteration of the quasi-monolithic coupling solves a preconditioned linear system for all fluid and solid unknowns, whereas a partitioned coupling iteration performs one segregated fluid solve and one solid solve.
The Newton iteration is therefore the more expensive, so the quasi-monolithic coupling is cheaper per step only when the partitioned scheme needs enough coupling iterations.
In this paper the quasi-monolithic coupling needs two to four Newton iterations per step independently of the added-mass ratio (Sections~\ref{sec:ring_m} and~\ref{sec:robustness}), whereas the coupling iterations of the partitioned schemes grow with it.
Measured on the same processes, a Newton iteration costs about as much as 1.2 IQN-ILS coupling iterations on the two-dimensional ring and half-cylinder and about 3.4 on the three-dimensional flag of Section~\ref{sec:lim_foil}, whose fluid block needs more Krylov iterations.
The break-even point, the number of Newton iterations times this cost ratio, is therefore case dependent: about 2.5--5 coupling iterations per step in the two-dimensional cases and about 11 on the flag (3.3 Newton iterations per step).
Above it the quasi-monolithic coupling is cheaper, by a factor of two on the ring at the strong added-mass level, where IQN-ILS needs 4--6, and by six to nine on the half-cylinder, where it needs 14--26.
Where few coupling iterations suffice, as for the flag (5.4 per step) and the steady flexible-bottom cavity (Section~\ref{sec:cavity_m}), the accelerated partitioned schemes are cheaper, by a factor of 2.1 on the flag.
On the Hron--Turek FSI3 benchmark, the only case of the paper with an established external reference and a work--precision comparison, the quasi-monolithic coupling costs \tbd{factor} times \tbd{more/less} than IQN-ILS at equal error (Section~\ref{sec:fsi3_m}).
