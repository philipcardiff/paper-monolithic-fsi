%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Accuracy and cost on benchmark problems}\label{sec:accuracy_cost}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% Streams feeding this section: R4 (Hron-Turek FSI3) and R6 (blobInTreacle).
% The 2D block with a flexible tail (Jaiman and Joshi 2022) has no case in
% solids4foam (A1 Section 1.14); it is added only if Philip approves it at G1.

A coupling is more efficient than another only if it reaches the same accuracy at lower cost.
This section compares the complete quasi-monolithic and partitioned IQN-ILS formulations on matched meshes and time steps (their discretisations differ as listed in Section~\ref{sec:consistency_partitioned}), and reports the error against the reference and the cost as a work--precision relation.

\subsection{Hron--Turek FSI3}\label{sec:fsi3_m}

The FSI3 problem of~\citet{Turek2006}, an elastic flag behind a rigid cylinder oscillating at a solid-to-fluid density ratio of one (Figures~\ref{fig:fsi3_geom_m} and~\ref{fig:fsi3_field_m}), is the most widely used test of strongly coupled FSI solvers, and its Featflow reference values carry an uncertainty of about $3$--$5\%$ in the displacement and force amplitudes and less than $0.3\%$ in the frequency and mean drag~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.

\paragraph{Set-up.}
Both couplings are run on one family of three unstructured fluid meshes, refined towards the flag tip, with $96\,712$, $194\,183$ and $387\,219$ cells (levels 1--3, the cell size reduced by $\sqrt{2}$ per level), and a uniform flag mesh of $430\times24$, $608\times34$ and $860\times48$ cells; the interface is non-matching and is transferred by AMI.
The time steps are $2\times10^{-4}$, $1.41\times10^{-4}$ and $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}$, with BDF2 in both regions.
The flow develops for $2\,\mathrm{s}$ around a fixed flag, after which the coupled problem is run to $t = 6\,\mathrm{s}$.
The flag is computed with the geometrically nonlinear velocity solid (quasi-monolithic) and with the geometrically nonlinear displacement solid under IQN-ILS with the strict convergence test, a tolerance of $10^{-6}$ on every residual measure (partitioned).
The quasi-monolithic solid uses the stabilisation of Equation~\eqref{eqn:rhie_chow_s} with $\alpha_d = 0.5$ (Table~\ref{tab:solver_settings}).
The stabilisation vanishes at the design order of the discretisation, so its effect should decrease with refinement; it is noticeable on the level-1 flag, where the jump stabilisation of Section~\ref{sec:lim_thin_solid} with factor $0.1$ gives a lift-amplitude error of $4.0\%$ instead of $6.35\%$, and changes the errors of the $u_x$ mean, the $u_y$ amplitude and frequency, and the drag mean and amplitude by $0.01$--$0.94$ percentage points.

For both couplings the fluid mesh is moved by the linear elastic pseudo-solid assembled on the current mesh, stiffened by the linear inverse distance to the flag and the cylinder (Sections~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion} and~\ref{sec:lim_mesh_motion}).
Three further quasi-monolithic runs check the level-2 result: time steps of $10^{-4}$ and $2\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}$, and a flag mesh refined twice in each direction.

\paragraph{Measured.}
The mean and amplitude of $u_x$(A), the amplitude and frequency of $u_y$(A), and the mean and amplitude of the drag and lift on the cylinder and flag are evaluated over the closing second ($t = 5$--$6\,\mathrm{s}$, about five periods) as in the companion paper, and compared with the Featflow level-4 values; the amplitudes of the last two periods agree with those of the two before to within $0.07\%$ in every run.
The cost follows the timing protocol of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}: each run is restarted from its state at $t = 5\,\mathrm{s}$ alone on an exclusive MeluXina node with its production number of processes, the first 50 steps are discarded, and the elapsed time of the next flag period ($0.183\,\mathrm{s}$) gives the cost in core-hours per simulated second, the median of three repeats.
The repeats agree within $4\%$, except for IQN-ILS on level 3 ($10\%$) and one repeat of each coupling on level 2, which ran $16$ and $21\%$ slower than the median.

\paragraph{Accuracy.}
At every level both couplings are within the acceptance tolerances of the companion paper (Table~\ref{tab:fsi3_m}): the displacement errors are below $3\%$, the frequency and mean-drag errors below $0.3\%$, and the drag-amplitude errors below $3.2\%$, all inside the uncertainty of the reference.
The lift amplitude has the largest error, $6.4\%$ on level 1, above the uncertainty of the reference, and $2.9$--$3.6\%$ on level 3; it falls under refinement at an observed order of about $1.3$ (quasi-monolithic) and $1.4$ (IQN-ILS), and refining the flag mesh of level 2 lowers it by $1.7\%$ (drag amplitude $0.7\%$, $u_y$ amplitude $0.05\%$), so the solid mesh accounts for most of the remaining lift error.
Halving or doubling the time step on level 2 changes every quantity by less than $0.05\%$.
On the same mesh the two couplings differ by $0.04$--$0.65\%$ in the frequency, the mean drag and the $u_y$ and lift amplitudes, and by $0.6$--$1.6\%$ in the $u_x$ statistics and the drag amplitude.
These differences do not shrink over the three levels (the $u_x$ mean differs by $0.6$, $1.0$ and $1.2\%$), unlike those of Section~\ref{sec:consistency_partitioned}: the flag is geometrically nonlinear, for which the velocity and displacement solids are not algebraically equivalent in time, and the quasi-monolithic fluid moves its mesh once per step rather than in every coupling iteration (Table~\ref{tab:discretisation_differences}). The differences stay well inside the reference uncertainty.
Over the three levels the $u_x$ mean changes by $1.3\%$ and then $0.05\%$ (quasi-monolithic), and by $1.7\%$ and then $0.14\%$ (IQN-ILS): on this mesh family neither coupling shows the first-order sequence of the companion paper's meshes, so that sequence comes from the discretisation and meshes rather than from the coupling.

\paragraph{Cost.}
The quasi-monolithic coupling needs two Newton iterations per step at every level, with $46$, $54$ and $67$ Krylov iterations per Newton iteration on levels 1--3, and IQN-ILS needs $4.9$--$6.0$ coupling iterations per step.
One Newton iteration therefore costs as much as $3.0$, $3.5$ and $5.0$ coupling iterations on levels 1--3, more than on the other two-dimensional cases and rising with the mesh together with the Krylov iterations of the fluid block, and the quasi-monolithic coupling is $1.2$, $1.3$ and $1.6$ times more expensive per simulated second on levels 1--3 (Figure~\ref{fig:fsi3_work_precision}).
Since the two couplings reach the same error on the same mesh to within the reference uncertainty, equal error means equal mesh, and the quasi-monolithic coupling costs $1.2$--$1.6$ times as much as IQN-ILS at equal error, the factor rising with refinement; below errors of about $3$--$5\%$, every quantity except the lift amplitude is limited by the reference rather than by either coupling.

\citet{Liu2014} proposed an elastic half-cylinder attached to the floor of a highly viscous channel flow (Figure~\ref{fig:blob_geom_m}) to measure the temporal order of their quasi-monolithic finite element formulation, on which the present method is based~\citep{JaimanJoshi2022}.
Repeating their temporal study with the present cell-centred finite volume formulation is therefore the most direct comparison with the finite element method; the companion paper uses the case only for partitioned self-convergence~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.

\paragraph{Set-up.}
An elastic half-cylinder of radius $0.5\,\mathrm{m}$ ($\lambda_s = 500\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $\mu_s = 50\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $\rho_s = 1\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) is immersed in a fluid of the same density and kinematic viscosity $1\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, so that the Reynolds number is of order one and the added mass is strong.
A parabolic inlet velocity is ramped up over $2\,\mathrm{s}$, after which the flow becomes steady.
\citet{Liu2014} use a small-strain linear elastic solid, but the top of the half-cylinder moves by more than a quarter of its radius, and a small-strain solid integrates the fluid traction over its undeformed surface, so that it receives about $12\%$ less force than the fluid exerts~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.
As in the companion paper, the solid is therefore finite strain, with the compressible neo-Hookean strain energy $\Psi = \tfrac{\mu_s}{2}(\bar{I}_1 - 3) + \tfrac{K_s}{4}(J^2 - 1 - 2\ln J)$, $K_s = \lambda_s + \tfrac{2}{3}\mu_s$, which reduces to Liu et al.'s model for small strains: the geometrically nonlinear velocity solid (quasi-monolithic) and the total Lagrangian displacement solid (partitioned).
Both regions use BDF2 in time.
% The quasi-monolithic variant uses backward for the fluid and the solid since
% solids4foam 6dc42799b (Euler for the fluid before, e.g. at 257b2e564).

\paragraph{Measured.}
The temporal study follows~\citet{Liu2014} and the partitioned study of the companion paper: on the tutorial mesh (480 fluid and 120 solid cells), $\Delta t = 0.25$ to $0.015625\,\mathrm{s}$, halved each time, to $t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$, monitoring the self-convergence of the top-point displacement $u_x$(A) and the interface position.
The quasi-monolithic coupling uses Newton tolerances of $10^{-8}$ and a Krylov tolerance of $10^{-6}$, so that its iterative error lies below the smallest change; IQN-ILS uses the strict test with the tutorial tolerance of $10^{-6}$, without reusing the modes of earlier steps.
The mesh study computes the steady $u_x$(A) on mesh factors 1, 2 and 4 ($\Delta t = 0.01$, $0.01$ and $0.005\,\mathrm{s}$) with the tutorial tolerances to $t = 3\,\mathrm{s}$, by which time it varies by less than $2\times10^{-4}$ of its value over the last $0.5\,\mathrm{s}$.
The temporal study of both couplings is timed serially under the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}, over each whole run excluding its first step; the mesh-study runs share one node, so their times are indicative.
Both couplings use one build, later than the campaign build (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).

\paragraph{Temporal convergence.}
The quasi-monolithic sequence is broadly compatible with second-order convergence (Figure~\ref{fig:blob_time}), with observed orders of 2.07 and 3.22 for $u_x$(A) and 3.66, 2.62 and 2.65 for the interface position, consistent with the second order reported by~\citet{Liu2014} for their finite element formulation; the last change in $u_x$(A), $7\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}$, approaches the level of the Newton tolerance (order 1.20).
IQN-ILS converges at a similar rate (orders 2.13 and 2.21 for $u_x$(A), 2.12, 2.00 and 2.63 for the interface), but its last change in $u_x$(A), $8\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}$, is smaller than second order predicts and depends on the coupling tolerance (with $10^{-7}$ the last order is 1.44), so at the smallest steps its iterative error is comparable to the temporal change.
The quasi-monolithic coupling needs 2.0--3.0 Newton iterations per step and IQN-ILS 11.0--14.5 coupling iterations (at most 22); the five quasi-monolithic runs take $21\,\mathrm{s}$ in total against $118\,\mathrm{s}$ for IQN-ILS, 5.6 times less, despite their tighter tolerances.
At the smallest step the couplings differ by $0.09\%$ in $u_x$(A), close to their $0.12\%$ difference in the steady state on this mesh (Table~\ref{tab:blob_mesh}), so the difference is spatial, not temporal.

\paragraph{Mesh convergence.}
At steady state (Figure~\ref{fig:blob_field}) the flow accelerates over the half-cylinder, which leans downstream by about $0.14\,\mathrm{m}$ at its top.
Both couplings converge at close to second order in space (Table~\ref{tab:blob_mesh}), and their difference changes sign between factors 1 and 2, falls to $0.05\%$ on factor 4 and to $0.02\%$ after extrapolation, so they converge to the same solution; this difference is discussed with the other discretisation differences in Section~\ref{sec:consistency_partitioned}.
The quasi-monolithic coupling needs 2.0, 2.1 and 2.7 Newton iterations per step on the three meshes, with 24, 38 and 65 Krylov iterations per Newton iteration, whereas IQN-ILS needs 9.1, 10.3 and 12.2 coupling iterations per step (at most 14, 16 and 18); the quasi-monolithic runs are two to four times faster.

\paragraph{Discussion: cost per step and number of iterations.}
A Newton iteration of the quasi-monolithic coupling solves a preconditioned linear system for all fluid and solid unknowns, whereas a partitioned coupling iteration performs one segregated fluid solve and one solid solve.
The Newton iteration is therefore the more expensive, so the quasi-monolithic coupling is cheaper per step only when the partitioned scheme needs enough coupling iterations.
In this paper the quasi-monolithic coupling needs two to four Newton iterations per step independently of the added-mass ratio (Sections~\ref{sec:ring_m} and~\ref{sec:robustness}), whereas the coupling iterations of the partitioned schemes grow with it.
Measured on the same processes, a Newton iteration costs about as much as 1.1--1.2 IQN-ILS coupling iterations on the two-dimensional ring and half-cylinder, $3.0$--$5.0$ on the FSI3 meshes and about 3.4 on the three-dimensional flag of Section~\ref{sec:lim_foil}, where the fluid block needs more Krylov iterations.
The break-even point, the number of Newton iterations times this cost ratio, is therefore case dependent: about 2.5--5 coupling iterations per step on the ring and half-cylinder, $6$--$10$ on FSI3 and about 11 on the flag (3.3 Newton iterations per step).
Above it the quasi-monolithic coupling is cheaper, by a factor of two on the ring at the strong added-mass level, where IQN-ILS needs 4--6, and by two to six on the half-cylinder, where it needs 9--15.
Where few coupling iterations suffice, as for FSI3 (4.9--6.0 per step), the flag (5.4) and the steady flexible-bottom cavity (Section~\ref{sec:cavity_m}), the accelerated partitioned schemes are cheaper, by factors of 1.2--1.6 on FSI3 and 2.1 on the flag.
On the Hron--Turek FSI3 benchmark, the only case of the paper with an established external reference and a work--precision comparison, the quasi-monolithic coupling costs $1.2$--$1.6$ times as much as IQN-ILS at equal error, the factor rising with refinement (Section~\ref{sec:fsi3_m}).
