\section{Accuracy and cost on benchmark problems}\label{sec:accuracy_cost}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% Streams feeding this section: R4 (Hron-Turek FSI3) and R6 (blobInTreacle).
% The 2D block with a flexible tail (Jaiman and Joshi 2022) has no case in
% solids4foam (A1 Section 1.14); it is added only if Philip approves it at G1.

A coupling is more efficient than another only if it reaches the same accuracy at lower cost.
This section compares the complete quasi-monolithic and partitioned IQN-ILS formulations on matched meshes and time steps (their discretisations differ as listed in Section~\ref{sec:consistency_partitioned}), and reports the error against the reference and the cost as a work--precision relation.

\subsection{Hron--Turek FSI3}\label{sec:fsi3_m}
% Every run of this subsection uses BDF2 (backward) for the fluid ddt(U),
% ddt(dU), ddt(p) and for the solid (fsi3g run files, checked 10 Nov 2026).
% Data: paper-data/fsi3_runs.csv (fsi3g runs on MeluXina, solids4foam
% a421f063d, qm-a421 build); statistics with the TurekHron2006 verification
% driver's periodic_statistics; scripts figures/scripts/fsi3_*.py.

The FSI3 problem of~\citet{Turek2006}, an elastic flag behind a rigid cylinder oscillating at a solid-to-fluid density ratio of one (Figure~\ref{fig:fsi3_geom_m}), is the most widely used test of strongly coupled FSI solvers, and its Featflow reference values carry an uncertainty of about $3$--$5\%$ in the displacement and force amplitudes and less than $0.3\%$ in the frequency and mean drag~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/HronTurek3-geometry}
	\caption{Hron--Turek FSI3: (a) geometry (m), boundary conditions and the level-1 fluid mesh; (b) the cylinder, the flag (solid region) and point A, where the fluid mesh is refined towards the flag tip.}
	\label{fig:fsi3_geom_m}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Set-up.}
Both couplings are run on one family of three unstructured fluid meshes, refined towards the flag tip, with $96\,712$, $194\,183$ and $387\,219$ cells (levels 1--3, the cell size reduced by $\sqrt{2}$ per level), and a uniform flag mesh of $430\times24$, $608\times34$ and $860\times48$ cells; the interface is non-matching and is transferred by AMI.
The time steps are $2\times10^{-4}$, $1.41\times10^{-4}$ and $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}$, with BDF2 in both regions.
The flow develops for $2\,\mathrm{s}$ around a fixed flag, after which the coupled problem is run to $t = 6\,\mathrm{s}$.
The flag is computed with the geometrically nonlinear velocity solid (quasi-monolithic) and with the geometrically nonlinear displacement solid under IQN-ILS with the strict convergence test, a tolerance of $10^{-6}$ on every residual measure (partitioned).
For both couplings the fluid mesh is moved by the linear elastic pseudo-solid assembled on the current mesh, stiffened by the linear inverse distance to the flag and the cylinder (Sections~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion} and~\ref{sec:lim_mesh_motion}).
Three further quasi-monolithic runs check the level-2 result: time steps of $10^{-4}$ and $2\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}$, and a flag mesh refined twice in each direction.

\paragraph{Measured.}
The mean and amplitude of $u_x$(A), the amplitude and frequency of $u_y$(A), and the mean and amplitude of the drag and lift on the cylinder and flag are evaluated over the closing second ($t = 5$--$6\,\mathrm{s}$, about five periods) as in the companion paper, and compared with the Featflow level-4 values; the amplitudes of the last two periods agree with those of the two before to within $0.07\%$ in every run.
The cost is the elapsed time of the coupled phase ($t = 2$--$6\,\mathrm{s}$) in core-hours per simulated second, without the first step of each job.
The runs of each level shared one exclusive MeluXina node, side by side, so these costs are indicative and do not follow the timing protocol of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}: over single seconds of the run the ratio of the two couplings' costs ranges from $1.1$ to $1.6$.

\paragraph{Accuracy.}
At every level both couplings are within the acceptance tolerances of the companion paper (Table~\ref{tab:fsi3_m}): the displacement errors are below $3\%$, the frequency and mean-drag errors below $0.3\%$, and the drag-amplitude errors below $3.2\%$, all inside the uncertainty of the reference.
The lift amplitude has the largest error, $6.4\%$ on level 1, above the uncertainty of the reference, and $2.9$--$3.6\%$ on level 3; it falls under refinement at an observed order of about $1.3$ (quasi-monolithic) and $1.4$ (IQN-ILS), and refining the flag mesh of level 2 lowers it by $1.7\%$ (drag amplitude $0.7\%$, $u_y$ amplitude $0.05\%$), so the solid mesh accounts for most of the remaining lift error.
Halving or doubling the time step on level 2 changes every quantity by less than $0.05\%$.
On the same mesh the two couplings differ by $0.04$--$0.65\%$ in the frequency, the mean drag and the $u_y$ and lift amplitudes, and by $0.6$--$1.6\%$ in the $u_x$ statistics and the drag amplitude.
These differences do not shrink over the three levels (the $u_x$ mean differs by $0.6$, $1.0$ and $1.2\%$), unlike those of Section~\ref{sec:consistency_partitioned}: the flag is geometrically nonlinear, for which the velocity and displacement solids are not algebraically equivalent in time, and the quasi-monolithic fluid moves its mesh once per step rather than in every coupling iteration (Table~\ref{tab:discretisation_differences}). The differences stay well inside the reference uncertainty.
Over the three levels the $u_x$ mean changes by $1.3\%$ and then $0.05\%$ (quasi-monolithic), and by $1.7\%$ and then $0.14\%$ (IQN-ILS): on this mesh family neither coupling shows the first-order sequence of the companion paper's meshes, so that sequence comes from the discretisation and meshes rather than from the coupling.

\paragraph{Cost.}
The quasi-monolithic coupling needs two Newton iterations per step at every level, with $46$, $54$ and $67$ Krylov iterations per Newton iteration on levels 1--3, and IQN-ILS needs $4.9$--$6.0$ coupling iterations per step.
One Newton iteration therefore costs as much as $2.9$--$4.2$ coupling iterations, more than on the other two-dimensional cases because the Krylov iterations of the fluid block grow with the mesh, and the quasi-monolithic coupling is $1.2$, $1.3$ and $1.4$ times more expensive per simulated second on levels 1--3 (Figure~\ref{fig:fsi3_work_precision}).
Since the two couplings reach the same error on the same mesh to within the reference uncertainty, equal error means equal mesh, and the quasi-monolithic coupling costs $1.2$--$1.4$ times as much as IQN-ILS at equal error; below errors of about $3$--$5\%$, every quantity except the lift amplitude is limited by the reference rather than by either coupling.

\citet{Liu2014} proposed an elastic half-cylinder attached to the floor of a highly viscous channel flow (Figure~\ref{fig:blob_geom_m}) to measure the temporal order of their quasi-monolithic finite element formulation, on which the present method is based~\citep{JaimanJoshi2022}.
Repeating their temporal study with the present cell-centred finite volume formulation is therefore the most direct comparison with the finite element method; the companion paper uses the case only for partitioned self-convergence~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.

	\label{fig:blob_geom_m}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Set-up.}
A linear elastic half-cylinder of radius $0.5\,\mathrm{m}$ ($\lambda_s = 500\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $\mu_s = 50\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $\rho_s = 1\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) is immersed in a fluid of the same density and kinematic viscosity $1\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, so that the Reynolds number is of order one and the added mass is strong.
A parabolic inlet velocity is ramped up over $2\,\mathrm{s}$, after which the flow becomes steady.
The solid is the geometrically linear velocity solid, and both regions use BDF2 in time.
% The quasi-monolithic variant uses backward for the fluid and the solid since
% solids4foam 6dc42799b (Euler for the fluid before, e.g. at 257b2e564).

\paragraph{Measured.}
The temporal study follows~\citet{Liu2014} and the partitioned study of the companion paper: on the tutorial mesh (480 fluid and 120 solid cells), $\Delta t = 0.25$ to $0.015625\,\mathrm{s}$, halved each time, to $t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$, monitoring the self-convergence of the top-point displacement $u_x$(A) and the interface position.
The quasi-monolithic coupling uses Newton tolerances of $10^{-8}$ and a Krylov tolerance of $10^{-6}$, so that its iterative error lies below the smallest change; IQN-ILS uses the strict test with the tutorial tolerance of $10^{-6}$.
The mesh study computes the steady $u_x$(A) on mesh factors 1, 2 and 4 ($\Delta t = 0.01$, $0.01$ and $0.005\,\mathrm{s}$) with the tutorial tolerances to $t = 3\,\mathrm{s}$, by which time it varies by less than $2\times10^{-4}$ of its value over the last $0.5\,\mathrm{s}$.
The temporal study of both couplings is timed serially under the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}, over each whole run excluding its first step; the mesh study is run on a workstation.

\paragraph{Temporal convergence.}
The quasi-monolithic sequence is broadly compatible with second-order convergence (Figure~\ref{fig:blob_time}), with observed orders of 2.31, 3.17 and 1.77 for $u_x$(A) and 3.68, 2.91 and 2.61 for the interface position, consistent with the second order reported by~\citet{Liu2014} for their finite element formulation.
IQN-ILS converges at a similar rate from the largest steps (orders 2.05 and 3.67 for $u_x$(A), 2.14 and 3.31 for the interface) but stops at an iterative floor, set by its tolerance of $10^{-6}$, of about $10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$ in $u_x$(A) and $5\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$ in the interface position: below $\Delta t = 0.03125\,\mathrm{s}$ the successive change no longer falls.
Tightening the tolerance does not remove the floor: the interface residual then stagnates at about $2.5\times10^{-8}$, the level set by the inner fluid and solid solver tolerances.
The quasi-monolithic coupling needs 2.0--3.0 Newton iterations per step and IQN-ILS 14--24 coupling iterations; the five quasi-monolithic runs take $20\,\mathrm{s}$ in total against $126\,\mathrm{s}$ for IQN-ILS, six times less, despite their tighter tolerances.
At the smallest step the couplings differ by $0.71\%$ in $u_x$(A), the same as in the steady state on this mesh (Table~\ref{tab:blob_mesh}), so the difference is spatial, not temporal.

\paragraph{Mesh convergence.}
At steady state (Figure~\ref{fig:blob_field}) the flow accelerates over the half-cylinder, which leans downstream by about $0.12\,\mathrm{m}$ at its top.
Both couplings converge at close to second order in space (Table~\ref{tab:blob_mesh}), and their difference halves with each refinement and falls to $0.06\%$ after extrapolation, so they converge to the same solution; this difference is discussed with the other discretisation differences in Section~\ref{sec:consistency_partitioned}.
The quasi-monolithic coupling needs 2.0, 2.0 and 2.6 Newton iterations per step on the three meshes, with 24, 36 and 62 Krylov iterations per Newton iteration, whereas IQN-ILS needs 18 and 26 coupling iterations per step on the two coarser meshes (up to 86 and 98 in a step); the quasi-monolithic runs are eight to nine times faster.

\paragraph{Discussion: cost per step and number of iterations.}
A Newton iteration of the quasi-monolithic coupling solves a preconditioned linear system for all fluid and solid unknowns, whereas a partitioned coupling iteration performs one segregated fluid solve and one solid solve.
The Newton iteration is therefore the more expensive, so the quasi-monolithic coupling is cheaper per step only when the partitioned scheme needs enough coupling iterations.
In this paper the quasi-monolithic coupling needs two to four Newton iterations per step independently of the added-mass ratio (Sections~\ref{sec:ring_m} and~\ref{sec:robustness}), whereas the coupling iterations of the partitioned schemes grow with it.
Measured on the same processes, a Newton iteration costs about as much as 1.2 IQN-ILS coupling iterations on the two-dimensional ring and half-cylinder, $2.9$--$4.2$ on the larger FSI3 meshes and about 3.4 on the three-dimensional flag of Section~\ref{sec:lim_foil}, where the fluid block needs more Krylov iterations.
The break-even point, the number of Newton iterations times this cost ratio, is therefore case dependent: about 2.5--5 coupling iterations per step on the ring and half-cylinder, $6$--$8$ on FSI3 and about 11 on the flag (3.3 Newton iterations per step).
Above it the quasi-monolithic coupling is cheaper, by a factor of two on the ring at the strong added-mass level, where IQN-ILS needs 4--6, and by six to nine on the half-cylinder, where it needs 14--26.
Where few coupling iterations suffice, as for FSI3 (4.9--6.0 per step), the flag (5.4) and the steady flexible-bottom cavity (Section~\ref{sec:cavity_m}), the accelerated partitioned schemes are cheaper, by factors of 1.2--1.4 on FSI3 and 2.1 on the flag.
On the Hron--Turek FSI3 benchmark, the only case of the paper with an established external reference and a work--precision comparison, the quasi-monolithic coupling costs $1.2$--$1.4$ times as much as IQN-ILS at equal error (Section~\ref{sec:fsi3_m}).
