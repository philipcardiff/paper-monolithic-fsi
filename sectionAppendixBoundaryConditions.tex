\section{Boundary conditions}\label{app:boundary_conditions}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

This appendix lists the boundary conditions of the cases in Sections~\ref{sec:verification_exact}--\ref{sec:limitations}.
They are those of the quasi-monolithic variants of the solids4foam tutorials (\texttt{./Allrun monolithic}), whose dictionaries give the full specification; the partitioned runs use the same conditions, apart from the interface conditions of the partitioned couplings.

\paragraph{Fluid.}
The fluid boundary conditions are of the following types:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \emph{Velocity inlet}: a prescribed velocity, $\bb{v}_F = \bb{v}_\text{in}(\bb{x}, t)$, with a zero normal pressure gradient.
	\item \emph{Pressure inlet or outlet}: a prescribed kinematic pressure, $p_F = p_\text{b}(t)$, with a zero normal velocity gradient, $\hat{\bb{n}}_F \cdot \nabla \bb{v}_F = \bb{0}$; at a \emph{parallel-flow} end, the tangential velocity is instead zero and only the normal velocity component has a zero normal gradient.
	\item \emph{No-slip wall}: $\bb{v}_F = \bb{0}$, with a zero normal pressure gradient.
	\item \emph{Slip wall}: zero normal velocity and zero normal gradient of the tangential velocity, with a zero normal pressure gradient.
	\item \emph{Symmetry plane}: the slip condition for the velocity and a zero normal gradient of the pressure; on the wedge planes of the axisymmetric case, the corresponding rotational symmetry.
\end{itemize}
On the fluid--solid interface, the velocity and pressure are set by the coupling (Section~\ref{sec:fsi_interface}).
If no boundary prescribes the pressure, its level is fixed by a reference value at one point.

\paragraph{Solid.}
The solid boundaries are clamped (zero displacement and velocity), loaded by a prescribed traction (traction-free unless stated), or symmetry planes (zero normal displacement and zero tangential traction).
On the interface, the solid is loaded by the fluid traction (Section~\ref{sec:fsi_interface}).

\paragraph{Fluid mesh motion.}
The mesh displacement equals the solid interface displacement on the interface, is zero on all other fluid boundaries, and is tangential on symmetry planes.
