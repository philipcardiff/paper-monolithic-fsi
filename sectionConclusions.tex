\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

This paper has presented a quasi-monolithic cell-centred finite volume method for fluid--solid interaction, in which the ALE fluid equations and the total Lagrangian solid momentum equation in velocity form are solved as one nonlinear system by a Jacobian-free Newton--Krylov method, with the fluid mesh motion outside the Newton system~\citep{Liu2014, JaimanJoshi2022}.
It was verified against exact solutions, compared with the partitioned solver of solids4foam on matched meshes and time steps, and assessed as a complete solver against partitioned couplings at matched verified error~\citetodo{companion verification paper}.
The main conclusions are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
	\item \emph{The compact finite volume linearisations are adequate as a preconditioner but not as a Jacobian.} As the Newton operator, the assembled approximate Jacobian converges only linearly (contraction factors $0.2$--$0.85$), whereas the matrix-free operator needs two to four Newton iterations per step in every completed run. The field-split preconditioner needs 14--30 Krylov iterations per Newton iteration on the ring and the tubes and 210--310 on the thin wall of the collapsible channel (Sections~\ref{sec:robustness} and~\ref{sec:tube3d_pc_study}).
	\item \emph{The interface pressure condition controls both the frequency and the damping of the added-mass response.} With the interface pressure gradient taken from the normal momentum equation, the ring reproduces the exact added-mass frequency ratio at observed orders of 3.2 and above and adds no measurable damping; a zero normal gradient reduces the order to first and adds up to $2.3$ percentage points of amplitude loss per period (Sections~\ref{sec:ring_m} and~\ref{sec:interface_comparison}). On a deforming wall, the traction must include the transpose part of the viscous stress (Section~\ref{sec:womersley_consistency}).
	\item \emph{The transpose transfer balances the interface force to round-off on non-conformal interfaces and lets the solid be refined independently.} The force imbalance falls from $3$--$9\times10^{-5}$ with interpolated traction to below $3\times10^{-15}$, and a $640\times8$ solid through AMI brings the collapsible-channel trough within $0.71\%$ of the oomph-lib reference, against $1.17\%$ for a conformal solid on a finer fluid mesh (Sections~\ref{sec:interface_comparison} and~\ref{sec:robustness_collapsible}).
	\item \emph{The quasi-monolithic and partitioned solutions converge towards each other under refinement.} On the finest meshes they differ by $0.055\%$ in the cavity displacement and by at most $0.13\%$ in the tube displacement and timing, and on the elastic half-cylinder their extrapolated values agree within $0.06\%$ (Sections~\ref{sec:consistency_partitioned} and~\ref{sec:blob_m}).
	\item \emph{The Newton iteration count is insensitive to the added mass over the tested range, but not to the nonlinearity over a step.} The method completes every cell of the ring and collapsible-channel maps, including the massless wall, with 2--3.2 Newton iterations per step, where, for density ratios of 0.1 and below, Aitken and IQN-ILS reach their iteration cap in 11 and 7 of 12 cells. On a thin flag in air, Newton and IQN-ILS both need half the tutorial time step (Sections~\ref{sec:robustness} and~\ref{sec:lim_foil}).
	\item \emph{Whether the method is cheaper depends on how many coupling iterations the partitioned scheme needs.} One Newton iteration costs about 1.2 IQN-ILS coupling iterations in two dimensions and 2.4 in three. The method is two to nine times cheaper where added mass is strong (ring, half-cylinder), 1.5 times dearer on the flag, and 3.2--3.6 times dearer than Robin--Neumann on the three-dimensional tube, on which it keeps a single-node parallel efficiency of 0.78 on 128 processes. On Hron--Turek FSI3 at matched error against the Featflow reference, it costs \tbd{factor} times \tbd{more/less} than IQN-ILS (Sections~\ref{sec:accuracy_cost} and~\ref{sec:scaling_3d}).
\end{enumerate}

The main extensions suggested by the limitations of Section~\ref{sec:limitations} are a scalable solid block for the preconditioner, assessed beyond the three-dimensional tube; a high-order reconstruction for the velocity solids, to remove the excess stiffness of thin bending-dominated structures; a globalisation of the Newton iteration, such as pseudo-transient continuation or adaptive time stepping on failure~\citep{Knoll2004}, for strongly nonlinear start-ups; and larger three-dimensional cases, possibly with overset meshes~\citep{miller2014overset}, since the mesh motion is already outside the Newton system.
