This paper has presented a quasi-monolithic cell-centred finite volume method for fluid--solid interaction, in which the arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian fluid momentum and continuity equations and the total Lagrangian solid momentum equation in velocity form are solved as one nonlinear system by a Jacobian-free Newton--Krylov method, with the fluid mesh motion treated outside the Newton system~\citep{Liu2014, JaimanJoshi2022}.
The method was verified against exact solutions, compared with the partitioned solver of solids4foam on the same discretisation, and assessed for robustness, cost and parallel scaling against Aitken, IQN-ILS and Robin--Neumann partitioned coupling at equal verified accuracy, using the references and the strict coupling convergence test of the companion verification study~\citetodo{companion verification paper}.
The main findings, in the order of the contributions listed in Section~\ref{sec:intro}, are as follows.

\paragraph{Newton operator and preconditioner.}
The compact finite volume linearisations of the fluid, solid and interface-coupling terms are adequate as a preconditioner but not as a Jacobian.
Used as the Newton operator, the assembled approximate Jacobian gives linear convergence, with a contraction factor of $0.2$--$0.7$ per iteration on the three-dimensional tube and $0.85$ on the ring, whereas the matrix-free operator converges in two to four Newton iterations per step in every case that completes (Sections~\ref{sec:tube3d_pc_study} and~\ref{sec:robustness}).
With the fluid--solid field-split preconditioner, 14 to 30 Krylov iterations per Newton iteration are needed on the ring and on the two tubes, and 210 to 310 on the collapsible channel, whose thin, finely resolved wall is coupled through AMI; the count grows with mesh refinement, by a factor of about 1.35 per refinement on the three-dimensional tube, and does not change with the process count (Sections~\ref{sec:verification_exact}, \ref{sec:robustness} and~\ref{sec:scaling_3d}).
Of the preconditioner settings, the solid block matters most: on the tube a replicated LU factorisation of the solid block costs eight times as much as one algebraic multigrid V-cycle, whereas the full Schur factorisation and a Jacobian lag of eight Newton iterations save $19\%$ and $20\%$ of the time respectively (Section~\ref{sec:tube3d_pc_study}).

\paragraph{Interface treatment.}
On the ring added-mass problem, the quasi-monolithic coupling reproduces the exact added-mass frequency ratio at every added-mass level with observed orders of 3.2 and above under mesh refinement, and at the strong level the amplitude loss of the coupled vibration equals that of the dry ring to within $0.004$ percentage points per period, so the coupling adds no measurable damping (Section~\ref{sec:ring_m}).
Replacing the interface pressure gradient taken from the normal momentum equation by a zero normal gradient reduces the order of the frequency ratio to first (observed orders $0.74$--$0.89$) and adds $0.34$ to $2.3$ percentage points of amplitude loss per period (Section~\ref{sec:interface_comparison}); the pressure treatment therefore controls the damping of the coupled vibration as well as its frequency.
Removing the one-step lag of the interface force in the velocity solid, and evaluating the viscous traction from the discrete momentum flux, were each shown during development to remove a loss of accuracy, the latter on a stationary-wall case (Section~\ref{sec:interface_comparison}).
On a deforming wall the traction must also contain the transpose part of the viscous stress, $\mu\bb{\nabla}(\bb{v}\cdot\hat{\bb{n}})$ at the wall: without it the quasi-monolithic wall phase of the pulsatile tube is offset from the partitioned solution by $4.8\times10^{-4}$\,rad on every mesh and time step, and with it, evaluated from the wall velocity, the offset falls tenfold (Sections~\ref{sec:fluid_differences} and~\ref{sec:womersley_consistency}).
Every quantity of the pulsatile tube converges at second order in time (observed orders 1.86--2.14); in space most quantities converge at second order (1.92--2.37), the exceptions being the velocity profile (1.81), the wall displacement phase (1.46) and the attenuation, whose error does not fall monotonically (Section~\ref{sec:womersley_m}).

\paragraph{Conservative non-conformal coupling.}
Transferring the fluid face forces to the solid with the transpose of the solid-to-fluid velocity transfer balances the total interface force to round-off (relative imbalance below $3\times10^{-15}$) on coarser and finer solid interface meshes, whereas with interpolated traction the imbalance is $3$--$9\times10^{-5}$, the same as on a conformal interface, because the linear-geometry solid integrates the traction over its undeformed face areas (Section~\ref{sec:interface_comparison}).
The choice of transfer changes the frequency ratio of the ring by at most $2.6\times10^{-5}$ relative.
With this transfer, the solid can be refined independently of the fluid: on the collapsible channel a $640\times8$ solid through AMI brings the first trough within $0.71\%$ of the oomph-lib reference on fluid level 2, against $1.17\%$ for the conformal $320\times8$ solid on the finer fluid level 4 (Sections~\ref{sec:robustness_collapsible} and~\ref{sec:lim_thin_solid}).

\paragraph{Consistency, robustness and cost.}
On matched meshes and time steps the quasi-monolithic and partitioned solutions differ by $0.47\%$ to $0.055\%$ for the flexible-bottom cavity and by at most $1.2\%$ to $0.13\%$ in displacement and timing for the three-dimensional tube, from the coarsest to the finest level, and the differences fall by a factor of 1.5 to 4 per refinement (Sections~\ref{sec:cavity_m} and~\ref{sec:tube3d_consistency}), so the two couplings are consistent to within their discretisation error.
The quasi-monolithic coupling completes every cell of the robustness maps of the ring and the collapsible channel, including the massless wall, with two Newton iterations per step on the ring and 2.0--3.2 on the channel, insensitive to the tested density ratios; on the channel, for density ratios of 0.1 and below, Aitken reaches its iteration cap in 11 and IQN-ILS in 7 of the 12 combinations of density ratio and time step, and Robin--Neumann in 3 of the 4 time steps at 0.01, while on the ring, where the partitioned couplings complete every cell up to an added-mass ratio of 100 except one Robin--Neumann run at the strong level, they need up to 10 (Aitken), 6 (IQN-ILS) and 42 (Robin--Neumann) coupling iterations per step (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}).
At equal error against the Featflow reference on Hron--Turek FSI3, the quasi-monolithic coupling costs \tbd{factor} times \tbd{more/less} than IQN-ILS, and below an error of \tbd{value} the reference uncertainty, not the coupling, limits the comparison (Section~\ref{sec:fsi3_m}).
On the elastic half-cylinder of \citet{Liu2014}, the temporal sequence of the quasi-monolithic coupling is broadly compatible with second order (observed orders 1.77--3.17 for the top-point displacement), and its mesh-extrapolated value agrees with that of IQN-ILS to within $0.06\%$ (Section~\ref{sec:blob_m}).
On the three-dimensional tube, where it costs 3.0--3.3 times as much as Robin--Neumann coupling, the method reaches a single-node parallel efficiency of 0.78 on 128 processes relative to 16, against 0.85 for Robin--Neumann (Section~\ref{sec:tube3d_scaling}).
In one case, a thin flag in air with little added mass, Newton stagnates during the start-up at the time step of the partitioned tutorial, as IQN-ILS does, and both couplings need half that step, at which the quasi-monolithic coupling costs 1.5 times as much as IQN-ILS (Section~\ref{sec:lim_foil}): convergence of the method is insensitive to the added mass over the tested range but not to the nonlinearity of the coupled problem over a step.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
Several extensions follow from the limitations identified in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
First, the solid block of the preconditioner is factorised by a sparse LU factorisation replicated on every process in the two-dimensional cases, which limits parallel scaling for large solids; the algebraic multigrid solid block that replaces it on the three-dimensional tube was assessed only there, and its robustness for thin, nearly incompressible or strongly nonlinear solids remains to be established.
% The physics-based preconditioner hook was removed from solids4foam in 974532fbe (20 Oct 2026), so it is not named.
Second, a high-order reconstruction for the velocity solids, as already available for the displacement solids, would remove the excess stiffness of thin, bending-dominated structures (Section~\ref{sec:lim_thin_solid}).
Third, the time-step limit of Section~\ref{sec:lim_foil} calls for a globalisation of the Newton iteration beyond a backtracking line search, such as pseudo-transient continuation or adaptive time stepping on Newton failure~\citep{Knoll2004}, or for a convective flux extrapolated from previous time levels, which removes the convective nonlinearity within a step at the price of a time-lagged flux (available in the implementation as an option; every case of this paper uses the implicit flux); neither has been assessed.
Finally, larger three-dimensional cases than the tube of Section~\ref{sec:scaling_3d} are needed to establish the cost of the method at the scale at which partitioned finite volume FSI solvers are applied, and, since the fluid mesh motion is already outside the Newton system, the coupled residual could be combined with overset meshes~\citep{miller2014overset} for motions that a body-fitted ALE mesh cannot follow.
