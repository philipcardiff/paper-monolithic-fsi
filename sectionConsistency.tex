\subsection{Consistency with partitioned coupling}\label{sec:consistency_partitioned}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% Stream feeding this section: R2 (campaign/HANDOFF.md), plus the
% womersleyTube runs of R1. Source of the discretisation comparison: audit
% A2, paper-data/a2_discretisation_differences.md (origin/evidence/
% a2-fluid-discretisation-differences), whose draft text is adapted below.

The quasi-monolithic method and the partitioned couplings solve the same continuum problem with different discretisations.
The quasi-monolithic method solves the fluid as one coupled velocity--pressure system by Newton's method (in solids4foam, \texttt{newtonIcoFluid}) and the solid in velocity form, whereas the partitioned couplings use a segregated pressure-correction (PIMPLE) fluid solver (\texttt{pimpleFluid}) and the solid in displacement form.
Their solutions should therefore differ only by discretisation error and approach each other under refinement.
This subsection lists where the discretisations differ and tests this on a steady and a transient case.
It is a consistency check between two formulations that share much of their implementation, not an independent verification.

\subsubsection{Differences between the discretisations}\label{sec:fluid_differences}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
% Source: paper-data/a2_discretisation_differences.md (origin/evidence/a2-fluid-discretisation-differences),
% items D1-D17, condensed; every entry rechecked on 17 Nov 2026 against the run files of the cavity
% (timing-ma tm-cav-{qm,aitken}-L1-r1, a421f063d; Mx p201287/philip/qm-a421/runs/timing-ma.tar) and the
% pulsatile-tube tutorial variants on feature-petsc-snes-quasi-monolithic: convection unlimited
% linearUpwind grad(U) (cavity) and Gauss linear (tube) in both couplings; interface p zeroGradient
% (Aitken, cavity), movingWallPressure (IQN-ILS, tube; reruns on 9e6d1bfb2, 17 Nov 2026), elasticWallPressure
% (Robin-Neumann), fixedGradient set by the coupling (QM);
% partitioned mesh motion velocityLaplacian, quadratic inverseDistance; QM solids4foamSolidModel.

The two couplings share the solid discretisation, including the face interpolation of the stresses and the solid stabilisation, and the form of the interface traction, including the transpose part of the viscous stress (Section~\ref{sec:interface_conditions}).
They also share the time integration when both use BDF2: the BDF2 velocity formulation with BDF2 kinematics is algebraically identical to the BDF2 second time derivative of the displacement solid, given consistent start-up histories, for the linear and the nonlinear solid alike.
They differ in the fluid and at the interface (Table~\ref{tab:discretisation_differences}).
In the fluid, the Newton solver adds the Rhie--Chow stabilisation to the continuity residual and builds the continuity flux from the interpolated velocity that also convects momentum, whereas the pressure-correction solver imposes continuity on the flux corrected by the pressure equation.
At the interface they differ in the pressure condition, the source of the wall velocity and the fluid mesh motion.

Each coupling is run with the settings for which it was developed and tested: the partitioned couplings use those of the companion paper~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}, so that their results are those reported there, and the quasi-monolithic coupling those of Section~\ref{sec:numerical_methods}.
Where a choice is not tied to the coupling, the same choice is made in both: the convection scheme, the gradient scheme and, in this section, the linear interpolation of the velocity in the continuity flux.
Two choices still differ.
The interface pressure gradient comes from the normal momentum equation in both couplings, as a cell-centred interface condition that is not specific to the coupling, but in different forms: the quasi-monolithic coupling uses the full gradient of Equation~\eqref{eqn:interface_pressure_gradient}, and the partitioned Dirichlet--Neumann couplings (Aitken, IQN-ILS) its acceleration term only, $\hat{\bb{n}}_f\cdot(\bb{\nabla}p)_f = -\hat{\bb{n}}_f\cdot(\partial\bb{v}/\partial t)_f$, without the viscous terms, which the pressure-correction solver used here does not provide~\citep{solids4foamIssue721}; Robin--Neumann coupling uses its Robin condition.
On the steady cavity the wall acceleration vanishes at convergence, so the acceleration term is zero and the Aitken runs use a zero normal gradient, which gives the same steady solution.
The quasi-monolithic method was developed with the pseudo-solid mesh motion of Section~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion} and the partitioned couplings with a Laplacian mesh motion, although either coupling can use either mesh motion.
Every difference in Table~\ref{tab:discretisation_differences} is a consistent discretisation difference, expected to vanish under mesh and time-step refinement, and the two cases below test whether it does.

\paragraph{Set-up.}
The Reynolds number is $Re = \bar{U}H/\nu = 100$, based on the mean inlet velocity $\bar{U} = 1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the channel height $H = 1\,\mathrm{m}$.
The St Venant--Kirchhoff plate is computed with the geometrically nonlinear (total Lagrangian) velocity solid on a conformal interface.
Four uniformly refined levels of structured block meshes generated with \texttt{blockMesh} ($\Delta x = 0.1$, $0.05$, $0.025$ and $0.0125\,\mathrm{m}$) are computed with both couplings.
Point A is the midpoint of the top surface of the plate, at $x = 4H$ on the interface (Figure~\ref{fig:cavity_geom_m}).
% Checked 31 Oct 2026: solidPointDisplacement point (4 0 0.5) in the a421 run files; solid block y in [-0.4, 0], so y = 0 is the top surface (solids4foam PR #584).

The steady state is reached by stepping in pseudo-time after the inlet velocity has been ramped up over the first $20\,\mathrm{s}$; the steady state does not depend on the time integration, and both couplings use the Euler scheme in the fluid.
In both, the solid momentum equation contains a velocity-proportional damping term, $\rho_s c_d \bb{v}$ with $c_d = 0.1\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, as in the companion paper, which accelerates the approach to the steady state without changing it.
The quasi-monolithic coupling uses BDF2 in the solid and $\Delta t = 0.5\,\mathrm{s}$ on every level, to $t = 300\,\mathrm{s}$.
The partitioned coupling is Aitken-accelerated Dirichlet--Neumann iteration, as in the companion paper, with the Euler scheme in the solid, run to $t = 200\,\mathrm{s}$ with the steps of the companion paper, halved with each level: $\Delta t = 0.5$, $0.25$, $0.125$ and $0.0625\,\mathrm{s}$ on levels 1--4.
The smaller steps are needed on the finest level: at $\Delta t = 0.5\,\mathrm{s}$ the partitioned coupling diverges on level 4, and there the pressure-correction fluid solver alone diverges at $\Delta t \geq 0.125\,\mathrm{s}$ (mean Courant number 8).
% Source: solids4foam feature-petsc-snes-quasi-monolithic tutorials/fluidSolidInteraction/cavityFlexibleBottom/verification/README.md
% lines 64-70 (level 4 diverges at 0.5 with Aitken and IQN-ILS), 137-139 (level 3 converges at 0.25), 303-307 (fluid-only start-up).
Each Aitken step is iterated until every interface residual measure is below $10^{-5}$ (the strict test of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}); the quasi-monolithic Newton tolerances are those of Table~\ref{tab:solver_settings}.
The quasi-monolithic coupling uses linear interpolation of the velocity in the continuity flux, as the pressure-correction solver does, so that the two couplings differ only in the items of Table~\ref{tab:discretisation_differences}.

The two couplings approach each other under refinement (Table~\ref{tab:cavity_m}).
The difference in $u_y$(A) falls from $0.48\%$ on level 1 to $0.066\%$ on level 2 and below $0.01\%$ on levels 3 and 4, where it changes sign, and that in $F_y$ from $0.60\%$ to $-0.0068\%$ on level 4.
Between levels 2, 3 and 4 both couplings converge at second order in $u_y$, with ratios of successive changes of 4.10 for the quasi-monolithic coupling and 4.05 for Aitken, and at observed orders of 2.5 and 2.3 in $F_y$; their extrapolated values agree to $0.009\%$ in $u_y$ and $0.010\%$ in $F_y$.
On the two finest levels the remaining difference is below $0.01\%$ and changes sign, so no rate of decrease can be formed from it.
If the quasi-monolithic coupling uses the skewness-corrected interpolation in the continuity flux instead (described after Equation~\eqref{eqn:fluid_momentum_residual}), the two couplings no longer discretise the continuity flux in the same way, and the difference in $u_y$(A) falls more slowly, from $0.47\%$ on level 1 to $0.055\%$ on level 4.
% Skewness-corrected QM values: timing-ma tm-cav-qm-L{1..4} (a421f063d): u_y -0.203601/-0.231044/-0.238200/-0.239986.

The quasi-monolithic coupling needs 1.6--1.7 Newton iterations per step at $\Delta t = 0.5\,\mathrm{s}$ on every level, and Aitken 4.2--5.0 coupling iterations per step at its smaller steps.
Both reach the steady state at about the same pseudo-time: $u_y$(A) is within $10^{-4}$ of its final value after $167\,\mathrm{s}$ with the quasi-monolithic coupling and after $155$--$174\,\mathrm{s}$ with Aitken, so the different solid time integration and time steps change the time to steady state little.
Even so, the quasi-monolithic coupling costs 1.6 to 2.9 times as much as Aitken to reach the steady state, the ratio falling with refinement as Aitken's time step falls: a quasi-monolithic step, whose Newton iterations need 25--89 Krylov iterations on average, costs 2.7 to 13 times as much as an Aitken step.
Aitken is used here because it is the partitioned coupling of the companion paper for this case.
IQN-ILS or Robin--Neumann coupling would probably need fewer coupling iterations per step and so cost less, but would converge to the same partitioned solution, to within the coupling tolerance.

In the pulsatile tube of Section~\ref{sec:womersley_m} the wall deforms, which tests the viscous interface traction.
On a deforming wall the traction includes the transpose part of the viscous stress (Section~\ref{sec:interface_conditions}); the wall flux $\mu\,\partial\bb{v}/\partial n$ of the Laplacian form of the viscous term, which is all that a fluid solver without the transpose term provides, omits $\mu\,\hat{\bb{n}}\cdot(\bb{\nabla}\bb{v})^{T}$, which vanishes on a stationary or rigidly translating wall but not on a deforming one.
This is a physical traction component, not a discretisation error, so it does not vanish under refinement; for a compliant tube of radius $R$, wall thickness $h$ and angular frequency $\omega$ its size relative to the pressure is about $\mu\omega R/(Eh)$.
Both couplings include it.
To show its effect, the quasi-monolithic runs are also made without the transpose term, in both the momentum equation and the traction, so that two forms of the viscous term are compared.
Both couplings are compared on mesh factors 1, 2 and 4 at 200 steps per period and on 50 to 400 steps per period on the factor-2 mesh.

% tutorial schemes) and wp0-wom-qm-vstno-m2n50; with it: rf-wom-qm (evidence/m-runs-a-rfix, 1252bd2e5).

\paragraph{Measured.}
We measure the differences of the quasi-monolithic solution from the Robin--Neumann and IQN-ILS solutions in every quantity of Section~\ref{sec:womersley_m}; the Robin--Neumann minus IQN-ILS difference sets the scale of the spread between the partitioned solutions.

	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/womersleyTube-consistency}
	\caption{Pulsatile compliant tube: magnitude of the quasi-monolithic minus Robin--Neumann difference (relative; phase in rad) with the quasi-monolithic traction without (open) and with (filled) the transpose part of the viscous stress, (a) against mesh factor at 200 steps per period and (b) against steps per period on the factor-2 mesh. Grey: the largest IQN-ILS minus Robin--Neumann difference of the four quantities, the partitioned spread.}
	\label{fig:womersley_consistency}

Without the transpose term the wall-displacement phase of the quasi-monolithic solution differs from Robin--Neumann by $-3.7$ to $-3.9\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{rad}$ on factors 2 and 4 at every time step ($-5.1\times10^{-4}$ on factor 1), and the wall amplitude by $-2.3$ to $-2.9\times10^{-4}$ on factors 2 and 4, while the flow rate, wave speed and pressure agree to $8\times10^{-5}$ or better (Figure~\ref{fig:womersley_consistency}): an offset that refinement does not remove, as expected from a traction missing a physical component on a deforming wall.
With the transpose term the phase difference falls five- to sevenfold, to $(5.7$--$7.2)\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{rad}$ on factors 2 and 4, and the attenuation difference to $4\times10^{-5}$ on factor 2 and $9\times10^{-5}$ on factor 4.
The wall-amplitude difference falls to $-0.5$ to $-0.7\times10^{-4}$ on factor 2 and $-1.1\times10^{-4}$ on factor 4, but does not decrease with refinement; it is of the size of the partitioned spread (IQN-ILS minus Robin--Neumann: $-1.8$ to $+1.0\times10^{-4}$ on factors 1 and 2 and $-4.5\times10^{-4}$ on factor 4).
The velocity profile at mid-length is the one quantity in which the couplings differ by much more than the partitioned spread on every mesh and step: by $6$--$8\times10^{-4}$ of the peak velocity in the second cell from the wall on every mesh and step, against $4$--$8\times10^{-5}$ on the axis, while the flow rate agrees to $4\times10^{-5}$.
We attribute this difference to where the linearised reference is sampled rather than to either solver.
The linearised solution does not fix whether it applies at the undeformed or at the current position of a point, and near the wall the two differ by $O(\eta\,\partial u/\partial r)$. The fluid mesh is displaced by $\mathbf{D}(t)-\mathbf{D}(0)$, so the near-wall cell centres keep a static offset $-\eta(0)\approx-2.3\times10^{-4}R$ at mid-length, set by the exact initial state; this changes the exact first harmonic there by $7$--$9\times10^{-4}$ of the peak velocity.
The pseudo-solid mesh motion of the quasi-monolithic runs carries the near-wall solution with the current cell centres, whereas the Laplacian mesh motion of the partitioned runs leaves it at the undeformed centres, at which the errors are evaluated.
A fluid-only run with the exact wall motion prescribed reproduces the difference when only the mesh motion is changed. Stiffening the wall a thousandfold leaves the difference unchanged ($6.8$ against $7.1\times10^{-4}$), consistent with an offset that does not depend on the wall stiffness.

With all amplitudes reduced tenfold (every exact amplitude scales by exactly $0.1$, while the wavenumber and the Womersley number are unchanged), the ambiguity falls to $8\times10^{-5}$ and the two couplings give the same velocity profile.
Its error, the largest over the radius relative to the scaled exact peak, differs between them by $0.2$--$2.4\%$ of its value on factors 1, 2 and 4 at 200 steps per period and on 50 to 400 steps per period on the factor-2 mesh, and by $5.5\%$ on the factor-8 mesh at 400 steps per period ($2.55$ against $2.69\times10^{-4}$).
The quasi-monolithic error converges at observed orders $1.84$, $2.01$ and $1.77$ between successive meshes, to $3.7\times10^{-4}$ on the factor-8 mesh, where at full amplitude it stops at $7.7\times10^{-4}$; the factor-8 Robin--Neumann run at 200 steps per period diverges in its first step, as it does at full amplitude.
At this amplitude the couplings agree in the other quantities on the factor-8 mesh to $6\times10^{-5}$ or better (phases in rad), except the wall displacement amplitude ($1.9\times10^{-4}$, the size of the partitioned spread above).
