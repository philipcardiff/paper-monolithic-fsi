% Set-up: solids4foam origin/fix/foilInWind-monolithic-timestep @ b157ecaf3
% (solids4foam PR #602), tutorials/fluidSolidInteraction/foilInWind/README.md
% and system/controlDict.monolithic (dt = 0.0025 s); case summary from A1
% Section 1.10. The README cites no benchmark; the flapping "has not been

A thin flexible foil attached behind a rigid block in a uniform air stream (Figure~\ref{fig:foil_geom_m}) flaps in three dimensions.
The block is $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ square in cross-section and $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ long; the foil is $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ long, $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ wide and $0.6\,\mathrm{mm}$ thick, a St Venant--Kirchhoff solid with $E = 2\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\rho_s = 2000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, in air at $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
The fluid mesh has 176\,248 cells and the solid mesh $32\times6\times24$ cells.
No reference solution exists; the case compares the time step and cost needed by the two couplings.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[scale=1]{figures/foilInWind-geometry}
	\caption{Flexible foil behind a rigid block: geometry and base mesh in side and top view (dimensions in cm).}
	\label{fig:foil_geom_m}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Measured.}
Both couplings are run to $t = 0.2\,\mathrm{s}$, through the start-up, with $\Delta t = 0.01$, $0.005$ and $0.0025\,\mathrm{s}$; IQN-ILS uses the strict convergence test with the tutorial tolerance of $10^{-5}$ and at most 50 coupling iterations, and the quasi-monolithic coupling the tutorial Newton tolerance of $10^{-4}$, a backtracking line search and at most 50 Newton iterations.
Adaptive time stepping, which the implementation provides, is off in every run.
The cost is compared at $\Delta t = 0.005\,\mathrm{s}$ on the same eight processes of one workstation.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/foilInWind-stall}
	\caption{Flexible foil behind a rigid block, $\Delta t = 0.01\,\mathrm{s}$: (a) Newton residual of the quasi-monolithic coupling relative to its value at the start of each step, and (b) interface residual of IQN-ILS, against the iteration count, for each step until the run stops; dotted: tolerance.}
	\label{fig:foil_stall}

\paragraph{Results.}
With $\Delta t = 0.01\,\mathrm{s}$ neither coupling completes the start-up (Figure~\ref{fig:foil_stall}).
Newton needs 4 to 5 iterations in the first three steps and 7 in the fourth; in the fifth, at $t = 0.05\,\mathrm{s}$, the residual falls to $37\%$ of its initial value within about 15 iterations and then stagnates, with the line search shortening the step, until the iteration limit.
IQN-ILS coupling iterations grow likewise (4, 5, 5, 6, 8, 10 and 18 in the first seven steps), and at $t = 0.08\,\mathrm{s}$ the interface residual stagnates at $2.5\times10^{-5}$, above the tolerance.
Right preconditioning, unlimited convection and other differencing parameters of the matrix-free product stall likewise, so the Newton stagnation is attributed to the nonlinearity of the coupled problem over a large step rather than to the linear solver, as the near-simultaneous failure of IQN-ILS supports.
With $\Delta t = 0.005\,\mathrm{s}$ both couplings complete: the quasi-monolithic coupling with 3.3 Newton iterations per step (at most 4) and 36 Krylov iterations per Newton iteration, and IQN-ILS with 5.4 coupling iterations per step (at most 8); the quasi-monolithic run takes $337\,\mathrm{s}$ and the IQN-ILS run $230\,\mathrm{s}$, 1.5 times less.
With $\Delta t = 0.0025\,\mathrm{s}$ the counts fall to 2.9 Newton and 4.7 coupling iterations per step.
Here, with a dense solid ($\rho_s/\rho_f \approx 1700$) and hence little added mass, the quasi-monolithic coupling gains no robustness and costs more per step than IQN-ILS.
The method has no globalisation beyond a backtracking line search; adaptive time stepping on Newton failure, or a continuation method, may let it pass such start-ups automatically, but neither is assessed here.

\subsection{Solid block of the preconditioner}\label{sec:lim_solid_pc}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

% Historical (April 2026, commit 2f99ea065): per-process bjacobi+LU on the

% Check B1: evidence/campaign-g1:paper-data/campaign/b1/findings.md
% (solids4foam 257b2e564; ring factor 2 moderate, cavity L3, 3dTube L2).
% Tube 4/16-rank times, memory and same-time claim: B1 (XenoSim shared node,
% tutorial tolerances of the time; 279 vs 1194 s at 4 ranks, 108 vs 1296 s at 16,
% i.e. 4.3x and 12x). The 16-rank factor quoted is the controlled MeluXina
% measurement of tab:tube3d_pc (a421f063d, current defaults, 3 repeats): 8.03x.
In the two-dimensional cases the solid block of the preconditioner is factorised exactly by sparse LU, replicated on every process: robust, and affordable because the solid block is small relative to the fluid block, but with a cost and memory that do not fall with the process count and grow with the solid size.
On level 2 of the three-dimensional tube the factorisation takes the same time on 16 processes as on 4, with its memory growing from 9 to 25\,GB; replacing it by one BoomerAMG V-cycle, at the same Newton and Krylov iteration counts, makes the run 8 times faster on 16 processes (Table~\ref{tab:tube3d_pc}) and about 4 times faster on 4 processes in an indicative measurement on a shared node, so BoomerAMG is used for the tube (Sections~\ref{sec:tube3d_pc_study} and~\ref{sec:tube3d_scaling}).
In the two-dimensional cases the exact factorisation remains cheaper: BoomerAMG doubles the Krylov iterations on the ring and costs 1.4--1.9 times as much, and on the cavity it costs 10--14\% more.
With either preconditioner, each monitored quantity agrees with a run converged to a hundred times tighter Newton tolerances to within $7\times10^{-5}$ of its largest magnitude, close to the monitors' output resolution, so the choice affects cost only.
An algebraic multigrid solid block for thin, nearly incompressible or strongly nonlinear solids has not been assessed.

\subsection{Velocity solid on thin structures}\label{sec:lim_thin_solid}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
% Source: companion paper sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex, "Collapsible channel"
% (Solid bullet) and "Ring added-mass problem" (Solid discretisation bullet);
% collapsibleChannel/verification/README.md "Quasi-monolithic coupling".
% The high-order (MLS) solid is not available for velocity solids and will
% not be added for this paper (Philip's decision).

The velocity solids use the standard second-order cell-centred discretisation with linear reconstruction.
For thin, bending-dominated structures meshed with few, elongated cells across the thickness, this discretisation is too stiff: in a static test of the collapsible-channel wall it is between $16.7\%$ and $0.22\%$ too stiff over the solid meshes of the companion paper, whereas a high-order reconstruction is within $0.25\%$ on every mesh~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.
The high-order reconstruction is unavailable for velocity solids, so the accuracy of the quasi-monolithic coupling on such cases is limited by the solid unless it is refined along its length; with a non-conformal interface the solid can be refined independently of the fluid: on the collapsible channel a $640\times8$ solid through AMI on fluid level 2 brings the first trough closer to the oomph-lib reference than the conformal $320\times8$ solid on fluid level 4 ($0.71\%$ against $1.17\%$), in about a third of the wall time (Section~\ref{sec:robustness_collapsible}).
% B2: evidence/m-runs-b:paper-data/campaign/wp3/b2_618/ (3823 s against 11080 s, shared XenoSim node, indicative).
The wet/dry frequency ratio of the ring problem is insensitive to this error, because the wet and dry runs share it (Section~\ref{sec:ring_m}).

\subsection{Fluid mesh motion}\label{sec:lim_mesh_motion}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

The fluid mesh is moved once per step to a displacement extrapolated from the solid (Section~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion}), outside the Newton system.
Two mesh-motion failures occurred in the campaign; both were removed by changing the mesh-motion set-up, not the coupling.
On the collapsible channel at the largest time step, the first iterate of the nonlinear pseudo-solid carried the whole wall increment of a step into the half-cells next to the wall and stopped the mesh-motion solve, while Newton still converged in three iterations per step; extrapolating the first iterate in time removed the failure (Section~\ref{sec:robustness_collapsible}).
On the refined Hron--Turek meshes, whose first cells at the flag tip are $0.3\,\mathrm{mm}$ thick, an incremental Laplacian mesh motion on the moving mesh inverted cells near the tip within the first second of a replayed flag motion on levels 2 to 4, whereas solving each step from the initial mesh, with a linear inverse-distance stiffness, kept every cell valid; the incremental error accumulates with the motion.
A linear elastic pseudo-solid with the same stiffness (Section~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion}) keeps every cell valid: assembled on the initial mesh, it moves levels 2 to 4 through $5.2\,\mathrm{s}$ of replayed flag motion, about 28 periods, with a largest non-orthogonality of $39$--$45^\circ$, close to that of the initial meshes; assembled on the current mesh (the form used in the coupled runs of Section~\ref{sec:fsi3_m}, as the only one the partitioned couplings accept), it moves levels 2 and 3 past the Laplacian failure times with no invalid cell.
A quasi-monolithic method that moves the mesh outside its Newton system thus inherits the limits of its mesh-motion solver, which must be chosen for the largest interface increment per step and the smallest cells next to the interface.

\subsection{Software}\label{sec:lim_platform}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
% Source: A3 Section 4 (paper-data/a3_pr233_merge_readiness.md): the coupler
% source is inside #ifdef USE_PETSC / #ifdef OPENFOAM_COM; CI on 257b2e564
% builds OpenFOAM v2312, v2412, v2512, OpenFOAM-9 and foam-extend-4.1, with and
% without PETSc; the monolithic tutorials stop on OpenFOAM.org and
% foam-extend.

The quasi-monolithic coupling requires the OpenFOAM.com (ESI) line of OpenFOAM and PETSc; solids4foam builds without it on the other OpenFOAM lines and without PETSc.
% MeluXina builds (qm-head, qm-a421): OpenFOAM-v2512, foss/2024a (GCC 13.3),
% PETSc 3.22.0, hypre 2.31.0; XenoSim build (wp3 3b runs): GCC 11.4, PETSc
% 3.24.1, hypre 2.33.0 (runs.csv); workstation (macOS, Clang): PETSc 3.22.2.
The results of this paper were obtained with OpenFOAM v2512 and PETSc 3.22 or 3.24; the coupling also builds with OpenFOAM v2312 and v2412 with PETSc.

\paragraph{Scope and modularity.}
Partitioned coupling in solids4foam combines any of its fluid and solid models, including displacement-based solids with high-order reconstruction, and runs on every supported OpenFOAM line~\citep{Tukovic2018, Cardiff2025}.
The quasi-monolithic coupling is restricted to the coupled Newton fluid model of solids4foam (\texttt{newtonIcoFluid}) and the velocity solids (Section~\ref{sec:numerical_methods}), to the OpenFOAM.com line with PETSc (Section~\ref{sec:lim_platform}), and to the standard second-order solid reconstruction, which limits its accuracy on thin structures (Section~\ref{sec:lim_thin_solid}).
A new physical model enters the quasi-monolithic coupling only with its contribution to the residual and to the approximate Jacobian, whereas it enters partitioned coupling through its interface data alone.
Where both couplings apply, the choice is thus between a solver whose convergence was insensitive to the added mass over the tested range, at a higher cost per iteration, and a modular solver whose cost and reliability depend on the added mass and on its acceleration settings.
