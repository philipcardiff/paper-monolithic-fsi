\section{Governing equations}\label{sec:math_model}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

% Notation is kept identical to the companion verification paper wherever the same quantity appears.
Throughout this paper, bold symbols denote vectors and tensors (e.g.\ $\bb{v}_F$, $\bb{\sigma}_S$), while a hat denotes a unit vector (e.g.\ $\hat{\bb{n}}_F$).
The uppercase subscripts $F$ and $S$ are used to denote fluid- and solid-region quantities, respectively, and a trailing subscript $0$ denotes evaluation in the initial (reference) configuration.
Where no ambiguity arises, the region subscript is omitted for conciseness.
The gradient operator $\bb{\nabla}$ is treated as a standard vector operator, consistent with the divergence $\bb{\nabla} \cdot$, curl $\bb{\nabla} \times$ and related operations, as adopted in OpenFOAM; with this convention, $(\bb{\nabla} \bb{a})_{ij} = \partial a_j / \partial x_i$ for a vector field $\bb{a}$, which is the transpose of the gradient tensor in the more usual continuum-mechanics convention.
The subscript $0$ on the gradient, $\bb{\nabla}_0$, indicates differentiation with respect to the initial (reference) coordinates.
Body forces are expressed per unit mass throughout.

The present work considers the dynamic interaction between an incompressible Newtonian fluid and a deformable elastic solid, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:fsi_problem}.
At time $t$, the coupled system occupies the moving domain
\begin{equation}
    \Omega(t) = \Omega_F(t) \cup \Omega_S(t),
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_F(t)$ and $\Omega_S(t)$ denote the fluid and solid domains, respectively.
Their boundaries are denoted by $\Gamma_F(t)$ and $\Gamma_S(t)$, with outward-facing unit normals $\hat{\bb{n}}_F$ and $\hat{\bb{n}}_S$.
The fluid--solid interface is the common boundary
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma_\text{fsi}(t)
    =
    \Gamma_F(t) \cap \Gamma_S(t),
\end{equation}
on which the fluid and solid satisfy kinematic compatibility and dynamic traction equilibrium.

The solid is formulated in a total Lagrangian description with respect to its initial configuration $\Omega_{S,0}$, while the fluid is formulated on the current moving domain $\Omega_F(t)$ using an arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian (ALE) description.
The solid velocity $\bb{v}_S$, rather than the solid displacement, is the primary solid unknown, so that velocity is the unknown on both sides of the interface.
For notational compactness, the explicit time dependence of moving domains and boundaries is omitted where no ambiguity arises.

\subsection{Solid model} \label{sec:solid_model}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

The motion of the solid is governed by conservation of linear momentum.
In the current configuration, this may be written in integral form as
\begin{equation}
    \int_{\Omega_S}
    \rho_S
    \frac{\partial \bb{v}_S}{\partial t}
    \, \text{d}\Omega_S
    =
    \oint_{\Gamma_S}
    \hat{\bb{n}}_S \cdot \bb{\sigma}_S
    \, \text{d}\Gamma_S
    +
    \int_{\Omega_S}
    \rho_S \bb{b}_S
    \, \text{d}\Omega_S ,
    \label{eqn:solid_momentum_current}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_S$ is the current density, $\bb{v}_S$ is the solid velocity, $\bb{\sigma}_S$ is the true (Cauchy) stress tensor, and $\bb{b}_S$ is a body force per unit mass.

The solid equations are solved in total Lagrangian form.
Let $\bb{D}_S$ denote the solid displacement from the initial configuration $\Omega_{S,0}$ to the current configuration $\Omega_S(t)$.
The displacement and velocity are related by
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial \bb{D}_S}{\partial t}
    =
    \bb{v}_S
    \qquad
    \text{in } \Omega_{S,0},
    \label{eqn:solid_displacement_velocity}
\end{equation}
where the time derivative is taken at fixed initial (material) coordinates.
The deformation gradient and its determinant are
\begin{equation}
    \bb{F}
    =
    \bb{I}
    +
    \left( \bb{\nabla}_0 \bb{D}_S \right)^T,
    \qquad
    J = \det \bb{F},
    \label{eqn:deformation_gradient}
\end{equation}
where $\bb{\nabla}_0$ denotes differentiation with respect to the initial solid coordinates, and $\bb{I}$ is the second-order identity tensor.
The transpose reflects the gradient convention stated in the introduction to this section; in index notation, $F_{ij} = \delta_{ij} + \partial D_{S,i} / \partial X_j$.

Using Nanson's relation,
\begin{equation} \label{eqn:nanson}
    \hat{\bb{n}}_S \, \text{d}\Gamma_S
    =
    J \bb{F}^{-T} \cdot \hat{\bb{n}}_{S,0} \, \text{d}\Gamma_{S,0},
\end{equation}
and introducing the first Piola--Kirchhoff stress tensor $\bb{P}_S = J \bb{\sigma}_S \bb{F}^{-T}$, the solid momentum equation may be expressed over the initial configuration as
\begin{equation}
    \int_{\Omega_{S,0}}
    \rho_{S,0}
    \frac{\partial \bb{v}_S}{\partial t}
    \, \text{d}\Omega_{S,0}
    =
    \oint_{\Gamma_{S,0}}
    \hat{\bb{n}}_{S,0} \cdot \bb{P}_S^T
    \, \text{d}\Gamma_{S,0}
    +
    \int_{\Omega_{S,0}}
    \rho_{S,0} \bb{b}_S
    \, \text{d}\Omega_{S,0} .
    \label{eqn:solid_momentum_TL}
\end{equation}
Here $\rho_{S,0}$ is the initial density, and the reference-configuration traction is $\bb{t}_0 = \hat{\bb{n}}_{S,0} \cdot \bb{P}_S^T$.
Equations~\eqref{eqn:solid_displacement_velocity}--\eqref{eqn:solid_momentum_TL}, closed by a constitutive law for $\bb{\sigma}_S$, are solved for $\bb{v}_S$, with $\bb{D}_S$ and hence $\bb{F}$ and $J$ following from Equation~\eqref{eqn:solid_displacement_velocity}.

For finite deformations, the solid is modelled as a Saint Venant--Kirchhoff material, for which the second Piola--Kirchhoff stress is linear in the Green--Lagrange strain $\bb{E}$:
\begin{equation}
    \bb{\sigma}_S
    =
    \frac{1}{J}
    \bb{F} \cdot \bb{S}_S \cdot \bb{F}^T,
    \qquad
    \bb{S}_S
    =
    2 \mu_S \bb{E}
    +
    \lambda_S \, \text{tr}\left( \bb{E} \right) \bb{I},
    \qquad
    \bb{E}
    =
    \frac{1}{2}
    \left(
    \bb{F}^T \cdot \bb{F} - \bb{I}
    \right),
    \label{eqn:stvk}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_S$ and $\lambda_S$ are the Lam\'e parameters, related to the Young's modulus $E_S$ and Poisson's ratio $\nu_S$ by $\mu_S = E_S / [2(1 + \nu_S)]$ and $\lambda_S = \nu_S E_S / [(1 + \nu_S)(1 - 2\nu_S)]$.
Where the displacement gradient remains small, a small-strain (linear geometry) formulation is used instead: the distinction between the initial and current configurations is neglected in Equation~\eqref{eqn:solid_momentum_TL} ($\bb{F} \approx \bb{I}$, $J \approx 1$, $\bb{P}_S \approx \bb{\sigma}_S$), and the stress is given by Hooke's law,
\begin{equation}
    \bb{\sigma}_S
    =
    2 \mu_S \bb{\varepsilon}
    +
    \lambda_S \, \text{tr}\left( \bb{\varepsilon} \right) \bb{I},
    \qquad
    \bb{\varepsilon}
    =
    \text{sym}
    \left[
    \bb{\nabla}_0 \bb{D}_S
    \right],
    \label{eqn:hooke}
\end{equation}
where $\text{sym}[ \bb{A} ] = \frac{1}{2} (\bb{A} + \bb{A}^T)$ denotes the symmetric part.
Both formulations share the velocity form of Equations~\eqref{eqn:solid_displacement_velocity} and \eqref{eqn:solid_momentum_TL}.
Planar two-dimensional cases assume plane strain, and axisymmetric cases (the compliant tube of Section~\ref{sec:womersley_m}) are solved on a wedge mesh one cell thick in the circumferential direction.

\subsection{Fluid model}
\label{sec:fluid_governing_eqn}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

The fluid is assumed to be laminar, incompressible, Newtonian and isothermal.
It is formulated on the current moving domain $\Omega_F(t)$ using an arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian (ALE) description.
Let $\bb{v}_F$ denote the fluid velocity and let $\bb{v}^{\Gamma}$ denote the velocity of the moving fluid domain.
The motion of $\Omega_F(t)$ is prescribed on the fluid--solid interface by the kinematic condition in Section~\ref{sec:fsi_interface}; its extension into the interior of the fluid domain is a numerical mesh-motion procedure described in Section~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion}.

For an incompressible fluid, conservation of mass over a moving control volume may be written as
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d}{dt}
    \int_{\Omega_F}
    \text{d}\Omega_F
    +
    \oint_{\Gamma_F}
    \hat{\bb{n}}_F
    \cdot
    \left(
    \bb{v}_F - \bb{v}^{\Gamma}
    \right)
    \, \text{d}\Gamma_F
    =
    0 .
    \label{eqn:fluid_continuity_ale_full}
\end{equation}
The moving domain is required to satisfy the geometric conservation law,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d}{dt}
    \int_{\Omega_F}
    \text{d}\Omega_F
    -
    \oint_{\Gamma_F}
    \hat{\bb{n}}_F
    \cdot
    \bb{v}^{\Gamma}
    \, \text{d}\Gamma_F
    =
    0 ,
    \label{eqn:gcl}
\end{equation}
and subtracting Equation~\eqref{eqn:gcl} from Equation~\eqref{eqn:fluid_continuity_ale_full}, the mass conservation equation reduces to
\begin{equation}
    \oint_{\Gamma_F}
    \hat{\bb{n}}_F
    \cdot
    \bb{v}_F
    \, \text{d}\Gamma_F
    =
    0 .
    \label{eqn:fluid_continuity_ale}
\end{equation}

For an incompressible Newtonian fluid with kinematic pressure $p_F$, the kinematic stress tensor is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \bb{\sigma}_F^*
    =
    2 \nu \; \text{sym}
    \left[
    \bb{\nabla} \bb{v}_F
    \right]
    -
    p_F \bb{I}
    =
    \nu
    \left[
    \bb{\nabla} \bb{v}_F
    +
    \left( \bb{\nabla} \bb{v}_F \right)^T
    \right]
    -
    p_F \bb{I},
    \label{eqn:fluid_stress}
\end{equation}
where $\nu$ is the kinematic viscosity.
The true (Cauchy) stress is recovered as $\bb{\sigma}_F = \rho_F \bb{\sigma}_F^*$, where $\rho_F$ is the fluid density.
Following standard incompressible CFD practice, the pressure $p_F$ used throughout this paper is the kinematic pressure (the physical pressure divided by $\rho_F$).
Conservation of linear momentum in ALE form is written as
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d}{dt}
    \int_{\Omega_F}
    \bb{v}_F
    \, \text{d}\Omega_F
    +
    \oint_{\Gamma_F}
    \hat{\bb{n}}_F
    \cdot
    \left(
    \bb{v}_F - \bb{v}^{\Gamma}
    \right)
    \bb{v}_F
    \, \text{d}\Gamma_F
    =
    \oint_{\Gamma_F}
    \hat{\bb{n}}_F
    \cdot
    \bb{\sigma}_F^*
    \, \text{d}\Gamma_F
    +
    \int_{\Omega_F}
    \bb{b}_{F}
    \, \text{d}\Omega_F ,
    \label{eqn:fluid_momentum_ale}
\end{equation}
where $\bb{b}_{F}$ is a body force per unit mass.
The body forces $\bb{b}_F$ and $\bb{b}_S$ are zero in all cases considered here and are retained only for generality.

The fluid boundary conditions considered are inlet, outlet, no-slip wall, slip and symmetry conditions; interface conditions coupling the fluid to the solid are described in Section~\ref{sec:fsi_interface}.

\subsection{Interface conditions}
\label{sec:fsi_interface}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

The fluid and solid are coupled by kinematic and dynamic conditions on the fluid--solid interface $\Gamma_\text{fsi}(t)$.
The kinematic condition enforces continuity of velocity between the fluid and solid and also prescribes the motion of the fluid-domain boundary:
\begin{equation}
    \bb{v}_F
    =
    \bb{v}_S
    =
    \bb{v}^{\Gamma}
    =
    \frac{\partial \bb{D}_S}{\partial t}
    \qquad
    \text{on } \Gamma_\text{fsi}(t).
    \label{eqn:kinematic_interface}
\end{equation}
This condition enforces no-slip at all times; combined with consistent initial conditions, it also prevents gap formation or overlap at the interface, the fluid interface displacement being equal to $\bb{D}_S$.
Since $\bb{v}_S$ is the primary solid unknown, Equation~\eqref{eqn:kinematic_interface} couples the fluid and solid unknowns directly, without differentiating the solid displacement in time.
Away from the interface, the extension of the boundary motion into the fluid domain is a numerical mesh-motion procedure and is described in Section~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion}.

The dynamic condition enforces equilibrium of the fluid and solid tractions,
\begin{equation}
    \bb{t}_F + \bb{t}_S = \bb{0} \qquad \text{on } \Gamma_\text{fsi}(t),
    \label{eqn:dynamic_interface_compact}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
    \bb{t}_F = \hat{\bb{n}}_F \cdot \bb{\sigma}_F = \rho_F \; \hat{\bb{n}}_F \cdot \bb{\sigma}_F^* ,
    \qquad \bb{t}_S = \hat{\bb{n}}_S \cdot \bb{\sigma}_S,
    \qquad \hat{\bb{n}}_F = -\hat{\bb{n}}_S.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, in terms of the kinematic fluid stress,
\begin{equation}
    \rho_F \; \hat{\bb{n}}_F \cdot \bb{\sigma}_F^* = -\hat{\bb{n}}_S \cdot \bb{\sigma}_S \qquad \text{on } \Gamma_\text{fsi}(t).
    \label{eqn:dynamic_interface}
\end{equation}
The fluid traction comprises both the pressure and the viscous contributions in Equation~\eqref{eqn:fluid_stress}.
In the total Lagrangian solid, Equation~\eqref{eqn:dynamic_interface} is imposed through the reference-configuration traction $\bb{t}_0$ via Nanson's relation~\eqref{eqn:nanson}.
In the monolithic method of Section~\ref{sec:numerical_methods}, Equations~\eqref{eqn:kinematic_interface} and \eqref{eqn:dynamic_interface} are satisfied within each Newton solve of a time step rather than by sub-iteration between separate fluid and solid solutions.
