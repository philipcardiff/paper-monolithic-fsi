This section describes the discretisation and solution of the problem of Section~\ref{sec:math_model}.
The fluid equations are discretised with cell-centred finite volumes on the current ALE mesh, and the solid equations on the reference mesh in total Lagrangian form.
The time step follows the quasi-monolithic formulation of \citet{Liu2014} and Algorithm~2 of \citet[Chapter~6]{JaimanJoshi2022}: the fluid velocity, fluid pressure and solid velocity form the Newton vector of a single coupled nonlinear solve, while the fluid mesh is moved once per step, before that solve, to a predicted interface position, and the solid displacement is updated from the converged solid velocity after it.
The method differs from those formulations in its cell-centred finite volume discretisation with Rhie--Chow-type pressure stabilisation, in treating the convective term fully implicitly rather than linearising it about an extrapolated velocity\footnote{The linearised form, with the convecting flux extrapolated from the previous time steps as in \citet[Chapter~6]{JaimanJoshi2022}, remains available as an option in the implementation, but it is not used for the results of this paper.}, and in solving the coupled system by JFNK, with an assembled approximate Jacobian used only to build the preconditioner.
The same symbols ($\bb{v}_F$, $\bb{v}_S$, $p_F$, $\bb{D}_S$, \ldots) denote the continuous fields of Section~\ref{sec:math_model} and their cell-centred discrete values.

%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
\subsection{Finite-volume discretisation}
\label{sec:fv_discretisation}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

The fluid and solid regions have separate meshes, with cell sets $\mathcal{P}^f$ and $\mathcal{P}^s$; a cell $P$ has volume $\Omega_P$ and faces $f \in \mathcal{F}_P$ with outward area vectors $\bb{\Gamma}_f$.
The unknowns are stored at the cell centroids.
Volume integrals use midpoint quadrature, $\int_{\Omega_P}\bb{\phi}\,\text{d}\Omega \approx \bb{\phi}_P\Omega_P$, and surface integrals the sum of the face fluxes, $\oint_{\Gamma_P}\bb{n}\cdot\bb{q}\,\text{d}\Gamma \approx \sum_{f}\bb{\Gamma}_f\cdot\bb{q}_f$, with face values interpolated linearly from the adjacent cell centres.
Cell gradients are computed by a first-neighbour least-squares fit, exact for linear fields~\citep{Cardiff2026},
\begin{equation}
    \left( \bb{\nabla} \bb{\phi} \right)_P
    =
    \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}_P}
    \bb{g}_{P,f} \otimes \left( \bb{\phi}_{N_f} - \bb{\phi}_{P} \right),
    \label{eqn:least_squares_gradient}
\end{equation}
where the coefficients $\bb{g}_{P,f}$ depend only on the mesh geometry.
Diffusive face fluxes are split into a compact normal-gradient part between the two adjacent cell centres and an explicit non-orthogonal correction from the interpolated cell gradient (Equation~\eqref{eqn:fluid_momentum_residual}), which keeps the implicit stencil compact.
These operators are nominally second order; the realised order depends on the convection scheme, the mesh, the boundary treatment and the stabilisation, and is assessed in Sections~\ref{sec:verification_exact}--\ref{sec:accuracy_cost}.

For the fluid equations, the convective flux is written in ALE form using the relative face flux
\begin{equation}
    \phi_f^\mathrm{rel}
    =
    \bb{\Gamma}_f
    \cdot
    \left(
    \bb{v}_f
    -
    \bb{v}^{\Gamma}_f
    \right),
    \label{eqn:relative_flux}
\end{equation}
where $\bb{v}^{\Gamma}_f$ is the face velocity of the moving fluid mesh. The
moving-mesh fluxes are constructed consistently with the discrete geometric
conservation law.

The rate of change volume integral in the fluid momentum equation is approximated using the second-order backward difference (BDF2) formula applied to a moving cell $P$:
\begin{equation}
    \left.
    \frac{d}{dt}
    \int_{\Omega_F(t)}
    \bb{v}_F
    \, \text{d}\Omega_F
    \right|_P^n
    \approx
    \frac{
    3 \Omega_{F,P}^{n} \bb{v}_{F,P}^{n}
    -
    4 \Omega_{F,P}^{n-1} \bb{v}_{F,P}^{n-1}
    +
    \Omega_{F,P}^{n-2} \bb{v}_{F,P}^{n-2}
    }
    {2\Delta t}.
    \label{eqn:moving_mesh_bdf2_time_term}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eqn:moving_mesh_bdf2_time_term} and the other BDF2 expressions in this section are written for a constant time step for clarity; when the time step is adapted (Section~\ref{sec:jfnk_solution}), the corresponding variable-step BDF2 coefficients are used.

The same approximation is applied to the rate of change term in the solid momentum equation, but can be simplified, as the integration is performed over the initial (unchanging) reference volume:
\begin{equation}
    \left.
    \int_{\Omega_{S,0}}
    \frac{\partial \bb{v}_S}{\partial t}
    \, \text{d}\Omega_{S,0}
    \right|^{n}_P
    \approx
    \frac{
    3\bb{v}^{n}_{S,P}
    -
    4\bb{v}^{n-1}_{S,P}
    +
    \bb{v}^{n-2}_{S,P}
    }
    {2\Delta t}
    \Omega_{{S,0}_P}.
    \label{eqn:bdf2}
\end{equation}
The solid displacement, which is not an independent unknown in the coupled Newton system, is obtained from the solid velocity by the same BDF2 formula, as described in the next section.
All transient cases of this work use BDF2 for both the fluid and the solid; steady problems computed by pseudo-time stepping (the flexible-bottom cavity) may use the first-order Euler scheme, since their pseudo-time history is not part of the result.
BDF2 needs two old time levels, so it is started with one Euler step: on the first time step the backward scheme of OpenFOAM.com, used throughout this work, reverts to the Euler coefficients, and the solid displacement update of Equation~\eqref{eqn:solid_bdf2_displacement} does the same.
With a constant time step this start keeps the global error of second order, although \citet{Nishikawa2019bdf2} showed that a BDF1 start can give a large start-up error in BDF2 for stiff problems, in particular when the first step is taken smaller than the following ones; here the first step equals the following ones, and the quantities of interest are periodic, steady or taken after the starting transient: the pulsatile tube is analysed over its last two of six periods, and the frequency of the ring is measured with its first zero crossing excluded.

%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
\subsection{Discrete coupled residual}
\label{sec:discrete_coupled_residual}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

The coupled solve from $t^{n-1}$ to $t^n$ determines the cell-centred fluid velocity, fluid pressure and solid velocity on the fluid mesh $\Omega_F^n$, which has already been moved (Section~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion}) and is fixed during the solve; region subscripts are omitted below where unambiguous. The unknown vector is
\begin{equation}
    \bb{\mathcal{U}}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
    \bb{v}_F^n \\
    p_F^n \\
    \bb{v}_S^n
    \end{bmatrix},
    \label{eqn:coupled_unknown_vector}
\end{equation}
the solid displacement and the fluid mesh displacement being stored auxiliary fields, and the discrete coupled problem is
\begin{eqnarray}
    \bb{R}(\bb{\mathcal{U}})
    &=&
    \begin{bmatrix}
    \bb{R}_F(\bb{v}_F,p_F;\bb{v}_S) \\
    \bb{R}_S(\bb{v}_S;\bb{v}_F,p_F)
    \end{bmatrix} \label{eqn:grouped_coupled_residual} \\
    &=&
    \begin{bmatrix}
    \bb{R}_{F_m}(\bb{v}_F,p_F;\bb{v}_S) \\
    R_{F_p}(\bb{v}_F,p_F;\bb{v}_S) \\
    \bb{R}_S(\bb{v}_S;\bb{v}_F,p_F)
    \end{bmatrix}
    =
    \bb{0}.
    \label{eqn:coupled_residual}
\end{eqnarray}
with the fluid momentum residual $\bb{R}_{F_m}$, the fluid pressure residual $R_{F_p}$ and the solid momentum residual $\bb{R}_S$.
The solid velocity enters the fluid equations through the interface velocity condition, and with it the interface pressure-gradient condition and the interface flux in the continuity equation; the fluid velocity and pressure enter the solid equation through the interface traction (Section~\ref{sec:interface_conditions} and Algorithm~\ref{alg:coupled_residual}).
With $\bb{\Gamma}_f = |\bb{\Gamma}_f|\, \hat{\bb{n}}_f$ and $\bb{d}_f = \bb{x}_{N_f} - \bb{x}_P$ the vector joining the centroids of $P$ and its neighbour $N_f$, the momentum residual of fluid cell $P$ is
\begin{eqnarray}
    \bb{R}_{m,P}
    &=&
    \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}_P}
    \bigg[
       \nu
       \frac{\bb{v}_{N_f} - \bb{v}_{P}}
            {\hat{\bb{n}}_f \cdot \bb{d}_f}
       |\bb{\Gamma}_f|
       +
       \nu \bb{k}_f \cdot
       \left( \bb{\nabla} \bb{v} \right)_f
       +
       \nu \bb{\Gamma}_f \cdot
       \mathrm{dev}_2\!\left[ \left( \bb{\nabla} \bb{v} \right)^{T} \right]_f
       -
       \phi^\mathrm{rel}_f
       \widetilde{\bb{v}}_f
    \bigg]
    \notag \\
    &&-\;
    %\Omega_P    \left( \bb{\nabla} p \right)_P
    \Omega_P \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}_P} \bb{g}_{P,f} \left( p_{N_f} - p_{P} \right)
    \;-\;
    \frac{
    3 \Omega_{P} \bb{v}_{P}
    -
    4 \Omega_{P}^{n-1} \bb{v}_{P}^{n-1}
    +
    \Omega_{P}^{n-2} \bb{v}_{P}^{n-2}
    }
    {2\Delta t},
    \label{eqn:fluid_momentum_residual}
\end{eqnarray}
where $\bb{v}_f$ is the face velocity interpolated linearly with a skewness correction, used in the convective and continuity fluxes.
The first face term is the compact orthogonal part of the viscous flux and the second its non-orthogonal correction, with
\begin{equation}
    \bb{k}_f
    =
    \bb{\Gamma}_f
    -
    \frac{\bb{d}_f}
         {\hat{\bb{n}}_f \cdot \bb{d}_f}
    |\bb{\Gamma}_f|
    \label{eqn:non_orthogonal_correction_vector}
\end{equation}
and $\left( \bb{\nabla} \bb{v} \right)_f$ the interpolated least-squares velocity gradient.
The third term is the transpose part of the viscous stress, with $\mathrm{dev}_2(\bb{A}) = \bb{A} - \tfrac{2}{3}\mathrm{tr}(\bb{A})\,\bb{I}$, evaluated explicitly from the interpolated least-squares velocity gradient, as in the partitioned fluid solver of solids4foam.
Its divergence vanishes for a divergence-free velocity with constant viscosity, but its boundary flux does not vanish on a deforming wall, where it is part of the physical traction (Section~\ref{sec:fluid_differences}); on walls with a prescribed velocity, including the interface, its boundary value is therefore taken from the wall velocity, as described for the traction in Section~\ref{sec:interface_conditions}.

The fourth term is the ALE convection, with the relative flux of Equation~\eqref{eqn:relative_flux} evaluated from the current Newton iterate, so that convection is fully implicit; the convected velocity $\widetilde{\bb{v}}_f$ is obtained by central differencing (the ring, the pulsatile tube and the collapsible channel) or by unlimited second-order upwind extrapolation from the upwind cell (the other cases; Table~\ref{tab:solver_settings}).
The remaining terms are the least-squares pressure gradient and the moving-mesh BDF2 inertia of Equation~\eqref{eqn:moving_mesh_bdf2_time_term}.

The discrete pressure residual for cell $P$ is
\begin{equation}
    R_{p,P}
    =
    -
    \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}_P}
    \bb{\Gamma}_f \cdot \bb{v}_f
    \;+\;
    \mathcal{D}_{p,P},
    \label{eqn:fluid_pressure_residual}
\end{equation}
where $\bb{v}_f$ is again this interpolated face velocity, so that the face flux $\bb{\Gamma}_f \cdot \bb{v}_f$ in the continuity equation is the same (absolute) face flux that convects momentum in Equation~\eqref{eqn:fluid_momentum_residual}.
Evaluating the divergence with a different interpolation from that of the convecting flux makes the convecting flux non-solenoidal whenever the two interpolations differ, which was observed to damp transient flows.
$\mathcal{D}_{p,P}$ is the finite-volume pressure-stabilisation contribution \citep{Rhie1983} calculated as
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eqn:rhie_chow}
	\mathcal{D}_{p,P}
	=
	\sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}_P}
	\frac{\alpha_p}{a_{P_f}}
        \bigg[
	\frac{p_{N_f} - p_{P}}{\bb{\hat{n}}_f \cdot \bb{d}_f}
       -
       \frac{\bb{d}_f}{\bb{\hat{n}}_f \cdot \bb{d}_f} \cdot \left( \bb{\nabla} p \right)_f
    \bigg]
    |\bb{\Gamma}_f|
\end{eqnarray}
where $\left( \bb{\nabla} p \right)_f$ is obtained by interpolating the cell-centred least-squares pressure gradients (Equation~\eqref{eqn:least_squares_gradient}) to the face.
The term $a_{P_f}$ is the momentum-equation diagonal coefficient, linearly interpolated to the face.
The user-defined parameter $\alpha_p > 0$ globally scales the amount of stabilisation and is set to 1.0 unless otherwise stated.

The cell-centred coefficient is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\alpha_p}{a_{P}}
    =
    \alpha_p \, \Omega_{F,P}
    \left[
    \frac{3}{2\Delta t}\Omega_{F,P}
    +
    \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{int}}_P}
    \nu
    \frac{|\bb{\Gamma}_{F,f}|}
         {\hat{\bb{n}}_{F,f} \cdot \bb{d}_{F,f}}
    +
    \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{int}}_P}
    \max\left(\phi^{\mathrm{rel}}_{F,f},0\right)
    \right]^{-1},
    \label{eqn:rAU_definition}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the cell volume divided by the compact (diagonal) coefficient of the discrete fluid-momentum operator, comprising the temporal, diffusive and convective contributions, so that $\alpha_p/a_P$ has the dimension of time.
Here and in Section~\ref{sec:approximate_block_preconditioner}, the sums over $\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{int}}_P$ also include the boundary faces at which an operator contributes to the diagonal (prescribed-velocity faces for the diffusion term and outflow faces for the convection term).
Note that a first-order upwind scheme is used for the convection coefficient in this stabilisation, independently of the convection scheme used in the fluid momentum residual calculation; this keeps the sign of $a_P$ fixed for any trial velocity encountered during the Newton and finite-difference Jacobian evaluations.
The face value $\alpha_p/a_{P_f}$ used in Equation~\eqref{eqn:rhie_chow} is then obtained by linear interpolation of the two adjacent cell values.
This Rhie--Chow-type stabilisation has been shown in \citet{Cardiff2026} to disappear in the limit of mesh refinement at a second-order rate.

Equations~\eqref{eqn:fluid_momentum_residual}, ~\eqref{eqn:fluid_pressure_residual} and ~\eqref{eqn:rhie_chow} are written as interior-cell expressions.
For fluid cells adjacent to the fluid--solid interface, the interface-face terms use the velocity and pressure-gradient conditions described in Section~\ref{sec:interface_conditions}.

Because the coupled unknown contains the solid velocity $\bb{v}_S^n$ rather than the solid displacement, the new-time displacement $\bb{D}^{n}$ required to evaluate the stress is obtained from $\bb{v}_S^n$ by applying the BDF2 formula of Equation~\eqref{eqn:bdf2} to the displacement, $(3\bb{D}^{n} - 4\bb{D}^{n-1} + \bb{D}^{n-2})/(2\Delta t) = \bb{v}_S^n$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
    \bb{D}^{n}
    =
    \frac{1}{3}
    \left(
    4\bb{D}^{n-1}
    -
    \bb{D}^{n-2}
    +
    2\Delta t\, \bb{v}_S^n
    \right),
    \label{eqn:solid_bdf2_displacement}
\end{equation}
and is therefore an implicit function of the current trial value of $\bb{v}_S \equiv \bb{v}_S^n$ during the nonlinear solve.
The stress, the interface traction and the inertia are thus all evaluated at $t^n$, and no part of the internal or interface force is lagged, as it would be with, for example, a trapezoidal average of the face forces at $t^{n-1}$ and $t^n$.
Since the interface traction enters the solid residual at the same time level as the fluid unknowns that produce it, the coupling itself introduces no time lag; the numerical damping of the complete scheme, from the BDF2 time discretisation, the spatial discretisation and stabilisation, the mesh prediction and the finite solver tolerances, is assessed numerically (Section~\ref{sec:ring_m}).

For a solid control volume $P$ of initial volume $\Omega_{0,P}$, the discrete solid equation is written as
\begin{eqnarray}
    \bb{R}_{S,P}
    &=&
    \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}_P}
    \bb{T}^{\,n}_f
    \;+\;  \rho_{S,0} \Omega_{0,P}   \bb{b}_S
    \;-\;
    \rho_{S,0} \Omega_{0,P}
        \frac{
    3\bb{v}_P
    -
    4\bb{v}^{n-1}_P
    +
    \bb{v}^{n-2}_P
    }
    {2\Delta t} \notag \\
    &&
    - \;
    c_d \rho_{S,0} \Omega_{0,P} \bb{v}_{P}
        \;+\;
    \bb{\mathcal{D}}_{s,P},
    \label{eqn:solid_velocity_residual}
\end{eqnarray}
where $\bb{T}^{\,n}_f$ is the area-weighted face force (not traction) vector at the new time, $\rho_{S,0}$ is the solid density in the reference configuration, $\bb{b}_S$ is the body force per unit mass,
and $c_d$ is an optional mass-proportional Rayleigh-type damping coefficient (used in this work only to accelerate convergence to steady-state solutions of nominally transient problems, where the damping force vanishes; see Section~\ref{sec:cavity_m}).
$\bb{\mathcal{D}}_{s,P}$ is a stabilisation term -- similar to $\mathcal{D}_{p,P}$ -- calculated as
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eqn:rhie_chow_s}
	\bb{\mathcal{D}}_{s,P}
	=
	\sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}_P}
	\alpha_d \bar{K}_f
        \bigg[
	\frac{\bb{D}^{n}_{N_f} - \bb{D}^{n}_{P}}{\bb{\hat{n}}_{f,0} \cdot \bb{d}_{f,0}}
       -
       \frac{\bb{d}_{f,0}}{\bb{\hat{n}}_{f,0} \cdot \bb{d}_{f,0}} \cdot \left( \bb{\nabla}_0 \bb{D} \right)^{n}_f
    \bigg]
    |\bb{\Gamma}_{0,f}|
\end{eqnarray}
where $\bar{K}$ is a stiffness-type parameter that gives the stabilisation term an appropriate scale and dimension: $\bar{K} = 2\mu_S + \lambda_S$ for a linear elastic solid, with $\mu_S$ and $\lambda_S$ the Lam\'e parameters, and the equivalent scalar tangent stiffness, evaluated at the start of the analysis, for finite strain laws.
The user-defined parameter $\alpha_d > 0$ globally scales the amount of stabilisation.
The cases of this paper use $\alpha_d = 0.5$ or $0.1$ (Table~\ref{tab:solver_settings}).
The displacement gradient $\left( \bb{\nabla}_0 \bb{D} \right)^{n}_f$ is obtained by interpolating the cell-centred least-squares displacement gradients (Equation~\eqref{eqn:least_squares_gradient}, evaluated on the reference mesh) to the face.
As the stabilisation is formed from the new-time displacement in Equation~\eqref{eqn:solid_bdf2_displacement}, it is also a function of the trial solid velocity.

Two variants of the solid model are used, which differ only in the kinematics used to evaluate the face force.
In the geometrically nonlinear total Lagrangian variant, the new-time face force is computed as
\begin{equation}
\bb{T}_f^{n}
= \bb{\Gamma}_f^{n} \cdot \bb{\sigma}_f^{n}
= \bb{\Gamma}_{0,f} \cdot \left(\bb{P}_f^{n}\right)^T
= \left(J_f^{n}\, \bb{F}_f^{-T,\,n} \cdot \bb{\Gamma}_{0,f}\right) \cdot \bb{\sigma}_f^{n},
\label{eqn:face_force}
\end{equation}
where $\bb{\Gamma}_f^{n}$ and $\bb{\Gamma}_{0,f}$ are the outward face area vectors in the deformed and reference configurations, related by Nanson's relation, $J_f^{n}\bb{F}_f^{-T,\,n}$ is the new-time cell-centred product $J\bb{F}^{-T}$ linearly interpolated to the face, $\bb{\sigma}_f^{n}$ is the corresponding Cauchy stress, and $\bb{P}_f^{n}$ is the first Piola--Kirchhoff stress (Section~\ref{sec:solid_model}). The third form is used in the present implementation, since the reference face area vector $\bb{\Gamma}_{0,f}$ is fixed and does not require updating as the solid deforms.
The cell-centred stress is computed from the displacement $\bb{D}^{n}$ (via $\bb{F}^{n} = \bb{I} + (\bb{\nabla}_0\bb{D}^n)^T$) by the run-time selectable constitutive law and then linearly interpolated to the faces.
In the linear-geometry variant, the face force is $\bb{T}_f^{n} = \bb{\Gamma}_{0,f} \cdot \bb{\sigma}_f^{n}$, with the stress computed from the small-strain tensor of $\bb{D}^n$.
In the geometrically nonlinear variant, the stabilisation is added to the face traction before multiplication by the deformed area, which therefore replaces $|\bb{\Gamma}_{0,f}|$ in Equation~\eqref{eqn:rhie_chow_s}.

For faces on the fluid--solid interface, the solid face force is replaced by the force obtained from the fluid traction, as described in Section~\ref{sec:interface_conditions}, and the stabilisation contribution is set to zero, as on all traction boundaries.
The fluid variables thus enter Equation~\eqref{eqn:solid_velocity_residual} through this traction boundary condition on $\Gamma_\mathrm{fsi}$.
After the coupled solve has converged, the stored displacement is set from the converged velocity using Equation~\eqref{eqn:solid_bdf2_displacement}, and the stress and point displacements are updated.

%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
\subsection{Interface conditions and transfer}
\label{sec:interface_conditions}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

The fluid and solid residuals are coupled through the following discrete interface conditions, imposed on the fluid interface patch as boundary conditions that are re-evaluated at every residual evaluation from the current trial state.

\paragraph{Velocity.}
The fluid velocity on the interface is a prescribed-value (Dirichlet) condition, set to the solid velocity,
\begin{equation}
    \bb{v}_{F,f}
    =
    \bb{v}^{\Gamma}_f
    =
    \mathcal{T}_{S\to F}(\bb{v}_S)_f
    \qquad
    f \in \Gamma_\mathrm{fsi},
    \label{eqn:mapped_solid_velocity}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}_{S\to F}$ denotes the solid-to-fluid interface transfer operator defined below.
The solid velocity on each solid interface face is extrapolated from the adjacent cell centre using the cell-centred velocity gradient, $\bb{v}_{S,P} + (\bb{x}_f - \bb{x}_P)\cdot(\bb{\nabla}_0\bb{v}_S)_P$; using the cell-centre value directly would give a first-order interface velocity.

\paragraph{Pressure.}
The fluid pressure on the interface is a prescribed-gradient (Neumann) condition, whose gradient is set from the normal component of the fluid momentum equation at the moving wall,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\bb{n}}_f \cdot (\bb{\nabla} p)_f
    =
    \hat{\bb{n}}_f \cdot
    \left[
    \bb{\nabla}\cdot\left(\nu \bb{\nabla}\bb{v}\right)_{P}
    +
    \bb{\nabla}\cdot\left\{\nu\, \mathrm{dev}_2\!\left[\left(\bb{\nabla}\bb{v}\right)^{T}\right]\right\}_{P}
    -
    \left(\frac{\partial \bb{v}}{\partial t}\right)_f
    \right],
    \label{eqn:interface_pressure_gradient}
\end{equation}
where the viscous terms, including the transpose part, are evaluated in the fluid cell $P$ adjacent to the interface face $f$, as in the momentum residual, and the interface acceleration is the BDF2 difference (with the time scheme of the fluid velocity) of the interface velocity in Equation~\eqref{eqn:mapped_solid_velocity}.
The convective term does not appear, as the fluid moves with the wall.
The interface pressure, which gives the solid traction, is then extrapolated from the adjacent cell using this gradient.
Because the gradient depends on the interface acceleration, this condition carries the added-mass effect of the fluid on the solid; with it, the interface pressure and the added mass are second-order accurate in space, whereas a zero normal pressure gradient on the interface is first order.

\paragraph{Traction.}
The fluid force on interface face $f$ is
\begin{equation}
    \bb{t}_{F,f} |\bb{\Gamma}_f|
    =
    \rho_F
    \left[
    - p_f \hat{\bb{n}}_f
    +
    \nu
    \left(\bb{\nabla}\bb{v}\right)^{\perp}_f
    +
    \bb{q}_f
    \right]
    |\bb{\Gamma}_f|,
    \label{eqn:interface_fluid_traction}
\end{equation}
where $p$ is the kinematic pressure and $(\bb{\nabla}\bb{v})^{\perp}_f = (\bb{v}_f - \bb{v}_P)/(\hat{\bb{n}}_f \cdot \bb{d}_f)$ is the compact face-normal gradient of the velocity, with $\bb{d}_f$ here the vector from the centre of the adjacent cell $P$ to the face centre; this is the boundary-face form of the diffusion term in Equation~\eqref{eqn:fluid_momentum_residual}, which has no non-orthogonal correction on boundary faces.
The second viscous part, $\bb{q}_f$, is the wall value of the transpose part of the viscous stress, $\nu\,\hat{\bb{n}}_f \cdot \mathrm{dev}_2[(\bb{\nabla}\bb{v})^T]_f$, which vanishes on a stationary or rigidly moving no-slip wall but not on a deforming one (Section~\ref{sec:fluid_differences}).
Rather than from the boundary value of the cell-centred gradient, whose tangential derivatives would carry the near-wall error of the cell velocities, it is evaluated from the wall velocity itself,
\begin{equation}
    \bb{q}_f
    =
    \nu
    \left[
    \left(\bb{I} - \hat{\bb{n}}_f\hat{\bb{n}}_f\right)\cdot\left(\bb{G}_f \cdot \hat{\bb{n}}_f\right)
    -
    \hat{\bb{n}}_f\, \mathrm{tr}\left(\bb{G}_f\right)
    \right],
    \label{eqn:interface_transpose_traction}
\end{equation}
where $\bb{G}_f$ is the surface gradient of the wall velocity, from an inverse-square-distance weighted least-squares fit over the neighbouring faces of the wall, and the normal derivative of the normal velocity is taken from continuity.
This fit is zero for a rigid translation, also on a curved wall, and exact for a linearly varying wall velocity on any face spacing.
The same value enters the wall flux of the transpose term in the momentum residual, so the viscous part of Equation~\eqref{eqn:interface_fluid_traction} is the boundary flux of the momentum equation's viscous terms, and the force applied to the solid is the momentum that the fluid loses through the interface.

\paragraph{Mass flux.}
On the interface, the relative (ALE) flux in the convective term is set to zero, consistent with the no-slip condition, while the flux in the continuity equation, Equation~\eqref{eqn:fluid_pressure_residual}, is the absolute flux of the interface velocity, $\bb{\Gamma}_f \cdot \bb{v}^{\Gamma}_f$, i.e.\ the same flux that would convect momentum.
Because the mesh is moved to the predicted interface position $\bb{D}_S^{n,*}$ (Section~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion}), the wall flux $\bb{\Gamma}_f\cdot\bb{v}^\Gamma_f$ differs from the mesh flux $\phi^\Gamma_f$ of Equation~\eqref{eqn:gcl_swept_volume} by $\varepsilon_f$.
With the solid displacement updated by BDF2 from the converged velocity, $\varepsilon_f$ is the BDF2 rate of the prediction error $\bb{D}_S^{n} - \bb{D}_S^{n,*} = O(\Delta t^3)$, so $\varepsilon_f = O(\Delta t^2)$; its sum over the steps telescopes, so the volume that crosses the wall relative to the mesh stays of the order of the current prediction error and does not accumulate.
Setting the relative flux in the convective term to zero on the interface omits $\varepsilon_f \bb{v}_f$, which is of the same order.
Measured after the start-up, $\sum_f|\varepsilon_f|/\sum_f|\phi^\Gamma_f|$ falls from $2.5\times10^{-2}$ to $1.4\times10^{-4}$ on the collapsible channel ($\Delta t = 0.025$ to $0.003125\,\mathrm{s}$) and from $1.4\times10^{-2}$ to $2.8\times10^{-5}$ on the ring (25 to 200 steps per period) as $\Delta t$ is halved three times, and the leakage accumulated on the channel to $t = 0.75\,\mathrm{s}$ falls from $2.5\times10^{-4}$ to $1.7\times10^{-6}$ of the swept volume.
In the ring, which is started impulsively with the solid moving and the fluid at rest, the wall velocity drops by the added-mass factor $1+\mu$ in the first step while the explicit predictor still uses the initial solid velocity; the mesh then over-moves for two to three steps, and the volume crossing the wall relative to the mesh, $1$--$3\times10^{-4}$ of the volume swept over the run, does not grow afterwards.

\paragraph{Transfer between non-conformal interfaces.}
Interface quantities are transferred between the fluid and solid interface faces with face-area weights.
Let $w_{fs}$ denote the fraction of the area of fluid face $f$ overlapped by solid face $s$, and $w_{sf}$ the fraction of the area of solid face $s$ overlapped by fluid face $f$.
For a conformal interface, each fluid face matches one solid face and $w_{fs} = w_{sf} = 1$ for the matching pair (a direct map); for a non-conformal interface, the weights are those of the arbitrary mesh interface (AMI) method~\citep{FarrellMaddison2011}.
The velocity transfer is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{T}_{S\to F}(\bb{v}_S)_f
    =
    \sum_{s} w_{fs}\, \bb{v}_{S,s},
    \label{eqn:solid_to_fluid_transfer}
\end{equation}
and, by default, the solid traction is interpolated from the fluid traction in the same way, $\bb{t}_{S,s} = -\sum_{f} w_{sf}\, \bb{t}_{F,f}$, as in the partitioned couplings of solids4foam.
The same weights are used for every transfer in the residual (interface velocity and traction) and in the interface coupling blocks of the approximate Jacobian (Section~\ref{sec:approximate_block_preconditioner}), so that the preconditioner is consistent with the residual for conformal and non-conformal interfaces alike.

With traction interpolation, the total interface force balances only to within the discretisation error of the interface geometry.
Optionally, a conservative transfer distributes the fluid face forces to the solid faces with the transpose of the velocity transfer in Equation~\eqref{eqn:solid_to_fluid_transfer},
\begin{equation}
    \bb{F}_{S,s}
    =
    -\sum_{f} w_{fs}\, \bb{t}_{F,f} |\bb{\Gamma}_f|,
    \qquad
    \bb{t}_{S,s} = \frac{\bb{F}_{S,s}}{|\bb{\Gamma}_{0,s}|},
    \label{eqn:conservative_transfer}
\end{equation}
and the solid applies the resulting nominal traction on its undeformed face areas.
Then $\sum_s \bb{F}_{S,s}\cdot\bb{v}_{S,s} = -\sum_f \bb{t}_{F,f}|\bb{\Gamma}_f| \cdot \bb{v}^{\Gamma}_f$, so the interface power evaluated with these face values balances exactly, and, where the weights of each fluid face sum to one, so does the total interface force; these are identities of the transfer operator at the interface faces, and they do not by themselves imply discrete conservation of momentum or energy by the complete discretisation, in which, for example, the solid face velocity is reconstructed from cell values; the logged total interface force balances to round-off (Section~\ref{sec:interface_comparison}).
For the geometrically nonlinear solid, this requires the interface traction to be applied on the undeformed area (rather than the deformed area $|\bb{\Gamma}^n_s|$ of Equation~\eqref{eqn:face_force}).
The conservative transfer is used in the non-conformal ring study of Section~\ref{sec:interface_comparison} and for the $640\times8$ solid of the collapsible channel (Section~\ref{sec:robustness_collapsible}); every other case has a conformal interface and uses the direct map.

The displacement that moves the fluid mesh (Section~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion}) is transferred with the same face weights for the face values and with the point transfer of the interface mapping for the mesh points.

\subsection{Jacobian-free Newton--Krylov solution}
\label{sec:jfnk_solution}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

The coupled residual of Equation~\eqref{eqn:coupled_residual} is solved by a Newton--Krylov method: at iteration $k$, the correction satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \bb{J}(\bb{\mathcal{U}}_k)
    \Delta \bb{\mathcal{U}}_k
    =
    -
    \bb{R}(\bb{\mathcal{U}}_k),
    \qquad
    \bb{\mathcal{U}}_{k+1}
    =
    \bb{\mathcal{U}}_k
    +
    s_k \Delta \bb{\mathcal{U}}_k ,
    \label{eqn:newton_linear_system}
\end{equation}
with $\bb{J} = \partial\bb{R}/\partial\bb{\mathcal{U}}$ and a step length $s_k$.
The Jacobian is not assembled; its action on a Krylov vector $\bb{w}$ is the finite-difference directional derivative of the residual,
\begin{equation}
    \bb{J}(\bb{\mathcal{U}}_k) \bb{w}
    \approx
    \frac{
    \bb{R}(\bb{\mathcal{U}}_k + \epsilon \bb{w})
    -
    \bb{R}(\bb{\mathcal{U}}_k)
    }
    {\epsilon},
    \label{eqn:jfnk_operator}
\end{equation}
with the default differencing parameter of PETSc~\citep{PETSc}, $\epsilon = e_r\sqrt{1+\|\bb{\mathcal{U}}_k\|}/\|\bb{w}\|$ and $e_r$ the square root of the machine precision, applied to the scaled residual (see below).
The residual evaluation of Section~\ref{sec:discrete_coupled_residual} thus defines the Newton operator.
The assembled approximate Jacobian $\tilde{\bb{J}}$ of Section~\ref{sec:approximate_block_preconditioner} is used only as the preconditioning matrix; it affects the convergence of the linear iterations but not the converged solution.
Used as the Newton operator itself, it gives only linear convergence (Section~\ref{sec:tube3d_pc_study}), and it is not used so for the production results.

The Krylov solver is restarted LGMRES with left preconditioning, stopped at a relative reduction of the preconditioned linear residual; since the first Newton iteration of a step is limited by the nonlinearity, a tighter linear tolerance only adds Krylov iterations, each costing one residual evaluation.
The Newton iteration stops when either the coupled residual norm, relative to its value at the start of the step, or the Newton step norm, relative to the current iterate, falls below its tolerance: $10^{-6}$ for both, with a Krylov tolerance of $10^{-4}$ and the full step $s_k = 1$, in every case except the flag of Section~\ref{sec:lim_foil} ($10^{-4}$ and a backtracking line search; Table~\ref{tab:solver_settings}).

\paragraph{System scaling.}
The fluid residuals are written per unit fluid density (kinematic pressure), whereas the solid residual is a force, so the two blocks can differ in magnitude by many orders.
Before the residual norm is formed, the fluid rows (momentum and continuity) are multiplied by a scale factor $s_F$ and the solid rows by $s_S$; the same factors scale the corresponding rows of $\tilde{\bb{J}}$.
Row scaling does not change the solution, but it balances the contributions of the two blocks to the residual norm used for the convergence test and to the Krylov iterations.
By default $s_S$ is the average over the solid cells of $1/(\mu_S \Delta t)$, evaluated once with the initial time step and not updated when the time step is adapted, and $s_F = 1$; $s_F = 10^{8}$ in all the cases of this work.
The solid factor $s_S$ takes its default value in every case.

\paragraph{Jacobian lagging and adaptive time stepping.}
$\tilde{\bb{J}}$ and the preconditioner are rebuilt at every Newton iteration, except on the three-dimensional tube, where they are reused for eight Newton iterations and across time steps, which reduces the cost without changing the converged solution.
The implementation can also adapt the time step to the number of Newton iterations and retry a failed step with a quarter of the time step; this is off in every run of this paper, whose accuracy results are for a constant time step, and a failed nonlinear solve stops the run.


\subsection{Approximate block preconditioner}
\label{sec:approximate_block_preconditioner}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

Although the Newton--Krylov operator is applied in a Jacobian-free manner, an
assembled matrix is used to precondition the Krylov iterations. This matrix is
not the exact Jacobian of the coupled residual in
Equation~\eqref{eqn:coupled_residual}. Instead, it is an approximate Jacobian,
denoted by $\tilde{\bb{J}}$, constructed from compact finite-volume
linearisations of the fluid, solid and interface-coupling terms. Related
compact approximate Jacobians were recently shown to provide effective
preconditioners for second-order and higher-order cell-centred finite-volume
solid mechanics solvers~\citep{Cardiff2026, Batistic2026}.

Using the top-level fluid--solid grouping in Equation~\eqref{eqn:grouped_coupled_residual}, the approximate Jacobian has the
block form
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\bb{J}}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bb{J}}_{ff} & \tilde{\bb{J}}_{fs} \\
    \tilde{\bb{J}}_{sf} & \tilde{\bb{J}}_{ss}
    \end{bmatrix},
    \label{eqn:block_preconditioner}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{ff}$ is the approximate fluid velocity--pressure block,
$\tilde{\bb{J}}_{ss}$ is the approximate solid velocity block, and
$\tilde{\bb{J}}_{fs}$ and $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{sf}$ approximate the interface
coupling derivatives.
Figure~\ref{fig:block_preconditioner} summarises the block structure of $\tilde{\bb{J}}$ and the field-split preconditioner built from it (Section~\ref{sec:fieldsplit}).

\begin{figure}[tbp]
    \centering
    \input{figures/tikz/blockPreconditioner.tex}
    \caption{(a) Block structure of the approximate Jacobian $\tilde{\bb{J}}$ (Equations~\eqref{eqn:block_preconditioner} and \eqref{eqn:fluid_block_preconditioner}) for the Newton correction of the fluid velocity, fluid pressure and solid velocity; block sizes are not to scale. (b) The nested field-split preconditioner built from $\tilde{\bb{J}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:fieldsplit}); the Newton operator $\bb{J}$ itself is applied matrix-free (Equation~\eqref{eqn:jfnk_operator}).}
    \label{fig:block_preconditioner}
\end{figure}

The fluid block is itself a coupled velocity--pressure system. With the fluid
momentum and pressure equations ordered as in
Equation~\eqref{eqn:coupled_residual}, the approximate fluid Jacobian is written
as
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\bb{J}}_{ff}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bb{J}}_{mm} & \tilde{\bb{J}}_{mp} \\
    \tilde{\bb{J}}_{pm} & \tilde{J}_{pp}
    \end{bmatrix},
    \label{eqn:fluid_block_preconditioner}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{mm}$ approximates the fluid momentum operator,
$\tilde{\bb{J}}_{mp}$ the pressure-gradient coupling,
$\tilde{\bb{J}}_{pm}$ the discrete continuity operator, and
$\tilde{J}_{pp}$ the pressure-stabilisation operator.

The entries of $\tilde{\bb{J}}$ are given in Appendix~\ref{app:jacobian}.
In brief, $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{mm}$ contains the compact two-cell diffusion operator, the BDF2 time derivative and a first-order upwind linearisation of the convection, whatever the convection scheme of the residual, and omits the explicit non-orthogonal correction and the transpose part of the viscous stress, whose effect the matrix-free Jacobian action retains.
$\tilde{\bb{J}}_{mp}$ is the derivative of the least-squares pressure gradient, $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{pm}$ that of the interpolated face velocity flux, and $\tilde{J}_{pp}$ the complete linear Rhie--Chow stabilisation operator: its compact part couples first neighbours, and its interpolated-gradient part extends to the neighbours of the neighbours.
The solid block $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{ss}$ extends the compact displacement-based approximate Jacobian of \citet{Cardiff2026} to the velocity unknown; it is not a consistent linearisation of the nonlinear constitutive response.
The coupling blocks approximate the interface face velocity and pressure by the values in the adjacent cells and apply the transfer weights of Section~\ref{sec:interface_conditions}: $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{fs}$ is the Dirichlet contribution of the solid velocity to the fluid momentum and continuity rows, and $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{sf}$ the derivative of the pressure and viscous interface force on the solid with respect to the adjacent fluid cell values.

Within the velocity--pressure split, the formal pressure Schur complement of the approximate fluid block is
\begin{equation}
\tilde{S}_p = \tilde{J}_{pp} - \tilde{\bb{J}}_{pm}\tilde{\bb{J}}_{mm}^{-1}\tilde{\bb{J}}_{mp}.
\label{eqn:schur_complement}
\end{equation}
Forming $\tilde{S}_p$ explicitly would require the inverse of $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{mm}$ and is avoided; instead, $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{mm}^{-1}$ is replaced by the inverse of its diagonal, giving the sparse approximation
\begin{equation}
\hat{S}_p = \tilde{J}_{pp} - \tilde{\bb{J}}_{pm}\,\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\bb{J}}_{mm})^{-1}\tilde{\bb{J}}_{mp},
\label{eqn:schur_complement_approx}
\end{equation}
which is assembled and used to precondition the Schur-complement solve (Section~\ref{sec:fieldsplit}).
Here the stabilisation block $\tilde{J}_{pp}$, which itself approximates the pressure coupling that arises through the velocity block when the velocity is eliminated, provides a compact pressure--pressure contribution that complements the wide-stencil diagonal approximation of the second term.

The off-diagonal interface blocks are compact approximations to the coupling
derivatives. They are used only in the preconditioner; the converged nonlinear
solution is determined by the residual evaluation described in
Section~\ref{sec:discrete_coupled_residual}. Thus, the approximate Jacobian in
Equation~\eqref{eqn:block_preconditioner} affects the efficiency and robustness
of the Krylov iterations, but it does not define the nonlinear equations being
solved.

\subsubsection{Field-split preconditioner}
\label{sec:fieldsplit}

The preconditioner is built from $\tilde{\bb{J}}$ (after the row scaling of Section~\ref{sec:jfnk_solution}) as a nested PETSc field split~\citep{PETSc}.
At the outer level, the fluid and solid unknowns are split according to Equation~\eqref{eqn:block_preconditioner}, and the two blocks are combined multiplicatively, i.e.\ as one block Gauss--Seidel sweep: the fluid block is solved approximately first, and the solid block is then solved approximately with a right-hand side updated by the coupling block $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{sf}$.
The interface coupling therefore enters the preconditioner in one direction, while the Krylov method, applied to the matrix-free operator, accounts for both.

The fluid block is preconditioned by a nested velocity--pressure Schur-complement split, based on the block factorisation of Equation~\eqref{eqn:fluid_block_preconditioner} with the upper-triangular (or full) factor.
The velocity block $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{mm}$ is approximated by block Jacobi, with an incomplete LU factorisation without fill-in, ILU(0), on each parallel subdomain.
The Schur complement is approximated by $\hat{S}_p$ in Equation~\eqref{eqn:schur_complement_approx} and preconditioned by one V-cycle of the BoomerAMG algebraic multigrid method of HYPRE~\citep{hypre}.
Each inner block is applied once (no inner Krylov iterations), so the preconditioner is a fixed linear operator and a standard (non-flexible) Krylov method can be used.

The solid block $\tilde{\bb{J}}_{ss}$, which is much smaller than the fluid block in the cases considered, is preconditioned either by an exact sparse LU factorisation, of which every process holds a complete copy in parallel (a replicated factorisation), or by one V-cycle of BoomerAMG.
The tutorial settings use the LU factorisation, which is the cheaper choice for the small two-dimensional solid blocks; for the three-dimensional tube, whose finer solid meshes make the replicated factorisation dominate the cost, BoomerAMG is used (the converged solution does not depend on the choice).
Every case of Sections~\ref{sec:verification_exact}--\ref{sec:accuracy_cost} uses the LU factorisation and the three-dimensional tube of Section~\ref{sec:scaling_3d} uses BoomerAMG (Table~\ref{tab:solver_settings}).

\subsection{Fluid mesh motion}
\label{sec:fluid_mesh_motion}

The fluid mesh motion extends the interface displacement into the fluid domain while keeping the mesh valid; it is a numerical device, not a conservation law.
The mesh is moved once per time step, before the coupled solve, and is not changed during the Newton iterations.
Following \citet{Liu2014} and \citet[Algorithm~2]{JaimanJoshi2022}, the interface position at the new time is predicted by extrapolating the solid displacement with the solid velocities of the two previous steps (a second-order Adams--Bashforth step),
\begin{equation}
    \bb{D}_S^{n,*}
    =
    \bb{D}_S^{n-1}
    +
    \frac{\Delta t}{2}
    \left(
    3\bb{v}_S^{n-1}
    -
    \bb{v}_S^{n-2}
    \right).
    \label{eqn:solid_predicted_displacement}
\end{equation}
These constant-step coefficients are used also when the time step is adapted, so the prediction is then not second-order accurate; every run in this paper uses a constant time step, and its temporal-accuracy results hold only for that case.
$\bb{D}_S^{n,*}$ is used only to move the fluid mesh: the solid stress uses the implicit displacement of Equation~\eqref{eqn:solid_bdf2_displacement}, and, as the next prediction starts from the corrected $\bb{D}_S^{n}$, the prediction error does not accumulate.
On the interface, the fluid mesh displacement $\bb{D}^{\Gamma}$ is the transferred prediction,
\begin{equation}
    \bb{D}^{\Gamma}
    =
    \mathcal{T}_{S\to F}\left(\bb{D}_S^{n,*}\right)
    \qquad
    \text{on } \Gamma_\mathrm{fsi},
    \label{eqn:mesh_displacement_interface}
\end{equation}
with the face transfer of Section~\ref{sec:interface_conditions} and the corresponding point transfer; it is zero on the other fluid boundaries and tangential on symmetry planes.

In the interior, $\bb{D}^{\Gamma}$, the total displacement from the initial fluid mesh, is computed by treating the fluid mesh as a pseudo-solid, with a solid model of solids4foam used as the mesh motion solver\footnote{In the implementation, the \texttt{solids4foamSolidModel} mesh motion solver.}.
For the linear elastic law, each fluid cell $P$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \oint_{\Gamma_{0,P}}
    \delta \;
    \hat{\bb{n}}_0
    \cdot
    \left[
    \mu^\Gamma
    \bb{\nabla}_0 \bb{D}^{\Gamma}
    +
    \mu^\Gamma
    \left(
    \bb{\nabla}_0 \bb{D}^{\Gamma}
    \right)^T
    +
    \lambda^\Gamma
    \left(
    \bb{\nabla}_0 \cdot \bb{D}^{\Gamma}
    \right)
    \bb{I}
    \right]
    \, \text{d}\Gamma_{0,P}
    =
    \bb{0},
    \label{eqn:mesh_motion_pseudo_solid}
\end{equation}
discretised with the cell-centred JFNK solid solver of \citet{Cardiff2026}, where $\mu^\Gamma$ and $\lambda^\Gamma$ are artificial stiffnesses and $\delta$ an optional dimensionless stiffness weight.
The equation is assembled on the initial fluid mesh (subscript $0$), so the mesh obtained for a given interface displacement does not depend on how the interface reached it.
The linear elastic law is used for the ring, the pulsatile tube, the three-dimensional tube and the flag; a total Lagrangian neo-Hookean pseudo-solid for the cavity and the half-cylinder; and a total Lagrangian hyperelastic pseudo-solid weighted by $\delta$ for the collapsible channel.
On the refined Hron--Turek meshes of Section~\ref{sec:fsi3_m}, the partitioned couplings accept only a pseudo-solid assembled on the current mesh, so for both couplings Equation~\eqref{eqn:mesh_motion_pseudo_solid} is assembled there on the mesh of the previous step, with $\delta$ recomputed on that mesh (Section~\ref{sec:lim_mesh_motion}).
With the nonlinear laws, the Newton iteration of the pseudo-solid starts from a second-order Taylor extrapolation of the mesh displacement rather than from the previous displacement, which keeps the first iterate from folding the cells next to the interface when the interface moves by more than their height in one step (Section~\ref{sec:robustness_collapsible}).

The weight $\delta$ makes the cells next to selected moving boundaries translate almost rigidly, so that the deformation is absorbed farther away and boundary-layer cells keep their quality.
It is the inverse distance $\ell_f$ from each face to those boundaries, linear or quadratic, normalised by its mean over the mesh and clipped, $\delta_f = \min\left(c_{\max}, \max\left(c_{\min}, \ell_f^{-q}/\overline{\ell^{-q}}\right)\right)$ with $q = 1$ or $2$ and, by default, $c_{\min} = 0.1$ and $c_{\max} = 100$; it is weighted towards the interface for the collapsible channel ($q = 2$) and towards the flag and the cylinder for the refined Hron--Turek meshes ($q = 1$).

The cell-centred displacement is transferred to the mesh points by extrapolating from each adjacent cell centre with the cell gradient and averaging with inverse-distance weights, and the points are moved to their initial positions plus this displacement.
The mesh face flux $\phi^{\Gamma}_f$ is computed from the volumes swept by the faces between successive meshes, combined with the BDF2 weights,
\begin{equation}
\phi^{\Gamma,n}_f
=
\frac{
3 \, \mathcal{V}^{n}_{f}
-
\mathcal{V}^{n-1}_{f}
}
{2\Delta t},
    \label{eqn:gcl_swept_volume}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{V}^{n}_{f}$ and $\mathcal{V}^{n-1}_{f}$ are the volumes swept by face $f$ over the most recent and previous steps.
This enforces the discrete geometric conservation law for the mesh fluxes: a uniform flow through the moving mesh produces no spurious mass source.
On the fluid--solid interface, the imposed fluid velocity is the solid velocity, which differs from the swept-volume rate of the predicted interface by a time-discretisation error (Section~\ref{sec:interface_conditions}).

\subsection{Implementation and solution algorithm}
\label{sec:implementation_details}

The time step is summarised in Algorithm~\ref{alg:quasi_monolithic_fsi} and the residual evaluation in Algorithm~\ref{alg:coupled_residual}.
A fluid--solid coupling interface calls the fluid and solid region models to evaluate the discrete equations and to assemble their blocks of $\tilde{\bb{J}}$, and assembles the coupling blocks itself.
The Newton vector holds the fluid block, with the velocity components and pressure of each cell stored contiguously, followed by the solid velocity block; index sets of the two blocks define the outer field split, and the block size of the fluid unknowns the inner one.
The residual is evaluated through callbacks of the scalable nonlinear equation solvers (SNES) component of PETSc, which copy each trial vector into the OpenFOAM fields, update the boundary conditions and interface transfers, and evaluate the three residuals; PETSc applies the Newton operator matrix-free and receives $\tilde{\bb{J}}$, converted from a nested to a single distributed sparse matrix, as the preconditioning matrix.
The interface transfers gather the interface face values of each region to all processes; the implementation supports a single, conformal or non-conformal, interface.

In parallel, the two regions are decomposed either independently, each process owning cells of both regions, or together, by partitioning one graph formed by the cells of both regions with extra edges across the interface pairs, which keeps the cells on either side of the interface on the same process.
The joint decomposition could give some processes cells of one region only, but the coupled solve currently requires every process to own fluid cells, so the three-dimensional runs of Section~\ref{sec:scaling_3d} use joint slab partitions (Section~\ref{sec:tube3d_scaling}); the two-dimensional runs decompose the regions independently with the method of the tutorial (the Scotch graph partitioner~\citep{Pellegrini2012}, or a simple geometric split for the Hron--Turek benchmark).

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Quasi-monolithic FSI solution procedure for one time step, $t^{n-1} \to t^n$}
\label{alg:quasi_monolithic_fsi}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Store the fluid, solid and mesh state at $t^{n-1}$ (for a possible retry).
\State Predict the solid displacement $\bb{D}_S^{n,*}$ explicitly from $\bb{D}_S^{n-1}$, $\bb{v}_S^{n-1}$ and $\bb{v}_S^{n-2}$ using Equation~\eqref{eqn:solid_predicted_displacement}, and interpolate it to the solid mesh points.
\State Transfer the predicted interface displacement to the boundary of the fluid mesh-motion pseudo-solid (Equation~\eqref{eqn:mesh_displacement_interface}).
\State Solve the fluid mesh-motion problem and move the fluid mesh to $\Omega_F^n$; compute the mesh face fluxes from the swept volumes (Equation~\eqref{eqn:gcl_swept_volume}).
\State Initialise the Newton vector $\bb{\mathcal{U}}$ from the current fluid velocity, fluid pressure and solid velocity fields.
\State Solve the coupled nonlinear system
$\bb{R}(\bb{\mathcal{U}})=\bb{0}$ for
$(\bb{v}_F^n,p_F^n,\bb{v}_S^n)$ with the Jacobian-free Newton--Krylov method of Section~\ref{sec:jfnk_solution} (residual: Algorithm~\ref{alg:coupled_residual}); $\tilde{\bb{J}}$ is assembled, and the preconditioner built, at the Newton iterations prescribed by the lagging setting.
\If{the nonlinear solve has not converged}
\State Restore the state stored in step~1.
\If{adaptive time stepping is enabled and the retry limit is not reached}
\State Set $\Delta t \gets \max(\Delta t/4, \Delta t_{\min})$ and restart the time step from step~1.
\Else
\State Stop.
\EndIf
\EndIf
\State Extract the converged fluid velocity, fluid pressure and solid velocity
from the coupled solution vector, and update the interface boundary conditions.
\State Set the solid displacement $\bb{D}_S^n$ from $\bb{v}_S^n$ using Equation~\eqref{eqn:solid_bdf2_displacement}, and update the solid stress and point displacements.
\State If adaptive time stepping is enabled, select the next time step from the number of Newton iterations.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Coupled residual evaluation $\bb{R}(\bb{\mathcal{U}})$}
\label{alg:coupled_residual}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Extract $(\bb{v}_F,p_F,\bb{v}_S)$ from the current nonlinear trial vector.
\State Extrapolate the solid velocity to the solid interface faces, transfer it to the fluid interface, and impose it as the fluid velocity boundary condition (Equation~\eqref{eqn:mapped_solid_velocity}).
\State Set the interface pressure gradient from the normal momentum equation (Equation~\eqref{eqn:interface_pressure_gradient}) and update the interface pressure.
\State Compute the fluid force on the fluid--solid interface (Equation~\eqref{eqn:interface_fluid_traction}).
\State Transfer the fluid force to the solid interface (by interpolation or by the conservative transfer of Equation~\eqref{eqn:conservative_transfer}) and impose it as a solid traction boundary condition.
\State Evaluate the solid momentum residual (Equation~\eqref{eqn:solid_velocity_residual}), with $\bb{D}_S^n$ from Equation~\eqref{eqn:solid_bdf2_displacement}.
\State Evaluate the fluid momentum and pressure residuals (Equations~\eqref{eqn:fluid_momentum_residual} and \eqref{eqn:fluid_pressure_residual}) on the fixed fluid mesh $\Omega_F^n$.
\State Scale the fluid and solid rows by $s_F$ and $s_S$ and insert the result into the coupled residual vector.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

%Although applying the same tolerances for the segregated and Jacobian-free Newton-Krylov solution procedures may seem reasonable, care must be taken with $s_{\text{tol}}$. The Jacobian-free Newton-Krylov method typically makes a small number of large corrections to the solution, whereas the segregated procedure often makes a large number of small corrections. Consequently, if the same $s_{\text{tol}}$ value were used, the segregated procedure might be declared convergent prematurely. To avoid this, $s_{\text{tol}}$ is set to zero in the present work, meaning convergence is based solely on residual reduction. Nonetheless, setting $s_{\text{tol}} > 0$ could yield a more robust procedure capable of handling potential stalling.
