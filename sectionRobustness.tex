\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robustness}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% Stream feeding this section: R3 (campaign/HANDOFF.md), and pre-campaign
% check B2 (collapsibleChannel solid resolution through AMI).

Partitioned Dirichlet--Neumann coupling converges more slowly, or not at all, as the added mass grows relative to the solid inertia, and Robin--Neumann coupling shifts but does not remove this limit~\citep{Causin2005, Forster2006, Tukovic2018b}.
The quasi-monolithic coupling solves the interface conditions within the Newton iteration, so its convergence should depend on the added mass only through the conditioning of the linear systems.
This section maps the outcome of each coupling over the density ratio and the time step on two cases with an exact or independent reference, so that completed runs are also checked for accuracy.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Each cell of a map is one run with one of four couplings: Aitken, IQN-ILS and Robin--Neumann under the strict convergence test of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}, with at most 200 coupling iterations per step, and the quasi-monolithic coupling with at most 20 Newton iterations per step.
A run fails if a step reaches its iteration cap, if the solution becomes non-finite or diverges, or, for the quasi-monolithic coupling, if the fluid mesh-motion solve fails; this criterion, common to all four couplings, was fixed before the runs.
The partitioned couplings use the tolerances, predictors, initial relaxation and Robin coefficients of the companion paper~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}, with IQN-ILS reusing no previous time steps.
The interface pressure gradient of Aitken and IQN-ILS is the acceleration term of the normal momentum equation on the collapsible channel and a zero normal gradient on the ring, whose enclosed fluid domain does not admit the former in the pressure-correction solver (Section~\ref{sec:ring_m}); Robin--Neumann uses its Robin condition.
The quasi-monolithic coupling uses $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{step}} = 10^{-6}$ and a Krylov tolerance of $10^{-4}$.
All runs use BDF2 in the fluid and the solid, and the quasi-monolithic and partitioned runs use the same least-squares gradient and central-differencing convection in the fluid.
Wall times were measured on shared nodes and are indicative only.

The ring of Section~\ref{sec:ring_m} is run on the factor-2 mesh at the weak, moderate and strong added-mass levels ($m^{*} = 0.101$, $1.008$ and $10.08$) and at an extended level, $m^{*} = 100.8$ ($\rho_f = 14\,000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$), crossed with 12, 25, 50, 100 and 200 steps per period, for two periods, with the coupled frequency as the quantity of interest.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/robustness-ring-map}
	\caption{Ring added-mass problem, factor-2 mesh, two periods: outcome of each coupling against the number of time steps per period and the added-mass level. Numbers: mean coupling iterations (partitioned) or Newton iterations (quasi-monolithic) per step; red: failed.}
	\label{fig:robustness_ring}

Of the 80 runs, 79 complete (Figure~\ref{fig:robustness_ring}); the one failure is Robin--Neumann at the strong level with 12 steps per period, which reaches the iteration cap in its tenth step.
The quasi-monolithic coupling converges in two Newton iterations in every step of every cell.
The partitioned couplings, which start each step from a predicted interface state, complete every other cell, even at $m^{*} \approx 100$.
IQN-ILS needs 3.0--6.1 and Aitken 3.2--10.2 coupling iterations per step on average, both rising with $m^{*}$ and, at the strong and extended levels, with the number of steps per period; Robin--Neumann needs 3.0--42, mostly at fewer steps per period.
In every completed cell the four couplings give frequencies within $0.25\%$ of each other; the error against the exact frequency, from $-1.9\%$ at 12 steps per period to $+6.3\%$ at 200, is the discretisation error of the standard solid on the factor-2 mesh.
Summed over the 20 cells, the quasi-monolithic runs take about half the wall time of IQN-ILS, 0.4 times that of Aitken and under a third of that of Robin--Neumann, in line with the per-step costs of Section~\ref{sec:ring_m}.
Despite its large added mass, the ring is therefore a mild case for the partitioned couplings: its response is small, essentially linear and dominated by one mode, and IQN-ILS needs at most 6.1 coupling iterations per step on average, even at $m^{*} \approx 100$.
The collapsible channel below combines strong added mass with a thin wall in large deformation, and there the partitioned couplings fail over much of the map.

A thin elastic section of a channel wall collapses under an external pressure ramped to $200\,\mathrm{Pa}$ over $0.25\,\mathrm{s}$ and then oscillates~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026} (Figures~\ref{fig:collapsible_geom} and~\ref{fig:collapsible_field}).
The Reynolds number is $Re = Ua/\nu = 50$, based on the mean velocity $U = 1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ of the Poiseuille flow that drives the channel and the channel width $a = 1\,\mathrm{m}$.
The case combines strong added mass with large deformation and fluid mesh motion.
It modifies the problem of~\citet{Jensen2003} and \citet{Heil2004}, as in the companion paper, so that the wall can be modelled as a continuum and computed with partitioned coupling.
The published wall is a massless Kirchhoff--Love beam with a large axial pre-stress, about a thousand times its effective modulus, so that it behaves as a tensioned membrane.
Here the wall is an initially unstressed continuum, thicker ($h/a = 1/20$, as in~\citet{Heil2008}), clamped at its ends and loaded by a ramped rather than an impulsive external pressure, and it is given the density of the fluid, without which the partitioned couplings fail at small time steps and on fine meshes; the density moves the oomph-lib solution by $0.28\%$ of the peak deflection.

\paragraph{Set-up.}
The meshes are structured block meshes generated with \texttt{blockMesh}.
Fluid level 2 of the companion paper (12\,800 cells) is coupled through AMI to a $640\times8$ solid mesh, with the geometrically nonlinear velocity solid and conservative traction transfer in the quasi-monolithic coupling and the total-displacement solid in the partitioned couplings.
Every run starts from the steady Poiseuille flow through the undeformed channel, with the wall at rest.
The density ratio $\rho_s/\rho_f$ takes the values 10, 1, 0.1, 0.01 and 0 (massless wall), crossed with $\Delta t = 0.025$, $0.0125$, $0.00625$ and $0.003125\,\mathrm{s}$, and each run continues to $t = 0.75\,\mathrm{s}$, past the first trough of the wall midpoint, the quantity of interest, which is compared with the independently converged solution of the finite element code oomph-lib~\citep{Heil2006} for $\rho_s/\rho_f = 1$ and for the massless wall.
The solid mesh is finer along the wall than the fluid interface mesh because the error of the trough is dominated by the resolution of the wall: at $\Delta t = 0.00625\,\mathrm{s}$ the quasi-monolithic trough lies $0.52\%$ from oomph-lib with the $640\times8$ wall on fluid level 2, but $0.98\%$ with a $320\times8$ wall, half as fine along its length, on the finer fluid level 4 with a conformal interface.
% B2 (prerun671, with the initial flow): L2 AMI 640x8 -0.52%, L4 conformal 320x8 -0.98%

The fluid mesh moves with the pseudo-solid mesh motion of Section~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion}, started in each step from a mesh displacement extrapolated in time (see the note on the mesh motion below).

\paragraph{Outcome.}
The quasi-monolithic coupling completes all 20 cells, including the massless wall at every time step, with 2.0--3.1 Newton iterations per step on average and at most four (Figure~\ref{fig:robustness_collapsible}); the count falls with the time step and is independent of the density ratio.
Its Krylov iterations per Newton iteration, 180--260, are about ten times those of the ring (18--29).
They do not reflect the added mass, which leaves them unchanged over the map, nor the field-split approximations of the fluid block, which an exact factorisation of the fluid block does not improve, but the compact solid block of the preconditioning matrix, which misrepresents the bending of the thin wall (Section~\ref{sec:solid_block}).
With the Jacobian of the solid residual as the solid block, and the solver settings otherwise unchanged, the method needs 20--29 Krylov iterations and two Newton iterations per step for $\rho_s/\rho_f = 0$, 1 and 10, for $\Delta t = 0.00625$ and $0.003125\,\mathrm{s}$ and on fluid levels 1 and 2, for walls of slenderness $L/h$ from 50 to 400 and moduli varied by a factor of 64 (Figure~\ref{fig:solid_block_iterations}), whereas with the compact block the count grows with both the slenderness and the stiffness of the wall.
In reruns on the solids4foam version and computer of these comparisons, the configuration of the map (fluid level 2, $640\times8$ wall, $\Delta t = 0.00625\,\mathrm{s}$, $\rho_s/\rho_f = 1$) needs 380 Krylov iterations per Newton iteration with the compact block over the first $0.15\,\mathrm{s}$, during the collapse, and 282 over $0.75\,\mathrm{s}$ (241 in the map runs); with the Jacobian of the solid residual these fall to 28 and 29, the Newton iterations per step from 2.71 and 2.75 to 2.00, and the run time over the first $0.15\,\mathrm{s}$ by a factor of 11.8 ($384.8$ against $32.5\,\mathrm{s}$, medians of three repeats run alternately on one core, which agree within 2\%); over $0.75\,\mathrm{s}$ the wall-midpoint history differs from that of the compact block by at most $3\times10^{-6}$ of its peak, and the first trough is $-0.2148775$ against $-0.2148777\,\mathrm{m}$.

The partitioned couplings complete all cells at $\rho_s/\rho_f = 10$ and, apart from IQN-ILS at the two smaller steps, at $\rho_s/\rho_f = 1$, with 8--61 coupling iterations per step.
Aitken fails at every time step for $\rho_s/\rho_f \le 0.1$; IQN-ILS fails for $\rho_s/\rho_f = 0.1$ and $0.01$ at $\Delta t \le 0.0125\,\mathrm{s}$ and for the massless wall at $\Delta t \le 0.00625\,\mathrm{s}$; Robin--Neumann completes $\rho_s/\rho_f = 0.1$ at every step but needs 51--66 iterations per step, fails at all four steps for $\rho_s/\rho_f = 0.01$ and is undefined for the massless wall, its Robin coefficient scaling with the wall density.
Every failure reaches the iteration cap within the first $0.35\,\mathrm{s}$, earlier at smaller time steps, as expected of added-mass instability, except IQN-ILS at $\rho_s/\rho_f = 1$ and $\Delta t = 0.00625\,\mathrm{s}$, where the Newton iteration of the solid diverges within a coupling iteration at $t = 0.62\,\mathrm{s}$; with a zero normal pressure gradient on the interface instead of the acceleration term, this cell completes.
In total Robin--Neumann completes 12 of the 16 cells to which it applies, IQN-ILS 10 and Aitken 8.

\paragraph{Accuracy.}
For $\rho_s/\rho_f = 1$ the quasi-monolithic trough lies $1.80$, $0.78$, $0.52$ and $0.45\%$ below the converged oomph-lib value at the four time steps, and the partitioned troughs $1.59$--$1.60$, $0.55$--$0.58$, $0.30$--$0.32$ and $0.24\%$ below it.
At the large steps the error is temporal and the same for every coupling; at the small steps the quasi-monolithic and partitioned troughs differ by about $0.2\%$ of the reference trough, owing to the different solid formulations and traction transfers rather than to the coupling.
The quasi-monolithic troughs for $\rho_s/\rho_f = 1$ and for the massless wall agree to $0.02\%$ at every time step, and the massless trough lies $0.45\%$ from the massless oomph-lib reference at the smallest step: the coupling computes the massless limit of the modified problem directly, without the regularising wall density that the partitioned couplings need at these time steps.

\paragraph{Note on the mesh motion.}\label{sec:robustness_collapsible_mesh}
A practical point for implementations in which the mesh motion is solved outside the Newton iteration: if each step starts the pseudo-solid solve from the previous mesh, its first iterate carries the whole wall increment of the step into the cells next to the wall.
At $\Delta t = 0.025\,\mathrm{s}$, near the time of fastest collapse, this increment exceeds the height of those cells, and the linear solve of the pseudo-solid, whose preconditioner is frozen at the start of the step, then fails at $t = 0.3\,\mathrm{s}$ for every density ratio, although the coupled Newton iteration had converged in about three iterations per step up to that time.
Starting the solve from a mesh displacement extrapolated in time from the previous steps avoids this, and changes the troughs at the smaller steps by at most $2\times10^{-6}$ (relative).

\paragraph{Discussion.}
Robustness to the added mass and robustness to the time step are distinct properties.
Solving the interface conditions within the Newton iteration made the convergence insensitive to the added mass in both maps: the Newton iteration count did not change with the density ratio over the three orders of magnitude tested, and the quasi-monolithic coupling computes the massless wall at the two smaller time steps, where both Aitken and IQN-ILS reach the iteration cap, consistent with the added-mass instability of Dirichlet--Neumann iterations growing as the time step decreases~\citep{Causin2005}, and where the Robin--Neumann coefficient is undefined.
It does not remove the dependence on the nonlinearity of the coupled problem over one step: like any Newton method without continuation, the iteration can fail to converge when a large time step spans a strongly nonlinear transient, and the step must then be reduced.
The quasi-monolithic coupling is therefore the more reliable choice where the added mass is strong and the step is limited by accuracy rather than by the nonlinearity, as in the ring and the collapsible channel, and not necessarily where large steps through strongly nonlinear transients are wanted.
The robustness of the partitioned couplings also depends on their parameters, in particular the Robin coefficient of Robin--Neumann coupling and the number of previous time steps whose information IQN-ILS reuses.
These are set as in the companion paper~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}, with no reuse for IQN-ILS, and were not tuned for these maps, so the maps describe the partitioned couplings at those settings; tuned parameters might extend the range of density ratios and time steps over which they converge.
