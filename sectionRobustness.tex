\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robustness}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% Stream feeding this section: R3 (campaign/HANDOFF.md), and pre-campaign
% check B2 (collapsibleChannel solid resolution through AMI).

Partitioned Dirichlet--Neumann coupling converges more slowly, or not at all, as the added mass grows relative to the solid inertia, and Robin--Neumann coupling shifts but does not remove this limit~\citep{Causin2005, Forster2006, Tukovic2018b}.
The quasi-monolithic coupling solves the interface conditions within the Newton iteration, so its convergence should depend on the added mass only through the conditioning of the linear systems.
This section maps the outcome of each coupling over the density ratio and the time step on two cases with an exact or independent reference, so that a run that completes is also checked for accuracy.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Each cell of a map is one run with one of four couplings: Aitken, IQN-ILS and Robin--Neumann under the strict convergence test with at most \tbd{100} coupling iterations per step, and the quasi-monolithic coupling.
For each run the outcome (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}), the mean number of Newton or coupling iterations per step, the wall time and the error of the quantity of interest against the reference are recorded.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
Robustness with respect to the added mass and robustness with respect to the time step are distinct properties.
Solving the interface conditions within the Newton iteration removes the dependence of convergence on the added mass, including the enclosed-fluid and massless-wall configurations in which Dirichlet--Neumann iterations cannot converge~\citep{Kuttler2006} (Section~\ref{sec:robustness_collapsible}).
It does not remove the dependence on the nonlinearity of the coupled problem over one step: in the flag of Section~\ref{sec:lim_foil}, Newton stagnates at the time step at which IQN-ILS converges, and the quasi-monolithic coupling converges only with \tbd{a quarter} of that step.
The quasi-monolithic coupling is therefore expected to be the more reliable choice where the added mass is strong and the step is limited by accuracy rather than by the nonlinearity, as in \tbd{cases}, and not necessarily where large steps through strongly nonlinear transients are wanted.
The robustness of the partitioned couplings, in turn, depends on settings such as the Robin coefficient and the IQN-ILS history, which are those of the companion paper~\citetodo{companion verification paper} for every case (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}); the maps above do not measure how far other settings would extend them.
