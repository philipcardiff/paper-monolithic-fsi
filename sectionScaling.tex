A pressure pulse in a fluid-filled elastic tube (Figures~\ref{fig:tube3d_geom_m} and~\ref{fig:tube3d_field}) is the standard three-dimensional test of strongly coupled FSI under strong added mass, with $\rho_s/\rho_f = 1.2$~\citep{kuttler2008fixed, Degroote2009}; its set-up, the partitioned three-level study and the literature references are given in the companion paper~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.
Here it compares the quasi-monolithic and partitioned couplings in three dimensions and measures how the cost of the method and its preconditioner grows with problem size and process count; as in Section~\ref{sec:accuracy_cost}, complete solvers are compared, whose discretisations differ as listed in Section~\ref{sec:consistency_partitioned}.

\subsection{Consistency on three levels}\label{sec:tube3d_consistency}

A quarter of the tube is computed with symmetry planes on the three geometrically similar mesh levels of the companion paper (16\,000, 128\,000 and 1\,024\,000 fluid cells), with the time step halved with the mesh ($2.5\times10^{-5}$, $1.25\times10^{-5}$ and $6.25\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}$), to the end of the response.
The solid is the geometrically linear velocity solid, and both couplings use BDF2 in the fluid and the solid.
Direct mapping of the interface fields between matching faces requires the fluid and solid wall points to coincide, so the quasi-monolithic runs use tutorial meshes with the wall arcs placed exactly on both sides (solids4foam PR \#625); the companion paper's Robin--Neumann runs predate that change and use AMI interpolation across a wall mismatch of up to about $1\,\mu\mathrm{m}$.
Repeating the Robin--Neumann runs on the corrected meshes changes the displacement and timing quantities by at most $0.13\%$ on every level, but the axial pressure wave speed by $+0.22\%$, $+0.39\%$ and $+0.67\%$ on levels 1--3, growing with refinement; the comparison below is therefore against these corrected-mesh runs, with the companion paper's partitioned settings.
% Data: Mx 5404489 (L1, L2), 5404701 (L3); paper-data/campaign/d/rn-exact on evidence/m-runs-d.
Newton is converged to a relative residual of $10^{-6}$ with a Krylov tolerance of $10^{-4}$; the Robin--Neumann runs meet displacement, pressure and leakage-flux tolerances of $10^{-6}$, $10^{-5}$ and $10^{-5}$ at every step~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.
% Data: QM a421f063d, Mx 5398197 (L1/L2) and 5399576 (L3);
% paper-data/campaign/d/l3 on evidence/m-runs-d (3dtube_qm_vs_rn_L1_L3.csv).
% RN: paper-fsi-verification:main:paper-data/3dtube_level3_mesh_study.csv.

The two couplings converge towards each other under refinement: the largest difference in a displacement or timing quantity falls from $1.2\%$ (level 1) to $0.3\%$ (level 2) and $0.13\%$ (level 3), well inside the companion paper's Robin--Neumann discretisation uncertainty.
The quasi-monolithic level-to-level changes have the same character as the partitioned ones ($u_{r,\max}$ and $c_w$ non-monotone, $u_{z,\min}$ and $t_\mathrm{arr}$ converging at low order), so the non-asymptotic behaviour belongs to the problem and the mesh family, not the coupling.
The axial pressure wave speed is the least converged quantity for both couplings: its difference changes sign without decreasing, but stays below the level-2-to-3 change of either solution ($0.60\%$ for Robin--Neumann, $0.25\%$ for the quasi-monolithic coupling).
Against the original companion-paper meshes the level-3 difference was $0.87\%$; the mesh correction accounts for about three quarters of it, so most of it came from the mesh, not the coupling.
The sensitivity of $c_p$ to a micrometre-scale wall change, and its lack of trend, come from its extraction, not the flow: the verification driver's axis pressure probes lie on axial cell faces at every level, and a probe returns its containing cell's value, so round-off, decomposition and mesh motion decide between the cells either side.
This shifts each station's arrival by up to $\Delta z/(2c_p)$, about $17\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ on level 3, matching the $20\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ shift of one station between the two Robin--Neumann meshes (the other stations agree within $2\,\mu\mathrm{s}$), giving a scatter in the fitted $c_p$ of about $0.2\%$ on level 3 and $0.7\%$ on level 1, the size of the differences in Table~\ref{tab:tube3d_consistency}, so the $c_p$ differences between the couplings are within the extraction uncertainty~\citep{solids4foamIssue643}.
Newton needs two iterations at every step on every level, as expected for this linear-elastic, small-strain problem, and the Krylov iterations per Newton iteration grow about 1.35-fold per refinement.

%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
\subsection{Strong scaling}\label{sec:tube3d_scaling}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

Level 2 is run on 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 and 64 processes and level 3 on 16, 32, 64 and 128 processes, over a fixed window of 50 steps restarted from $t = 5\,\mathrm{ms}$, with both couplings.
All runs use one exclusive MeluXina node (two 64-core AMD EPYC 7H12 processors, eight NUMA domains of 16 cores), one run at a time, with the processes pinned to consecutive cores; the time reported is the median elapsed time of three repeats, excluding the first step.
File-system stalls spread the repeats by up to $34\%$ and make the elapsed time 11--39\% larger than the CPU time, but the efficiencies from the medians agree with those from the CPU time to within $0.02$.
The fluid and solid regions are decomposed together (Section~\ref{sec:implementation_details}) into axial slabs ($1\times1\times N$ subdomains), which give every process fluid and solid cells, with a load imbalance of at most $6\%$ in the fluid cells and $20\%$ in the solid cells.
A Scotch partition of the combined graph left some processes with solid cells only on the finer partitions (for example 13 of 128 on level 3), and the coupled solve does not proceed when a process owns no fluid cells~\citep{solids4foamIssue639}.
% Source: applications/utilities/decomposeParMonolithic/README.md at 257b2e564.
The solid block is preconditioned by one BoomerAMG V-cycle, the 3dTube verification-driver default, instead of the exact LU factorisation replicated on every process, which dominates the cost on the finer meshes (Table~\ref{tab:tube3d_pc}).

\paragraph{Measured.}
Time per step, parallel efficiency, Newton iterations per step and Krylov iterations per Newton iteration against process count (Figure~\ref{fig:tube3d_scaling}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/3dTube-strong-scaling}
	\caption{Three-dimensional tube, single-node strong scaling over a 50-step window: (a) elapsed time per step, with ideal scaling from 16 processes (dotted) and the processes of one NUMA domain shaded; (b) parallel efficiency relative to 16 processes; (c) Krylov iterations per Newton iteration of the quasi-monolithic coupling. Medians of three repeats.}
	\label{fig:tube3d_scaling}
\end{figure}

Within one NUMA domain neither coupling scales well: on level 2 the time per step falls only 3.9-fold (quasi-monolithic) and 6.1-fold (Robin--Neumann) from 1 to 16 processes, whereas going to 32 processes, which adds a second domain's memory channels, roughly halves it for both (Figure~\ref{fig:tube3d_scaling}a).
This is consistent with both solvers being memory-bandwidth limited within a domain, although not confirmed by profiling; the efficiency is therefore measured from 16 processes, one full domain.
On level 3 the quasi-monolithic coupling keeps an efficiency of 1.10 and 1.09 on 32 and 64 processes and 0.78 on 128 processes, about 8000 fluid cells per process, against 1.07, 1.15 and 0.87 for Robin--Neumann; on level 2 it falls to 0.70 on 64 processes, about 2000 fluid cells per process, against 0.80.
The Krylov iterations per Newton iteration do not change with the process count (18.0--18.6 on level 2 from 1 to 64 processes, 27.2--27.5 on level 3 from 16 to 128), and Newton converges in two iterations per step throughout, so neither the block-Jacobi ILU(0) velocity block nor the BoomerAMG solid block degrades under partitioning; the somewhat lower efficiency than Robin--Neumann lies in the cost per Krylov iteration.
The quasi-monolithic coupling costs 3.2 times as much as Robin--Neumann on level 3 with 16 processes and 3.6 times with 128 (3.0 and 3.3 in CPU time), and 1.2 to 2.1 times on level 2 from 1 to 64 processes: with its Robin interface condition the partitioned coupling needs only about three coupling iterations per step here, so on the tube a well-tuned partitioned coupling is cheaper.

\subsection{Effect of the preconditioner and Jacobian settings}\label{sec:tube3d_pc_study}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
% All variants are PETSc options on the tag (A4 Sections 5 and 6, route A).

The default solver settings (Table~\ref{tab:solver_settings}) are varied one at a time on level 2 with 16 processes, over the 50-step window from $t = 5\,\mathrm{ms}$ during the passage of the pulse.
Each variant must reproduce the default run's quantities of interest to within the iterative floor (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}), or is reported as failing to.
The variants are: an upper-triangular instead of a full Schur factorisation of the fluid block; the Jacobian and preconditioner rebuilt every Newton iteration instead of every eighth; a Krylov tolerance of $10^{-3}$ instead of $10^{-4}$; a replicated exact LU factorisation of the solid block instead of one BoomerAMG V-cycle; BoomerAMG instead of block-Jacobi ILU(0) for the fluid velocity block; and the assembled approximate Jacobian instead of the matrix-free operator as the Newton operator (Section~\ref{sec:jfnk_solution}).

The default settings are the fastest of the seven; every variant reaches the same solution, so the choice is one of cost only.
The solid-block preconditioner has the largest effect: the replicated LU factorisation multiplies the cost by eight at unchanged iteration counts.
A looser Krylov tolerance saves Krylov iterations but costs Newton iterations and is slower overall.
Rebuilding the Jacobian and preconditioner every Newton iteration (lag 1) leaves the iteration counts unchanged and costs $26\%$ more than the default lag of eight; the upper-triangular Schur factorisation saves the second pressure solve but needs $38\%$ more Krylov iterations, for $25\%$ more time.
With the assembled approximate Jacobian as the Newton operator, Newton converges only linearly (Figure~\ref{fig:newton_convergence}): on the tube the residual falls by a factor of $0.2$--$0.7$ per iteration and meets the $10^{-6}$ tolerance after 12 iterations per step, at 1.8 times the default cost (Table~\ref{tab:tube3d_pc}).
On the ring, whose added mass is stronger, the rate is $0.85$ per iteration and 50 iterations reduce the residual only to $10^{-5}$, against 2 iterations to $10^{-6}$ matrix-free.
The approximate Jacobian is thus suited to building the preconditioner but not to defining the Newton step.
With the matrix-free operator the Krylov tolerance sets the rate, as expected of inexact Newton: each iteration reduces the residual by a factor of $10^{-4}$--$5\times10^{-4}$ at the default tolerance of $10^{-4}$, and tightening it to $10^{-8}$ gives superlinear convergence, which is not needed at the residual tolerance used.

\paragraph{Discussion: parallel cost of the coupled solve.}
The coupled linear system adds a preconditioner with no partitioned counterpart, whose solid block decides how its cost scales: one algebraic multigrid V-cycle is eight times cheaper than replicated LU on level 2 (Table~\ref{tab:tube3d_pc}; Section~\ref{sec:lim_solid_pc}), and keeps the Krylov iteration counts independent of the process count.
The joint slab decomposition leaves a solid-cell imbalance of up to $20\%$; a partition weighting the combined fluid and solid work was not tested.
