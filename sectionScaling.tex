A pressure pulse travelling through a fluid-filled elastic tube (Figure~\ref{fig:tube3d_geom_m}) is the standard three-dimensional test of strongly coupled FSI under strong added mass, with $\rho_s/\rho_f = 1.2$~\citep{kuttler2008fixed, Degroote2009}; its set-up, the partitioned three-level study and the literature references are given in the companion paper~\citetodo{companion verification paper}.
It is used here to check the quasi-monolithic coupling against the partitioned one in three dimensions and to measure how the cost of the method and of its preconditioner grows with problem size and process count.

\subsection{Consistency on three levels}\label{sec:tube3d_consistency}
% TIME-SCHEME NOTE (Philip, 22 Oct 2026), for anyone adding results here:

% run (runs.csv); rerun any result computed with Euler, and say so here.

A quarter of the tube is computed with symmetry planes, on the three geometrically similar mesh levels of the companion paper (16\,000, 128\,000 and 1\,024\,000 fluid cells) with the time step halved with the mesh ($2.5\times10^{-5}$, $1.25\times10^{-5}$ and $6.25\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}$), to the end of the response.
The solid is the geometrically linear velocity solid, and both couplings use BDF2 (\texttt{backward}) in the fluid and the solid.
The quasi-monolithic coupling maps the interface fields directly between matching faces, which requires the fluid and solid wall points to coincide; the quasi-monolithic runs therefore use the tutorial meshes with the wall arcs placed exactly on both sides (solids4foam PR \#625), whereas the partitioned Robin--Neumann runs of the companion paper predate that change and use AMI interpolation across a mismatch of up to about $1\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ between the two walls.
Repeating the Robin--Neumann runs on the corrected meshes changes the displacement and timing quantities by at most $0.13\%$ on every level, but the axial pressure wave speed by $+0.22\%$, $+0.39\%$ and $+0.67\%$ on levels 1--3, a shift that grows with refinement; the comparison below is therefore made against these corrected-mesh Robin--Neumann runs, with the same partitioned settings as the companion paper.
% Data: Mx 5404489 (L1, L2), 5404701 (L3); paper-data/campaign/d/rn-exact on evidence/m-runs-d.
The Newton iteration is converged to a relative residual of $10^{-6}$ with a Krylov tolerance of $10^{-4}$; the Robin--Neumann runs meet displacement, pressure and leakage-flux tolerances of $10^{-6}$, $10^{-5}$ and $10^{-5}$ at every step~\citetodo{companion verification paper}.
% Data: QM a421f063d, Mx 5398197 (L1/L2) and 5399576 (L3);
% paper-data/campaign/d/l3 on evidence/m-runs-d (3dtube_qm_vs_rn_L1_L3.csv).
% RN: paper-fsi-verification:main:paper-data/3dtube_level3_mesh_study.csv.

\paragraph{Measured.}
The peak radial displacement $u_{r,\max}$ and its time, the minimum axial displacement $u_{z,\min}$ and its time, and the late radial minimum at point A, the arrival time $t_\mathrm{arr}$ of the pulse at A, and the wave speeds of the pulse along the wall ($c_w$) and along the axis ($c_p$), compared level by level with the partitioned Robin--Neumann results of the companion paper (Table~\ref{tab:tube3d_consistency}).

The two couplings converge towards each other under refinement: the largest difference in a displacement or timing quantity falls from $1.2\%$ on level 1 to $0.3\%$ on level 2 and $0.13\%$ on level 3, well inside the Robin--Neumann discretisation uncertainty of the companion paper.
The level-to-level changes of the quasi-monolithic solution have the same character as the partitioned ones ($u_{r,\max}$ and $c_w$ non-monotone, $u_{z,\min}$ and $t_\mathrm{arr}$ converging at low order), so the non-asymptotic behaviour belongs to the problem and the mesh family, not to the coupling.
The axial pressure wave speed is the least converged quantity for both couplings: its difference changes sign between levels ($+0.08$, $-0.16$, $+0.20\%$) without decreasing, but stays below the level-to-level change of either solution ($0.60\%$ for Robin--Neumann and $0.25\%$ for the quasi-monolithic coupling between levels 2 and 3).
Against the original companion-paper meshes the level-3 difference was $0.87\%$; the corrected meshes account for about three quarters of it, so most of it came from the mesh rather than from the coupling.
The sensitivity of $c_p$ to a micrometre-scale wall change, and its lack of a trend, come from its extraction rather than from the flow: the axis pressure probes of the verification driver lie on axial cell faces at every level, and a probe returns the value of the cell that contains it, so round-off, the decomposition and the mesh motion decide between the cells either side of the face.
This shifts each station's arrival time by up to $\Delta z/(2c_p)$, about $17\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ on level 3, which matches the $20\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ shift of one station between the two Robin--Neumann meshes while the other stations agree within $2\,\mu\mathrm{s}$, and gives a scatter in the fitted $c_p$ of about $0.2\%$ on level 3 and $0.7\%$ on level 1, the size of the differences in Table~\ref{tab:tube3d_consistency}~\citetodo{solids4foam issue 643}.
The $c_p$ differences between the couplings are therefore within the extraction uncertainty.
The Newton iteration converges in two iterations at every step on every level, as expected for this linear-elastic, small-strain problem, and the Krylov iterations per Newton iteration grow by a factor of about 1.35 per refinement.

\subsection{Strong scaling}\label{sec:tube3d_scaling}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

Level 2 is run on 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 and 64 processes and level 3 on 16, 32, 64 and 128 processes, over a fixed window of 50 steps restarted from $t = 5\,\mathrm{ms}$, with the quasi-monolithic coupling and with Robin--Neumann coupling.
All runs use one exclusive node of the MeluXina system (two 64-core AMD EPYC 7H12 processors, with eight NUMA domains of 16 cores each), one run at a time, with the processes pinned to consecutive cores; the time reported is the median \texttt{ExecutionTime} of three repeats, which agree within $4\%$.
The fluid and solid regions are decomposed together by the \texttt{decomposeParMonolithic} utility of solids4foam, which partitions the fluid and solid cells as one graph, with extra edges across the interface face pairs, so that a process may own fluid cells, solid cells or both; for the scaling runs the graph is cut into slabs along the tube axis (the \texttt{simple} method with $1\times1\times N$ subdomains), because the \texttt{scotch} partition of the combined graph left some processes with solid cells only on the finer partitions (for example 13 of 128 processes on level 3), and the coupled solve does not proceed when a process owns no fluid cells~\citetodo{solids4foam issue 639}.
The slabs give every process fluid and solid cells, with a load imbalance of at most $6\%$ in the fluid cells and $20\%$ in the solid cells.
% Source: applications/utilities/decomposeParMonolithic/README.md at 257b2e564.
The solid block is preconditioned by one V-cycle of BoomerAMG, the default of the solids4foam 3dTube verification driver, which replaces the exact LU factorisation gathered on one process because that factorisation dominates the cost on the finer meshes: on level 2 with 16 processes it makes a run 8.25 times as expensive at unchanged iteration counts (Table~\ref{tab:tube3d_pc}).

\paragraph{Measured.}
Time per step and parallel efficiency, Newton iterations per step and Krylov iterations per Newton iteration as functions of the process count (Figure~\ref{fig:tube3d_scaling}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/3dTube-strong-scaling}
	\caption{Three-dimensional tube, strong scaling over a 50-step window: (a) \texttt{ExecutionTime} per step, with ideal scaling from 16 processes (dotted) and the processes of one NUMA domain shaded; (b) parallel efficiency relative to 16 processes; (c) Krylov iterations per Newton iteration of the quasi-monolithic coupling. Medians of three repeats.}
	\label{fig:tube3d_scaling}
\end{figure}

Within one NUMA domain neither coupling scales well: on level 2 the time per step falls by a factor of only 3.6 (quasi-monolithic) and 5.3 (Robin--Neumann) from 1 to 16 processes, whereas going from 16 to 32 processes, which adds the memory channels of a second domain, roughly halves it for both (Figure~\ref{fig:tube3d_scaling}a).
Both solvers are therefore limited by memory bandwidth on this node, and the efficiency is measured from 16 processes, one full domain.
On level 3 the quasi-monolithic coupling keeps an efficiency of 1.06 and 1.04 on 32 and 64 processes and 0.78 on 128 processes, about 8000 fluid cells per process, against 1.08, 1.16 and 0.85 for Robin--Neumann; on level 2 it falls to 0.69 on 64 processes, about 2000 fluid cells per process, against 0.77.
The Krylov iterations per Newton iteration do not change with the process count (18.0--18.6 on level 2 from 1 to 64 processes, 27.2--27.5 on level 3 from 16 to 128), and Newton converges in two iterations per step throughout, so neither the block-Jacobi ILU(0) velocity block nor the BoomerAMG solid block loses effectiveness as the domain is partitioned; the somewhat lower efficiency than Robin--Neumann lies in the cost per Krylov iteration, not in the iteration count.
Over these windows the quasi-monolithic coupling costs 3.0 times as much as Robin--Neumann on level 3 with 16 processes and 3.3 times with 128, and 1.3 to 2.0 times on level 2 from 1 to 64 processes: with its Robin interface condition the partitioned coupling needs only about three coupling iterations per step on this case, so the tube is a case in which a well-tuned partitioned coupling is cheaper.

\subsection{Effect of the preconditioner and Jacobian settings}\label{sec:tube3d_pc_study}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
% All variants are PETSc options on the tag (A4 Sections 5 and 6, route A).

The default solver settings (Table~\ref{tab:solver_settings}) are examined by changing one setting at a time on level 2 with 16 processes over a 50-step window restarted from $t = 5\,\mathrm{ms}$, during the passage of the pulse; each variant is run three times and the median execution time is reported.
Each variant must reproduce the quantities of interest of the default run to within the iterative floor (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}); a variant that does not is reported as such.
The variants are: an upper-triangular instead of a full Schur factorisation of the fluid block; the Jacobian and preconditioner rebuilt every Newton iteration instead of every eighth; a Krylov tolerance of $10^{-3}$ instead of $10^{-4}$; an exact LU factorisation of the solid block gathered on one process instead of one BoomerAMG V-cycle; BoomerAMG instead of block-Jacobi ILU(0) for the fluid velocity block; and the assembled approximate Jacobian used as the Newton operator instead of the matrix-free operator (Section~\ref{sec:jfnk_solution}).

The default settings are the fastest of the seven.
Every variant reaches the same solution, so the choice is one of cost only.
The largest effect is that of the solid-block preconditioner: the gathered LU factorisation leaves the iteration counts unchanged but multiplies the cost by eight, which is why one BoomerAMG V-cycle is the default on the three-dimensional meshes.
A looser Krylov tolerance saves Krylov iterations but costs Newton iterations (2.7 instead of 2 per step), and is slower overall.
Rebuilding the Jacobian and preconditioner at every Newton iteration (lag 1) leaves the iteration counts unchanged and costs $25\%$ more than the default lag of eight, and the upper-triangular Schur factorisation saves the second pressure solve but needs $38\%$ more Krylov iterations, for $23\%$ more time.
With the assembled approximate Jacobian as the Newton operator, Newton converges only linearly and needs four times as many iterations, which confirms that the approximate Jacobian is suited to building the preconditioner but not to defining the Newton step; its cheaper iterations limit the extra cost to $30\%$ on this problem.

\paragraph{Discussion: parallel cost of the coupled solve.}
The coupled linear system brings a preconditioner whose cost is not present in partitioned coupling.
In the present implementation the solid block is preconditioned by one V-cycle of algebraic multigrid (Section~\ref{sec:lim_solid_pc}), which on level 2 is eight times cheaper than the gathered LU factorisation (Table~\ref{tab:tube3d_pc}), and on level 3 of the three-dimensional tube the method reaches a parallel efficiency, relative to 16 processes, of 0.78 on 128 processes against 0.85 for Robin--Neumann, with Krylov iteration counts independent of the process count (Section~\ref{sec:tube3d_scaling}).
The joint decomposition of the fluid and solid cells is a further difference: partitioned coupling decomposes each region separately, whereas the coupled residual is distributed as one graph, and the slab partitions used here balance the fluid cells to within $6\%$ but the solid cells only to within $20\%$; the efficiency nevertheless stays at or above 0.78 on level 3 up to 128 processes, and a partition that weights the combined fluid and solid work was not tested.
