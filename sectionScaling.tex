A pressure pulse in a fluid-filled elastic tube (Figures~\ref{fig:tube3d_geom_m} and~\ref{fig:tube3d_field}) is the standard three-dimensional test of strongly coupled FSI under strong added mass, with $\rho_s/\rho_f = 1.2$~\citep{kuttler2008fixed, Degroote2009}; its set-up, the partitioned three-level study and the literature references are given in the companion paper~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.
Here it compares the quasi-monolithic and partitioned couplings in three dimensions and measures how the cost of the method and its preconditioner grows with problem size and process count; as in the preceding subsections, complete solvers are compared, whose discretisations differ as listed in Section~\ref{sec:consistency_partitioned}.

\subsubsection{Consistency on three levels}\label{sec:tube3d_consistency}
% TIME-SCHEME NOTE (Philip, 22 Oct 2026), for anyone adding results here:
% all transient results in this paper must use BDF2 (backward) for the fluid
% ddt(U) and the solid. The solids4foam tutorial default for this case is

A quarter of the tube is computed with symmetry planes on three geometrically similar block-structured hexahedral mesh levels (16\,000, 128\,000 and 1\,024\,000 fluid cells), with the time step halved with the mesh ($2.5\times10^{-5}$, $1.25\times10^{-5}$ and $6.25\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}$), to the end of the response.
The solid is the geometrically linear velocity solid, and both couplings use BDF2 in the fluid and the solid.
Both couplings use the same meshes, with conformal fluid and solid interface faces whose points coincide; the quasi-monolithic coupling transfers the interface fields by the direct map and the Robin--Neumann coupling by AMI between the same matching faces.
The circular wall arcs must be placed identically in the fluid and solid mesh definitions: generating the two sides independently can leave a mismatch of up to about $1\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ between them, which then has to be bridged by interpolation; in preliminary runs such a mismatch changed the displacement and timing quantities by at most $0.13\%$, but the axial pressure wave speed by up to $0.7\%$, increasingly with refinement.
The partitioned runs use the Robin--Neumann coupling with the settings of the companion paper.
% Data: Mx 5404489 (L1, L2), 5404701 (L3); paper-data/campaign/d/rn-exact on evidence/m-runs-d.
Newton is converged to a relative residual of $10^{-6}$ with a Krylov tolerance of $10^{-4}$; the Robin--Neumann runs meet displacement, pressure and leakage-flux tolerances of $10^{-6}$, $10^{-5}$ and $10^{-5}$ at every step~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.
% Data: QM a421f063d, Mx 5398197 (L1/L2) and 5399576 (L3);
% paper-data/campaign/d/l3 on evidence/m-runs-d (3dtube_qm_vs_rn_L1_L3.csv).
% RN: paper-fsi-verification:main:paper-data/3dtube_level3_mesh_study.csv.

The two couplings converge towards each other under refinement: the largest difference in a displacement or timing quantity falls from $1.2\%$ (level 1) to $0.3\%$ (level 2) and $0.13\%$ (level 3), well inside the companion paper's Robin--Neumann discretisation uncertainty.
The quasi-monolithic level-to-level changes have the same character as the partitioned ones ($u_{r,\max}$ and $c_w$ non-monotone, $u_{z,\min}$ and $t_\mathrm{arr}$ converging at low order), so the non-asymptotic behaviour belongs to the problem and the mesh family, not the coupling.
The axial pressure wave speed is the least converged quantity for both couplings: its difference changes sign without decreasing, but stays below the level-2-to-3 change of either solution ($0.60\%$ for Robin--Neumann, $0.25\%$ for the quasi-monolithic coupling).
Its sensitivity, and its lack of trend, come from how it is extracted rather than from the flow, which illustrates where care is needed when extracting results from cell-centred fields.
The pressure arrival times are sampled at axial stations that lie on cell faces at every level; a probe there returns the value of one of the two adjacent cells, and round-off, the decomposition and the mesh motion decide which.
This shifts each station's arrival time by up to $\Delta z/(2c_p)$, about $17\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ on level 3, and gives a scatter in the fitted $c_p$ of about $0.2\%$ on level 3 and $0.7\%$ on level 1, the size of the differences in Table~\ref{tab:tube3d_consistency}, so the $c_p$ differences between the couplings are within this extraction uncertainty; sampling at cell centres, or interpolating between the adjacent cells, would remove it.
Newton needs two iterations at every step on every level, as expected for this linear-elastic, small-strain problem, and the Krylov iterations per Newton iteration grow about 1.35-fold per refinement.

%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
\subsubsection{Strong scaling}\label{sec:tube3d_scaling}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%

Level 2 is run on 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 and 64 processes and level 3 on 16, 32, 64 and 128 processes, over a fixed window of 50 steps restarted from $t = 5\,\mathrm{ms}$, with both couplings on the same meshes.
All runs use one exclusive MeluXina node (two 64-core AMD EPYC 7H12 processors, eight NUMA domains of 16 cores), one run at a time, with the processes pinned to consecutive cores; the time reported is the median elapsed time of three repeats (five for the quasi-monolithic coupling on level 3), excluding the first step.
The case files are held in node-local memory during the runs: timings that include shared file-system access can vary by tens of per cent between repeats, which should be avoided when comparing solvers.
The repeats then agree within $7\%$ ($15\%$ for the quasi-monolithic coupling on level 3), the elapsed time exceeds the CPU time by $6$--$12\%$, and the efficiencies from the elapsed and CPU times agree within $0.03$.
% Rerun 17 Nov 2026: paper-data/tube3d_scaling_rerun (README), 18.1 node-h, 74 runs.
The fluid and solid regions are decomposed together (Section~\ref{sec:implementation_details}) into axial slabs ($1\times1\times N$ subdomains), which give every process fluid and solid cells, with a load imbalance of at most $6\%$ in the fluid cells and $20\%$ in the solid cells; a partition weighting the combined fluid and solid work was not tested.
The solid block is preconditioned by one BoomerAMG V-cycle instead of the exact LU factorisation replicated on every process, which dominates the cost on the finer meshes (Section~\ref{sec:tube3d_pc_study}).

\paragraph{Measured.}
Time per step, parallel efficiency, Newton iterations per step and Krylov iterations per Newton iteration against process count (Figure~\ref{fig:tube3d_scaling}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/3dTube-strong-scaling}
	\caption{Three-dimensional tube, single-node strong scaling over a 50-step window: (a) elapsed time per step, with ideal scaling from 16 processes (dotted) and the processes of one NUMA domain shaded; (b) parallel efficiency relative to 16 processes; (c) Krylov iterations per Newton iteration of the quasi-monolithic coupling. Medians of three repeats (five for the quasi-monolithic coupling on level 3).}
	\label{fig:tube3d_scaling}
\end{figure}

Within one NUMA domain neither coupling scales well: on level 2 the time per step falls only 3.6-fold (quasi-monolithic) and 5.0-fold (Robin--Neumann) from 1 to 16 processes, whereas going to 32 processes, which adds a second domain's memory channels, roughly halves it for both (Figure~\ref{fig:tube3d_scaling}a).
This is consistent with both solvers being memory-bandwidth limited within a domain, although not confirmed by profiling; the efficiency is therefore measured from 16 processes, one full domain.
On level 3 the quasi-monolithic coupling keeps an efficiency of 1.08 and 1.05 on 32 and 64 processes and 0.81 on 128 processes, about 8000 fluid cells per process, against 1.08, 1.16 and 0.88 for Robin--Neumann; on level 2 it falls to 0.71 on 64 processes, about 2000 fluid cells per process, against 0.76.
The Krylov iterations per Newton iteration do not change with the process count (18.0--18.6 on level 2 from 1 to 64 processes, 27.2--27.5 on level 3 from 16 to 128), and Newton converges in two iterations per step throughout, so neither the block-Jacobi ILU(0) velocity block nor the BoomerAMG solid block degrades under partitioning; the somewhat lower efficiency than Robin--Neumann lies in the cost per Krylov iteration.
The quasi-monolithic coupling costs 3.2 times as much as Robin--Neumann on level 3 with 16 processes and 3.5 times with 128 (3.1 and 3.4 in CPU time), and 1.2 to 2.1 times on level 2 from 1 to 64 processes: with its Robin interface condition the partitioned coupling needs only 3.1 (level 3) to 4.1 (level 2) coupling iterations per step here, so on the tube a well-tuned partitioned coupling is cheaper.

\subsubsection{Effect of the preconditioner and Jacobian settings}\label{sec:tube3d_pc_study}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
% All variants are PETSc options on the tag (A4 Sections 5 and 6, route A).

The default solver settings (Table~\ref{tab:solver_settings}) are varied one at a time on level 2 with 16 processes, over the 50-step window from $t = 5\,\mathrm{ms}$ during the passage of the pulse.
Each variant must reproduce the default run's quantities of interest to within the iterative floor (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}), or is reported as failing to.
The variants are: an upper-triangular instead of a full Schur factorisation of the fluid block; the Jacobian and preconditioner rebuilt every Newton iteration instead of every eighth; a Krylov tolerance of $10^{-3}$ instead of $10^{-4}$; a replicated exact LU factorisation of the solid block instead of one BoomerAMG V-cycle; BoomerAMG instead of block-Jacobi ILU(0) for the fluid velocity block; and the assembled approximate Jacobian instead of the matrix-free operator as the Newton operator (Section~\ref{sec:jfnk_solution}).

The default settings are the fastest of the seven; every variant reaches the same solution, so the choice is one of cost only.
The Jacobian of the solid residual as the solid block (Section~\ref{sec:solid_block}), tested separately on level 1 (run logs available from the author) in serial over 60 steps, leaves the Krylov iterations unchanged on this thick wall (14.2 against 14.5 per Newton iteration), but the run costs about 30\,\% more, as BoomerAMG then works on a solid block with three times the non-zeros; the automatic option keeps the compact block here.
The solid-block preconditioner has the largest effect: the replicated LU factorisation multiplies the cost by eight at unchanged iteration counts, since its cost and memory do not fall with the number of processes (on 4 processes it costs 2.6 times as much as BoomerAMG); in the two-dimensional cases, whose solid blocks are small, it remains the cheaper choice, as BoomerAMG there doubles the Krylov iterations on the ring.
A looser Krylov tolerance saves Krylov iterations but costs Newton iterations and is slower overall.
Rebuilding the Jacobian and preconditioner every Newton iteration (lag 1) leaves the iteration counts unchanged and costs $26\%$ more than the default lag of eight; the upper-triangular Schur factorisation saves the second pressure solve but needs $38\%$ more Krylov iterations, for $25\%$ more time.
With the assembled approximate Jacobian as the Newton operator, Newton converges only linearly (Figure~\ref{fig:newton_convergence}): on the tube the residual falls by a factor of $0.2$--$0.7$ per iteration and meets the $10^{-6}$ tolerance after 12 iterations per step, at 1.8 times the default cost (Table~\ref{tab:tube3d_pc}).
On the ring, whose added mass is stronger, the rate is $0.85$ per iteration and 50 iterations reduce the residual only to $10^{-5}$, against 2 iterations to $10^{-6}$ matrix-free.
The approximate Jacobian is thus suited to building the preconditioner but not to defining the Newton step.
With the matrix-free operator the Krylov tolerance sets the rate, as expected of inexact Newton: each iteration reduces the residual by a factor of $10^{-4}$--$5\times10^{-4}$ at the default tolerance of $10^{-4}$, and tightening it to $10^{-8}$ gives superlinear convergence, which is not needed at the residual tolerance used.
