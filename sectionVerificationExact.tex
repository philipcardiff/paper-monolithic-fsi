\subsection{Assessment protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
% Source of the protocol: campaign/HANDOFF.md (origin/campaign/handoff),
% Section 4 "Common protocol" and Section 6 "Data formats".

The results in this paper were computed with development versions of solids4foam, to be released as \tbd{release tag}, built with OpenFOAM v2512, PETSc 3.22 or 3.24 and HYPRE~\citep{hypre}; in every comparison except the Hessenthaler flap (Section~\ref{sec:hessenthaler_m}) both couplings use the same version, and the version of each run is recorded with its data (see Provenance below).
The differences between these versions that affect the quasi-monolithic method are stated where they apply (the interface displacement transfer and the assembly of the mesh-motion pseudo-solid, Section~\ref{sec:fluid_mesh_motion} and Table~\ref{tab:mesh_motion_settings}).
% Source: provenance.json of the 6b runs (Mx 5402592).
The quasi-monolithic coupling is compared with the partitioned Dirichlet--Neumann coupling accelerated by Aitken relaxation or IQN-ILS and with Robin--Neumann coupling~\citep{Tukovic2018b}, with the partitioned schemes and settings of the companion paper~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026} for each case.

\paragraph{Control of iterative error.}
The Newton iterations of the quasi-monolithic coupling stop when either the relative nonlinear residual or the relative Newton step falls below its tolerance, both fixed for each case before the runs (Table~\ref{tab:solver_settings} in Appendix~\ref{app:settings}).
Their iterative error is measured by repeating selected runs with both tolerances reduced a hundredfold.
The partitioned runs use the strict coupling convergence test of the companion paper, which requires every interface residual measure to be below the tolerance, chosen so that their iterative error is no larger than that of the quasi-monolithic runs.
The residual definitions differ, so equal tolerances do not imply equal iterative error; the repeated runs bound the iterative error of each coupling below the differences reported.

\paragraph{Accuracy.}
Errors are measured against the references of the companion paper, with the uncertainty stated there: exact solutions (Section~\ref{sec:verification_exact}), the oomph-lib~\citep{Heil2006} solution for the collapsible channel (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}) and the Featflow tables of \citet{Turek2006} for FSI3 (Section~\ref{sec:accuracy_cost}).
No new reference values are formed in this paper.
Where no independent reference exists, the two couplings are compared on the same meshes and time steps.

\paragraph{Cost.}
Timings are taken on exclusive nodes of the MeluXina supercomputer~\citep{MeluXina} (two 64-core AMD EPYC 7H12 processors per node, with eight NUMA domains of 16 cores), each run on a single node with the processes pinned to consecutive cores.
Unless stated otherwise, the cost is the median over three repeats of the elapsed (wall-clock) time of a fixed window of time steps run from a restart, excluding start-up; the solver CPU time of the first process is recorded for reference.
On these nodes the elapsed time exceeds the CPU time by 1--4\%.
% TODO: the 3-D tube strong-scaling timings are being rerun without file-system stalls; add a statement here if needed.
Times from runs on shared nodes, such as those of the robustness study of Section~\ref{sec:robustness}, the half-cylinder mesh study of Section~\ref{sec:blob_m} and the order studies of Appendix~\ref{app:high_order_solid}, are indicative only and are identified as such with the results.
Newton and Krylov iterations (quasi-monolithic) and coupling iterations (partitioned) are extracted from the solver logs.
Timings from different machines are never compared.

\paragraph{Outcome of a run.}
Each run is recorded as completed, diverged (non-finite residual, inverted cell or linear solver failure) or stalled (maximum number of iterations reached without convergence).
A run that does not complete is reported as such and is not repeated with other settings.

\paragraph{Provenance and data.}
Each run records the solids4foam commit, the OpenFOAM, PETSc and HYPRE versions, compiler and flags, host and CPU model, process count and full PETSc options.
The run tables, extracted quantities of interest, provenance records and the scripts for every figure and table are in the \texttt{paper-data} directory of the paper repository \tbd{public URL and Zenodo DOI}.

\subsection{Ring added-mass problem}\label{sec:ring_m}

% companion paper (paper-fsi-verification, sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex,
% Section "Ring added-mass problem").
% Monolithic solid: linearGeometryVelocity
% (constant/solid/solidProperties.monolithic); conformal direct map
% (constant/fsiProperties.monolithic).
% Driver: verification/Allverify --study monolithic (time steps) and the
% conformal arm of --study nonconformal (meshes); see A1 Section 1.1.

An elastic ring vibrating freely in its $n = 2$ mode inside a rigid, fluid-filled annulus has an exact frequency that depends on the added mass of the fluid alone; the ratio of the wet to the dry frequency isolates the added-mass effect from the solid and temporal errors that the wet and dry runs share~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}.
The question is whether the quasi-monolithic coupling reproduces the exact added mass at second order and adds no damping to the free vibration.

\paragraph{Set-up.}
A ring of mean radius $a = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ and thickness $h = 0.1\,\mathrm{m}$ (plane strain, $E = 1\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\nu = 0.3$, $\rho_s = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) is surrounded by fluid out to a rigid wall of radius $b = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$; one quadrant is modelled with symmetry planes.
The fluid densities $\rho_f = 14$, $140$ and $1400\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ give added-mass ratios (the ratio of the added mass of the fluid to the modal mass of the ring~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026}) of $m^{*} = 0.101$, $1.008$ and $10.08$, the weak, moderate and strong levels; round densities were chosen rather than round ratios.
A dry ring is run with each mesh and time step.
The solid is the geometrically linear velocity solid with the standard second-order discretisation, and the interface is conformal.
The meshes are structured block meshes generated with the OpenFOAM utility \texttt{blockMesh}~\citep{Weller1998}, refined by factors 1, 2 and 4 at 100 steps per period, and 25 to 100 steps per period are used on the factor-2 mesh; the fluid and the solid both use the second-order backward (BDF2) scheme.
Figure~\ref{fig:ring_field_m} shows the pressure of the $n = 2$ mode at the strong added-mass level.

On the factor-2 mesh every quasi-monolithic step converges in two Newton iterations at every added-mass level and time step; at the strong level it needs 20--22 Krylov iterations per Newton iteration, and a step costs about half as much as an IQN-ILS step at every time step (Table~\ref{tab:ring_cost}).
At its coupling tolerance IQN-ILS leaves a ratio error of $0.03$--$0.13\%$ and an amplitude loss above those of the other two couplings, which agree with each other in both quantities.
Robin--Neumann reaches the same accuracy as the quasi-monolithic coupling, but its coupling iterations grow with the time step, so that it is 3 to 13 times as expensive and, unlike the other two, becomes more expensive per period as the time step grows.

Under mesh refinement at 100 steps per period, the ratio error of the quasi-monolithic coupling falls faster than second order at every added-mass level (observed orders 3.2--3.3 at the strong level, 3.3--4.1 at the moderate level and higher at the weak level), to below $4\times10^{-4}\%$ on the factor-4 mesh (Figure~\ref{fig:ring_accuracy}a), as the shared solid and temporal errors cancel in the ratio.
The IQN-ILS ratio error converges at order 1.8--1.9 and is 25 to 600 times larger on the factor-4 mesh.
The coupling adds no measurable damping.
At the strong level, the quasi-monolithic amplitude loss per period equals that of the dry ring to within $0.004\%$ of the amplitude at every time step (Figure~\ref{fig:ring_accuracy}b).
The loss is therefore the BDF2 damping of the ring itself, which falls by a factor of 7--8 per halving of the time step.
On all three meshes and levels at 100 steps per period, the wet and dry losses differ by at most $0.08\%$ of the amplitude, whereas IQN-ILS adds $0.06$ to $2.1\%$ to the loss.

A harmonic pressure wave travelling along an axisymmetric thick-walled elastic tube combines added mass, viscous attenuation and a deforming wall; its exact linear solution~\citep{CardiffFSIVerification2026} measures the error of the viscous interface traction, which the effectively inviscid ring problem does not.

\paragraph{Set-up.}
The tube of the companion paper is used unchanged ($h/R = 0.1$, $L/R = 15$, Womersley number $5.01$, $\rho_s h/(\rho_f R) = 0.1$), with exact travelling-wave data at both ends and exact initial fields, on a structured axisymmetric wedge mesh generated with \texttt{blockMesh}.
Meshes are refined by factors 1, 2 and 4 at 200 steps per period, and 50 to 400 steps per period are used on the factor-2 mesh.
First Fourier coefficients over periods 5--6 are compared with the exact solution; the fluid and the solid both use BDF2, and the Newton tolerances are tightened to $10^{-8}$ (Krylov $10^{-6}$) so that the iterative error is negligible.
The partitioned runs of the companion paper need a flux-consistent treatment of the tube ends to recover second order in time; the quasi-monolithic runs reach it without one (Table~\ref{tab:womersley_m}).

Every quantity converges at second order in time (Table~\ref{tab:womersley_m}), and the flow rate, the wall displacement amplitude, the wave speed and the pressure at second order in space (the velocity profile, at a tenfold reduced amplitude, at 1.78), with every quasi-monolithic step converging in two Newton iterations and 23--28 Krylov iterations per Newton iteration; the partitioned runs need 15--16 (IQN-ILS) and 11--18 (Robin--Neumann) coupling iterations per step at the strict test.
The wall displacement phase and the attenuation are the exceptions: their errors fall only from $2.4$ to $1.2\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$ and from $0.23\%$ to $0.19\%$ between the factor-1 and factor-4 meshes, and about $1.2\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$ and $0.15\%$ remain on the factor-2 mesh when the time step is extrapolated to zero.

\subsection{Effect of the interface treatment}\label{sec:interface_comparison}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------%%
% Comparisons selected per Philip's decision (20 Oct 2026): only those that are
% an option on the tag and informative. Routes and run sets from audit A4
% (paper-data/a4_ablation_reproducibility.md, Sections 1 and 7).

For two ingredients of the interface treatment of Section~\ref{sec:interface_conditions} a simpler alternative is common: the interface pressure can be given a zero normal gradient instead of the gradient from the normal momentum equation, and the load can be transferred between non-conforming interface meshes by interpolating the traction instead of by the conservative transfer.
Each pair of approaches is compared here on the ring problem, whose exact solution attributes any change in the result to the approach compared.

\paragraph{Interface pressure.}
The normal pressure gradient of Equation~\eqref{eqn:interface_pressure_gradient} is replaced by a zero normal gradient on the interface, with everything else unchanged, including the face extrapolation of the solid velocity.

% arm of Allverify --study nonconformal, --levels moderate,strong.
The moderate and strong levels are run on mesh factors 1, 2 and 4 at 100 steps per period.

With a zero normal pressure gradient the ratio error converges at first order, observed orders 0.74 and 0.88 at the moderate level and 0.75 and 0.89 at the strong level, and is 300 to 600 times larger on the factor-4 mesh than with the normal-momentum gradient ($0.059\%$ and $0.108\%$, against $9\times10^{-5}\%$ and $4\times10^{-4}\%$; Figure~\ref{fig:ring_pressure_condition}a).
The zero gradient also damps the coupled vibration: the amplitude loss per period exceeds that of the dry ring by $1.3$, $0.66$ and $0.34\%$ of the amplitude at the moderate level and by $2.3$, $1.2$ and $0.65\%$ at the strong level on the three meshes, halving with each refinement, whereas with the normal-momentum gradient the wet and dry losses differ by at most $0.08\%$ of the amplitude (Figure~\ref{fig:ring_pressure_condition}b).
On a moving interface the normal momentum equation gives a pressure gradient that accelerates the fluid with the wall; a zero gradient neglects it when the pressure is extrapolated to the interface, so the interface pressure, and with it the added-mass force on the solid, carries an error proportional to the wall acceleration and to the size of the cells next to the interface, which grows with the added mass and falls at first order under refinement.
The interface pressure condition therefore strongly influences both the frequency and the damping of the coupled vibration.

\paragraph{Non-conformal transfer.}
The ring is computed with solid interface meshes coarser and finer than the fluid interface mesh, coupled through the AMI weights, once with the traction interpolated from the fluid to the solid faces and once with the conservative transfer of Equation~\eqref{eqn:conservative_transfer}, which transfers face forces with the transpose of the velocity interpolation.

The total interface force transferred is logged on both sides of the interface at every step.

\begin{figure}[htbp]

	\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/ringAddedMass-nonconformal.pdf}
	\caption{Ring added-mass problem on non-conformal interfaces, quasi-monolithic coupling, strong level, 100 steps per period: (a) largest relative imbalance of the total interface force over the run and (b) magnitude of the relative error of the wet/dry frequency ratio against mesh refinement factor, for a conformal interface (direct map) and for solid interfaces with $32$ (coarser) and $72$ (finer) circumferential faces per mesh factor against $48$ on the fluid side, with interpolated (open) and conservative (filled) traction transfer.}

With the conservative transfer the total force on the solid equals that of the fluid to round-off, with a relative imbalance below $3\times10^{-15}$ at every step, mesh and level (Figure~\ref{fig:ring_nonconformal}a).
With interpolated traction the imbalance is $3$--$9\times10^{-5}$ and does not decrease with refinement.
This imbalance is dominated by a geometric mismatch rather than by the interpolation.
The solid is geometrically linear, so the interpolated traction is integrated over the undeformed solid face areas without conversion to the deformed configuration, whereas the fluid mesh moves with the interface and the fluid force is integrated over the deformed faces.
The same imbalance therefore appears on a conformal interface with a direct face-to-face map, to within $10\%$ on the factor-2 and factor-4 meshes ($2$--$3\times10^{-5}$ at the strong level, $5$--$8\times10^{-5}$ at the moderate level).
The conservative transfer removes it by construction, because it transfers forces rather than tractions, so the force balance alone does not compare the accuracy of the two transfers on this case.
The frequency ratio does, and the two transfers give nearly the same result: the interpolated and conservative ratios differ by at most $2.6\times10^{-5}$ relative on every mesh and level, neither being systematically closer to the exact value, and on the factor-4 mesh the ratio error on a non-conformal interface is $1.9\times10^{-5}$ or less, against $4\times10^{-6}$ or less on the conformal interface (Figure~\ref{fig:ring_nonconformal}b).
On this case the benefit of the conservative transfer is thus its exact force balance; no systematic gain in accuracy is observed.

Although not shown here, two further ingredients were found to be important during the development of the method: the interface force must enter the velocity solid at the current iterate rather than lagged by one step, which otherwise increased the amplitude loss per period of the ring more than tenfold, and the viscous traction must be evaluated consistently with the discrete viscous wall flux of the fluid momentum equation, which otherwise lowered the observed spatial order of the steady half-cylinder displacement of Section~\ref{sec:blob_m}.
